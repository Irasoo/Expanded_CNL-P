Feedback on RISEN:
1. Easy to use. You just follow the five sections (Role, Instructions, Steps, End Goal, Narrowing) and fill them in. Rarely got stuck.
2. Too simple for complex tasks. When the task has many steps or branches, I had to cram everything into long sentences, and the edge cases got mixed together. Felt messy.
3. Easy to edit. Simple structure, quick to find what needs changing.
4. Weak guidance. It doesn't remind me of things like inputs/outputs or exception handling. I'm basically on my own there.

Feedback on CNL-P:
1. Much more powerful structure. The modules (PERSONA, CONSTRAINTS, WORKER, INPUTS/OUTPUTS, TYPES, EXAMPLES, different FLOWs) force me to think through inputs, outputs, constraints, and edge cases one by one.
2. Better coverage. It reminded me of things I would have missed with RISEN.
3. Much slower to write. I had to remember a lot of syntax (which modules need an END tag, how to write IF/FOR/WHILE blocks, the COMMAND format), so the same content took noticeably longer.
4. Harder to maintain. Changing one part often means updating variables or types in another module.
5. As a beginner, I often didn't think of using the advanced flows (like ALTERNATIVE_FLOW) at all. It's not that the syntax is hard to learn — I just never had the habit of asking "what if the user changes their mind halfway?" The framework expects you to think like an experienced designer, but that mindset doesn't come from reading the tutorial.
6. It tends to get longer and longer as you write. The same idea that takes one sentence in natural language gets spread across inputs, outputs, constraints, and commands. Part of that is intentional, but for a beginner it's hard to tell when the extra length is necessary and when it's just me over-adding.
7. The biggest question for me: with LLMs today, a model can read the tutorial once and convert a natural-language prompt into CNL-P almost perfectly. So why do we still have to write it by hand? For a beginner, hand-writing it feels like an unnecessary burden — I spend time fighting the syntax instead of thinking about the actual requirements.
