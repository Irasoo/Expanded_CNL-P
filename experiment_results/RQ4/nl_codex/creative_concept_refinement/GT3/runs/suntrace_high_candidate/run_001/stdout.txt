=== Final Creative Concept ===
## SunTrace: Bookmark the Change of Plans

### Core idea  
Young adults do not usually forget sunscreen at the start of the day—they forget it when the day changes. A class becomes lunch on the lawn, a walk becomes an outdoor hangout, or a quick errand becomes hours outside.

SunTrace turns its reusable UV-sensitive sticker into a **sun bookmark for changing plans**. The sticker marks the beginning of outdoor time, then gives the user a physical cue to check in and reapply when their day moves into its next “chapter.”

**The day changed. Check your bookmark.**

### Main execution  
Create a repeatable 12–15-second vertical video using one person, one sticker, SunTrace SPF 50, and an ordinary campus setting.

1. **Hook — the plan changes**  
   A student exits class and receives a message or sees friends heading outside. They look at the sticker already placed on their arm.  
   On-screen text:  
   **“Your plans changed. Did your sunscreen?”**

2. **Bookmark the outdoor day**  
   Close-up: the person applies transparent SunTrace SPF 50 to exposed skin, places the reusable sticker on the skin, and joins the outdoor activity.  
   Text:  
   **“Bookmark your sun time.”**

3. **Check in at the next transition**  
   A quick cut shows the person leaving the lawn or moving to another outdoor spot. They notice the sticker while reaching for their bag or drink.  
   They remove it, reapply SunTrace SPF 50 to the exposed area, and place the sticker back in the same spot.  
   Text:  
   **“New plan? Reapply.”**

4. **The visual payoff**  
   After more time outdoors, they remove the sticker completely. In a clear close-up, the sticker’s shape temporarily appears in the surrounding exposed area while the covered area remains unchanged.  
   Text:  
   **“The sticker shows where the sun reached—not how much damage occurred.”**

5. **End card**  
   **“Apply. Bookmark. Check. Reapply.”**  
   **“SunTrace SPF 50.”**

### Product and brand role  
SunTrace SPF 50 is the protection action, while the included reusable sticker makes the normally invisible experience of being in the sun visible and memorable. The sticker is not presented as a UV meter or a measure of sunscreen performance; it is a physical cue to check in and reapply, followed by a visual demonstration of sun exposure.

### Audience connection and practicality  
The idea is built around a recognizable 18–30-year-old behavior: outdoor plans change throughout a campus day, and sunscreen reapplication disappears when attention shifts. The “change of plans” moment provides an immediate, believable trigger instead of relying on a timer or extra technology.

Each video can be filmed in one continuous campus day with natural light, a single actor, one sticker, and SunTrace sunscreen. Variations can use simple transitions—class to lawn, library to café patio, or commute to outdoor meet-up—while keeping the same clear structure: **plan changes, notice the bookmark, reapply, reveal.**
