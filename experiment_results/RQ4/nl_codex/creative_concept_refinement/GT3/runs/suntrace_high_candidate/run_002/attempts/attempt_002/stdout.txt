=== Final Creative Concept ===
## SunTrace: The Sun Receipt — Before You Go Back Out

### Core idea

SunTrace turns a forgotten sunscreen routine into a simple campus habit: **check your sun receipt before your next outdoor stop**.

The reusable UV-sensitive sticker acts as a visible bookmark for a sun outing. After the user spends time outside, removing it reveals a temporary trace of its shape on the skin. That trace does not measure UV dose, skin damage, or whether SPF has expired. It simply signals: **you have been out in the sun—check your face and reapply before going back outside.**

### Main execution: “The Back-Out Check”

Create 15–20-second vertical videos for TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social channels. Each video uses the same repeatable campus rhythm:

**Before the first outdoor stop**  
A student applies transparent **SunTrace SPF 50** to their face and places one included reusable sticker on an unobstructed area of their forearm.

On-screen text:

**“First stop: outside.”**  
**“SunTrace protects the face. The sticker is your reminder.”**

**During the everyday transition**  
The student walks from a lecture to an outdoor lunch, crosses campus, or takes a break outside. The footage is phone-shot in an ordinary university setting.

**When they come back in or reach shade**  
The student peels off the sticker in a close-up. The surrounding exposed skin temporarily reveals the sticker’s shape while the covered area remains unchanged.

The opening or reveal is paired with:

**“Got a sun receipt?”**

A concise footer clarifies:

**“Shows sun exposure visually—not exact UV dose, damage, or SPF expiry.”**

**Before the next outdoor stop**  
The student looks at the trace, checks the SunTrace bottle, and reapplies the facial sunscreen before heading back outside.

On-screen text:

**“Receipt checked. Face reapplied. Back out.”**

End card:

**“SunTrace SPF 50 — Apply. Go. Check. Reapply.”**

### Product and brand role

SunTrace SPF 50 is the product that provides facial sun protection and applies transparently in the normal routine. The included reusable sticker does not demonstrate the sunscreen’s exact performance; it creates a recognizable visual prompt linked to the user’s own outdoor transition.

The brand’s role is to connect an invisible protective habit with a visible, repeatable check: **the sticker shows that a sun outing happened, and SunTrace gives the user a reason to check and reapply before the next one.**

### Audience and objective

The idea is built around the moments when 18–30-year-olds commonly lose track of reapplication: leaving class for lunch, moving between campus buildings, or returning outdoors after a break. The unexpected sticker reveal earns attention, while the “before you go back out” framing turns it into a practical behavior rather than a claim about skin damage.

Every video follows the same three-beat structure—**first outing, sun receipt, next outing**—so the concept is easy to understand, remember, and repeat across creators and university communities.

### Execution requirements

Use only the real SunTrace sunscreen and included stickers. Film with a phone in natural campus locations, keeping the sticker reveal as a clear close-up in shade. No special effects, celebrities, expensive locations, or additional technology are needed.
