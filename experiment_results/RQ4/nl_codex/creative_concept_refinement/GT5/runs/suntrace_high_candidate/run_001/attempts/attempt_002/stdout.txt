==================== USER INPUT 1/1 ====================
Brand:
SunTrace

Product:
A facial sunscreen that is transparent when applied normally. The package includes reusable UV-sensitive stickers. When a sticker is placed on the skin before sun exposure and removed later, the protected area remains unchanged while the surrounding exposed area temporarily reveals the sticker shape.

Target audience:
Young adults aged 18-30 who understand that sunscreen is important but frequently forget to reapply it during outdoor activities.

Objective:
Make the invisible protective effect of sunscreen visually understandable and motivate users to remember sunscreen application and reapplication.

Key product benefit:
The sunscreen provides SPF 50 protection, while the included UV-sensitive demonstration stickers make the effect of sun exposure easier to visualize.

Channels:
TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social media communities.

Budget:
Low to medium.

Constraints:
Use only the real sunscreen and included demonstration stickers. Do not claim that the temporary visual demonstration measures exact UV damage. Do not use celebrities or expensive locations. The idea must be understandable within a short video.

==================== AGENT OUTPUT ====================

=== Final Creative Concept ===
**Concept: “Show Up for Your Skin”**

SunTrace turns an invisible sunscreen habit into a short, visual reminder. In each video, a young adult prepares for an ordinary outdoor activity—such as spending time on campus or meeting friends outside—by applying SunTrace SPF 50 facial sunscreen as usual. They place one of the included reusable UV-sensitive stickers on their skin before going outdoors.

After the activity, they remove the sticker on camera. The area under the sticker remains unchanged while the surrounding exposed area temporarily reveals the sticker’s shape, making the contrast visible in a single close-up. The creator then reapplies SunTrace SPF 50 and says: “If you can’t see sun exposure happening, make sunscreen the part you remember.” The video ends with the simple prompt: “Apply. Reapply. Show up for your skin.”

The sticker demonstration is presented as a visual reminder of exposure, not as a measurement of exact UV damage. SunTrace’s role is the transparent SPF 50 protection that users may otherwise forget to reapply; the sticker makes the invisible context easier to understand and remember. The reusable sticker can be used again in another short video, reinforcing reapplication during different outdoor activities.

The format is designed for TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social media communities: a quick preparation shot, a brief outdoor moment, the sticker-removal reveal, and the reapplication prompt. It uses only the real sunscreen and included stickers, can be filmed in an everyday outdoor setting without celebrities or an expensive location, and communicates the idea within a short video.

Reasons: The concept gives SunTrace a strong, product-specific visual device: the reusable UV-sensitive sticker turns normally invisible sunscreen protection into an immediate before-and-after reveal. This is more distinctive than generic sunscreen reminders and makes the SPF 50 product central to the story. It directly addresses young adults who know sunscreen matters but forget to reapply, using ordinary campus and social activities that fit the audience and requested channels. The short preparation, outdoor activity, reveal, and reapplication sequence is easy to understand and likely to hold attention in short-form video. It is also practical at low to medium budget because it requires only the real product, the included stickers, a creator, and an everyday location. The main limitation is that the phrase "Show up for your skin" is relatively general, and viewers could misread the temporary sticker-shaped effect as a precise measure of UV damage or as proof of exact sunscreen performance unless the disclaimer is highly visible and consistently phrased.
Refinement suggestions: Make the demonstration's meaning unmistakable in the video itself by adding concise on-screen language such as "Visual reminder of sun exposure, not a UV damage measurement" at the reveal, while keeping the explanation brief. Strengthen the reapplication behavior by showing a clear time or activity transition and making reapplication the payoff rather than only a spoken prompt. Build more variation into repeatable executions, such as different campus or social scenarios and a recurring reveal close-up, so the reusable sticker reinforces habit formation instead of functioning as a one-off trick. Consider a more specific campaign line tied to the product mechanism, while retaining the human tone of "Show Up for Your Skin."
