=== Final Creative Concept ===
**Concept: “The SunTrace Time Receipt”**

Young adults rarely decide to spend the whole day outdoors; the day accumulates one small extension at a time. SunTrace turns those extensions into a visible, repeatable “time receipt” for remembering sunscreen.

Each short video starts with the participant applying SunTrace SPF 50 and placing one reusable UV-sensitive sticker on the skin. A phone-screen style receipt appears beside the activity: “OUTDOOR PLAN: 20 MINUTES.” Every time the plan extends, the receipt gains another line: “+ lunch,” “+ one more match,” “+ campus event,” or “+ walk home.” The activity continues through quick, ordinary phone-shot moments, making the audience recognize the familiar way a short outing quietly becomes much longer.

When the participant finally pauses, they remove the sticker. The protected area remains visually unchanged while the surrounding exposed area temporarily reveals the sticker shape. The reveal is not presented as a measurement of UV damage or exact sunscreen performance. It is SunTrace’s visual “receipt” for the time the original plan outlasted the original sunscreen moment.

The participant looks at the completed receipt, notices how the plan has changed, and immediately reapplies SunTrace before continuing outdoors. The receipt then updates from “20 MINUTES” to “REAPPLIED,” creating the consistent branded payoff: **“Plans add up. Reapply SunTrace.”** A brief supporting line identifies the product as SPF 50 and clarifies that the sticker is a visual demonstration, not a precise UV-damage measurement.

The distinctive role of the sticker is not simply to show a before-and-after mark; it behaves like a physical time receipt that is completed by an unexpectedly extended day. The format directly fits young adults who forget reapplication because plans shift in social, campus, and recreational settings rather than because they deliberately reject sunscreen. It makes the reapplication trigger unmistakable: when the “receipt” grows beyond the original outing, the person reapplies before carrying on.

The execution needs only SunTrace, an included reusable sticker, a phone, and an ordinary outdoor setting. Creators and university social communities can repeat the same recognizable structure with different “receipt” additions, keeping the idea fresh while preserving the product demonstration, the short-video hook, and the behavioral message: apply before going out, then reapply when the day extends.

Weak criteria: originality
Reasons: The time-receipt metaphor gives a familiar sunscreen reminder a meaningful product-specific structure: ordinary plans accumulate, the sticker creates a visible demonstration, and the completed receipt prompts reapplication. This is clearly relevant to young adults whose outdoor plans extend through lunch, sports, campus events, or walking home. The product and included sticker remain central, and the concept respects the constraints by avoiding celebrities, expensive locations, and claims that the reveal measures exact UV damage. The short-video format is easy to understand and offers a repeatable hook for TikTok, Reels, and university communities. Originality is strong but not exceptional because receipt-style overlays, time accumulation, and reminder mechanics are recognizable advertising devices. The main strategic weakness is that the receipt metaphor may imply that reapplication is required only when the planned duration increases, while users may also need reminders based on normal sunscreen guidance and changing conditions. The reveal also needs very clear staging so viewers understand that it is a visual demonstration rather than a literal record of damage or protection performance.
Refinement suggestions: Make the behavioral rule more precise and less dependent on the receipt reaching a particular duration: use the receipt as a prompt that appears whenever the day extends or a relevant reapplication moment occurs, with concise SPF 50 reapplication guidance kept consistent across executions. Clarify the visual sequence with a simple split or matched framing showing the sticker area and surrounding skin before and after exposure, while retaining the disclaimer that the effect is illustrative and does not measure exact UV damage. Make the receipt language more brand-owned and consistent, such as a fixed SunTrace format with recognizable additions like '+ lunch' or '+ game,' rather than relying heavily on generic phone-interface styling. Keep the videos short by limiting each story to two or three extensions and making the sticker reveal the central payoff. Test whether viewers immediately understand that the protected area is the unchanged sticker shape and that the exposed surrounding area reveals it; if not, add a brief spoken or on-screen clarification without slowing the format.
