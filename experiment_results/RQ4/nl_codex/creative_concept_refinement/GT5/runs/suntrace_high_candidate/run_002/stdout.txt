Provide one complete creative brief with brand, product, target audience, objective, key product benefit, channels, budget, and constraints:


==================== USER INPUT 1/1 ====================
Brand:
SunTrace

Product:
A facial sunscreen that is transparent when applied normally. The package includes reusable UV-sensitive stickers. When a sticker is placed on the skin before sun exposure and removed later, the protected area remains unchanged while the surrounding exposed area temporarily reveals the sticker shape.

Target audience:
Young adults aged 18-30 who understand that sunscreen is important but frequently forget to reapply it during outdoor activities.

Objective:
Make the invisible protective effect of sunscreen visually understandable and motivate users to remember sunscreen application and reapplication.

Key product benefit:
The sunscreen provides SPF 50 protection, while the included UV-sensitive demonstration stickers make the effect of sun exposure easier to visualize.

Channels:
TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social media communities.

Budget:
Low to medium.

Constraints:
Use only the real sunscreen and included demonstration stickers. Do not claim that the temporary visual demonstration measures exact UV damage. Do not use celebrities or expensive locations. The idea must be understandable within a short video.
================== END USER INPUT 1/1 ==================

==================== AGENT OUTPUT ====================

=== Final Creative Concept ===
**Concept: “Make Your Mark. Meet It Again.”**

**Core Idea**  
SunTrace turns its UV-sensitive sticker into a personal “return point” for an outing: a small mark placed before sun exposure that users deliberately revisit at the next natural break. Instead of treating the reveal as a standalone sunscreen proof moment, every short video follows one simple challenge: **put down a SunTrace mark before you go out, then meet it again when you pause to reapply.**

The temporary sticker shape gives the audience a distinctive visual payoff, but the repeatable behavior is the point. The sticker is a physical marker that travels with their day and makes an otherwise forgettable second application feel like completing a small ritual.

**Main Execution**  
A TikTok, Reels, and university-community series opens on an immediate close-up: a creator presses a reusable SunTrace sticker onto skin after applying SunTrace SPF 50.

On-screen text: **“Leave yourself a mark before you go out.”**

The video then uses a quick, first-person-style progression through one ordinary outing. The sticker remains briefly visible as a recurring visual detail while the creator moves through a realistic activity, such as arriving at a picnic, beginning a campus walk, taking a study break outdoors, or joining a casual game.

At the outing’s next clear transition, the creator stops for a drink, changes activity, or regroups with friends. They return to the sticker, remove it in close-up, and show the temporary shape against the surrounding exposed area.

The reveal is immediately followed by a clear product shot and reapplication of SunTrace SPF 50. The recurring closing line is:

**“Meet your mark. Reapply SunTrace SPF 50 according to directions.”**

A compact on-screen clarification appears during the reveal: **“Visual reminder only. Not a measure of exact UV damage.”**

Each execution retains the same opening, physical marker, revisit moment, reveal, and reapplication, while changing the person and outdoor context. University social accounts can post the videos with a simple accompanying prompt: **“What pause will you use to meet your mark?”** This invites viewers to choose their own practical reapplication cue without adding an app, reminder technology, giveaway, or other production mechanism.

**Product and Brand Role**  
SunTrace SPF 50 is shown clearly at both application points: first as the protection users start with, then as the action the ritual is designed to prompt. The included reusable UV-sensitive sticker is not presented as a diagnostic tool or the campaign’s substitute product; it is SunTrace’s distinctive physical reminder device. Its temporary reveal makes the invisible difference between covered and exposed skin visually understandable, while the product remains the hero of the routine.

**Audience and Objective**  
Young adults often do not forget sunscreen because they reject it; they forget because an outdoor day has no memorable moment that tells them to return to it. “Make Your Mark. Meet It Again.” gives them a concrete, personal trigger tied to the next break they already take. The opening sticker placement creates instant visual curiosity, the reveal gives the short video a memorable payoff, and the immediate second product application connects that payoff to the intended behavior. The result makes SunTrace’s protection visible while giving users a simple ritual they can repeat across ordinary outdoor plans.

Weak criteria: executability
Reasons: The concept builds a distinctive, product-specific behavior around the included UV-sensitive sticker rather than using it only as a demonstration prop. The phrase and ritual of leaving a mark and meeting it again give the product a memorable role in prompting reapplication. Strategic fit is strong because the idea directly addresses the stated problem of young adults forgetting to reapply during ordinary outdoor activities, shows SPF 50 at both application points, and works within TikTok, Reels, and university social channels without relying on celebrities, technology, or expensive production. Audience impact should be strong because the close-up sticker reveal provides immediate visual curiosity and a satisfying payoff, while the personal reminder cue is easy to understand. Executability is practical for low-to-medium production, but results may vary with UV intensity, exposure duration, skin application, and how clearly the temporary reveal appears on camera. The repeated setup could also become formulaic, and the demonstration may be misread as an exact measurement of UV damage despite the disclaimer.
Refinement suggestions: Make the reapplication cue more concrete and consistently timed by linking the reveal to a natural break such as lunch, arriving at a second location, or changing activities, while keeping the user's chosen pause central. Establish a short, repeatable video structure with a clear time or activity transition so viewers understand that the sticker is a reminder ritual rather than a diagnostic test. Keep the disclaimer visually prominent at the reveal and use language such as 'visual reminder of sun exposure' instead of implying that the shape proves an exact level of protection or damage. Demonstrate correct sticker placement, removal, and reuse in a brief shot, and test framing, lighting, and exposure conditions so the reveal remains legible in user-generated videos. Vary the outdoor contexts and participants to preserve the strong repeatable format without making every execution feel identical.
