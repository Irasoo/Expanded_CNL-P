Brand:
FoldBrew

Product:
A premium instant coffee concentrate packaged inside an edible, water-soluble coffee cup sleeve. When the sleeve is placed in hot water, it dissolves and becomes one serving of coffee.

Target audience:
Urban office workers aged 22-35 who regularly buy takeaway coffee but want a faster and lower-waste option at work.

Objective:
Drive first-time trial of FoldBrew and make the product's unusual "dissolve the packaging to make the coffee" behavior memorable.

Key product benefit:
The package itself becomes the coffee, so the user does not need a coffee machine, coffee pod, or disposable coffee sachet.

Channels:
Instagram, TikTok, and short-form digital video.

Budget:
Medium.

Constraints:
Use ordinary product photography and short-form video production. Do not use celebrities, large public installations, or fictional environmental claims. The central idea must visibly demonstrate the package dissolving into the drink.

==================== AGENT OUTPUT ====================

=== Final Creative Concept ===
**Concept: “Remove the Coffee Run, Not the Sleeve”**

FoldBrew turns the most unexpected part of the coffee routine into the campaign’s signature: the package is not something to unwrap, discard, or set aside. It is the coffee.

Each short-form video opens on a familiar office interruption: a worker sees **“COFFEE RUN,” “NO MACHINE,”** or **“12 MINUTES BETWEEN MEETINGS”** appear beside their task. Their hand reaches for the FoldBrew sleeve as though preparing to remove packaging, then pauses on a simple instruction: **“Don’t remove it.”**

The worker places the complete FoldBrew sleeve into a mug, adds hot water, and the camera holds on a clear close-up of the sleeve dissolving into the drink. As the sleeve disappears, the interruption disappears with it: the **“COFFEE RUN”** note folds away, the empty machine queue clears, or the meeting countdown stops at the moment the coffee is ready. The finished mug remains, with no separate pod or sachet to deal with.

Every execution follows the same memorable reversal:

**1. Spot the office coffee obstacle.**  
**2. Keep the package and place it in hot water.**  
**3. Watch the package become one serving of coffee.**

The end line is:

**“Remove the coffee run, not the sleeve. FoldBrew.”**

The product demonstration is the creative idea, not simply a proof point. Viewers see the sleeve before, during, and after its transformation, making FoldBrew immediately recognizable as the coffee package that gets poured instead of thrown away. The direct first-use prompt, **“Place it. Watch it dissolve. Drink,”** gives first-time users a clear action to try.

The concept speaks directly to urban office workers who need coffee between tasks and often rely on takeaway runs, machines, pods, or sachets. It preserves the original “remove the interruption” benefit while giving it a more distinctive product-specific mechanism: the thing that normally creates a disposal step is the thing that makes the coffee. The executions require only an office setting, a mug, hot water, FoldBrew, simple interruption props, and a controlled close-up dissolve, making the format repeatable across Instagram, TikTok, and short-form digital video.

Weak criteria: executability
Reasons: The concept has a strong, memorable product-specific reversal: the item that appears to be packaging is deliberately kept and dissolved to make the coffee. That gives FoldBrew a distinctive visual behavior rather than relying on a generic convenience or sustainability message. Strategic fit is strong because the idea directly addresses office interruptions, takeaway coffee habits, lack of equipment, and first-time trial while keeping the product demonstration central. Audience impact should be high because the opening situations are recognizable to urban office workers and the dissolve creates a satisfying reveal, although the office-obstacle framing is somewhat familiar and may become repetitive across executions. Executability is practical for Instagram, TikTok, and short-form video using ordinary office settings and product photography, but the dissolve must be filmed clearly and credibly every time. The initial instruction to not remove the sleeve could also briefly confuse viewers, particularly if the packaging appearance suggests normal disposal.
Refinement suggestions: Make the first two seconds unmistakable by showing a clear visual contrast between a normal sleeve-removal gesture and the corrected action: a hand starts to peel, a bold 'Keep it on' cue appears, and the complete sleeve immediately enters the mug. Prioritize one highly repeatable visual system for the interruptions so the campaign feels branded rather than like a series of unrelated office jokes. Keep the dissolve in a continuous, well-lit close-up with enough duration for viewers to understand that the package itself becomes the serving, and use the finished mug as a consistent proof point. Test alternate end-line wording against 'Remove the coffee run, not the sleeve' to ensure the key action is understood without requiring explanation, while preserving the memorable reversal. Avoid suggesting broader environmental outcomes beyond the directly demonstrated absence of a separate pod or sachet.
