==================== USER INPUT 1/1 ====================
Brand:
FoldBrew

Product:
A premium instant coffee concentrate packaged inside an edible, water-soluble coffee cup sleeve. When the sleeve is placed in hot water, it dissolves and becomes one serving of coffee.

Target audience:
Urban office workers aged 22-35 who regularly buy takeaway coffee but want a faster and lower-waste option at work.

Objective:
Drive first-time trial of FoldBrew and make the product's unusual "dissolve the packaging to make the coffee" behavior memorable.

Key product benefit:
The package itself becomes the coffee, so the user does not need a coffee machine, coffee pod, or disposable coffee sachet.

Channels:
Instagram, TikTok, and short-form digital video.

Budget:
Medium.

Constraints:
Use ordinary product photography and short-form video production. Do not use celebrities, large public installations, or fictional environmental claims. The central idea must visibly demonstrate the package dissolving into the drink.

==================== AGENT OUTPUT ====================

=== Final Creative Concept ===
**Concept: “Not the Bin. The Mug.”**

**Core idea**  
Turn FoldBrew’s unusual packaging into a workplace reflex people have to unlearn. In every film, the sleeve initially looks like something that belongs in the office bin, then takes an unexpected route: into a mug of hot water, where it visibly dissolves and becomes the coffee.

The recurring visual reversal makes the product behavior distinctive and memorable. FoldBrew is not merely packaged coffee; its package is the preparation step.

**Main execution**  
Create a series of short vertical videos built around recognizable office coffee moments: a worker reaches for coffee during a busy morning, a meeting is about to start, or a desk worker pauses between tasks. Each film opens with a tight close-up of the FoldBrew sleeve moving toward the bin, accompanied by the on-screen question:

**“Where does this go?”**

Before it reaches the bin, the action sharply redirects to a clear mug of hot water. The sleeve is placed inside, and the camera stays close as it visibly dissolves into the drink. A clear before-and-after composition shows the same sleeve entering the mug and the finished coffee in its place.

The final frame shows the worker taking the coffee back to their desk, with the line:

**“Not the bin. The mug.”**  
**“FoldBrew: the package becomes the coffee.”**

The full transformation appears early in each edit and works without sound. The series can vary the office situation, but the repeated “wrong destination, right destination” pattern gives the films a recognizable signature while keeping production simple.

**Product and brand role**  
FoldBrew is the reason the visual reversal works. The coffee sleeve is not removed, discarded, or opened into another container; it is placed directly into hot water and becomes one serving of coffee. The demonstration communicates that users do not need a coffee machine, pod, or disposable coffee sachet for that serving.

**Audience and objective**  
The office scenarios reflect urban workers aged 22–35 who want takeaway-style coffee at work without leaving their desk or using a machine. The opening creates an immediate visual question, while the unexpected move into the mug gives the audience a satisfying payoff. Repeated across Instagram, TikTok, and short-form digital video, the format makes first-time trial feel easy to understand: when coffee is needed, put the FoldBrew sleeve in hot water and watch the package become the drink.

This gives FoldBrew a distinctive visual signature and directly communicates the unusual package-as-preparation behavior, supporting the first-time-trial objective. The office situations are relevant to urban workers aged 22-35, and the format is naturally adaptable to Instagram, TikTok, and short-form digital video. Showing the transformation early, using close product photography, and designing the films to work without sound make the idea practical within the stated production constraints. The concept also avoids unsupported environmental claims while still making the lower-waste benefit understandable through the absence of a discarded sleeve. Its main limitations are that the bin setup could initially create confusion or make the product feel like waste, and the repeated format may become predictable without enough variation. The coffee benefit, taste, and premium positioning are less prominent than the packaging behavior.
Refinement suggestions: Keep the bin-to-mug reversal as the core device, but make the product identifiable before the apparent disposal moment so the opening creates curiosity rather than uncertainty. Use a clear, repeated visual or supersized action showing the FoldBrew sleeve entering hot water and fully dissolving, followed by a finished coffee shot, so viewers cannot mistake the behavior for a metaphor or assume the sleeve is removed. Vary the office triggers and the timing of the interruption while preserving the same recognizable destination reversal. Add concise product cues that reinforce premium coffee quality and the practical benefit of needing no machine, pod, or sachet, without crowding the demonstration. Ensure the final frame clearly shows the exact preparation action and one serving of coffee, and use close-ups that make the dissolving process legible on small screens.
