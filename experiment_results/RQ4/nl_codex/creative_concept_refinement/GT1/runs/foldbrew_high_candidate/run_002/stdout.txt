# Creative Concept: **“Watch the Cup Become Coffee”**

## Core idea

FoldBrew turns the most unexpected part of making coffee—the packaging—into the preparation itself.

The campaign makes the product behavior the entire story: an office worker drops the FoldBrew sleeve into hot water, watches it dissolve, and sees the water become one serving of coffee. Instead of explaining that FoldBrew removes the need for a machine, pod, or disposable coffee sachet, the content demonstrates it visibly and memorably.

**Core line:**  
**“Drop the sleeve. Watch it become coffee.”**

## Principal execution

A series of short-form videos and still posts titled **“The Disappearing Coffee Run”** show ordinary urban office workers preparing FoldBrew at their desks.

Each video follows the same simple visual sequence:

1. A worker reaches for coffee during the workday.
2. The usual office coffee setup is absent: no coffee machine, pod, or disposable coffee sachet is used.
3. They place a FoldBrew sleeve into a clear cup of hot water.
4. A close-up visibly captures the sleeve dissolving in the water.
5. The water changes into the finished coffee.
6. The worker picks up the cup and gets back to work.

The key moment is filmed as a clear product demonstration rather than implied through animation or a verbal claim. The dissolve is shown in macro detail, with the cup kept in frame so viewers can see that the package itself is becoming the coffee.

The end frame shows the FoldBrew product alongside the finished drink, with the line:

**“The package is the coffee. Try FoldBrew.”**

## Brand and product role

FoldBrew is the central visual event, not merely a logo attached to a coffee-making story.

Its unusual format provides the campaign’s memorable transformation:

- The sleeve is the product packaging.
- The sleeve is placed directly into hot water.
- The sleeve dissolves.
- The result is one serving of coffee.

This directly expresses the key product benefit: FoldBrew can be prepared without a coffee machine, coffee pod, or disposable coffee sachet. The brand’s premium positioning can come through in restrained product photography, clean office settings, close-up texture, and a deliberate preparation ritual, without adding unsupported product claims.

## Target audience relevance

The executions are set in ordinary office moments familiar to urban workers aged 22–35: starting the workday, taking a mid-morning break, or needing coffee while remaining at their desk.

The creative frames FoldBrew as a practical alternative for people who regularly buy takeaway coffee but want something faster and lower-waste at work. It does not ask the audience to learn a new system or use extra equipment. The entire preparation is communicated through one simple action: place the sleeve in hot water and watch it become coffee.

The satisfying dissolve also gives the audience a reason to stop scrolling. The transformation is unusual enough to create curiosity, while the repeated office setting makes the use case immediately understandable.

## Channel executions

### Instagram Reels and TikTok: **“Did the packaging just disappear?”**

Short videos begin with the most visually surprising moment: a FoldBrew sleeve already dissolving in a clear cup.

On-screen text:

**“This is the coffee package.”**  
**“Watch what happens when it hits hot water.”**

The video then shows the full preparation from sleeve to finished drink. The final frame identifies FoldBrew and repeats:

**“The package is the coffee.”**

Each film can use a different office situation while keeping the same demonstration structure:

- A worker skips leaving the office for takeaway coffee.
- A worker prepares coffee at their desk.
- A colleague notices the sleeve disappearing and asks, “Where did the coffee go?”
- A worker holds up the empty cup and the finished drink, making the transformation clear.

The scenarios should remain grounded in the brief: urban office workers, workday coffee occasions, and the need for a faster, lower-waste option at work.

### Short-form digital video: **“One Cup. One Drop.”**

A tighter six-to-fifteen-second cut focuses almost entirely on the transformation:

- FoldBrew sleeve in hand.
- Sleeve placed into hot water.
- Macro shot of dissolution.
- Finished coffee.
- Product and line.

The video can use a clean sound design built around the physical action: the cup being placed down, hot water being poured, and the subtle movement of the dissolving sleeve. No celebrity, large installation, or fictional environmental statistic is needed; the product behavior carries the idea.

### Instagram stills and carousels: **“From Sleeve to Coffee”**

Still photography presents the transformation as a three- or four-frame sequence:

1. **Before:** FoldBrew sleeve beside a clear cup of hot water.
2. **During:** The sleeve visibly dissolving.
3. **After:** The finished coffee, with the packaging no longer separate from the drink.
4. **Explanation:** “No coffee machine. No pod. No disposable coffee sachet.”

The photography should use an ordinary office desk, natural or practical office lighting, and clear cups that allow the dissolution to remain visible. The product should be shown at a scale where the sleeve and its transformation are easy to understand.

## Tone and visual style

The tone is curious, satisfying, and practical rather than exaggerated.

The visual language should be:

- Clean and premium.
- Close-up and tactile.
- Centered on the cup and sleeve.
- Set in recognisable office environments.
- Simple enough that the product behavior is never obscured.

The camera should prioritize the moment of dissolution. A real-time or carefully captured close-up can be used, with edits only where needed to keep the content short. The demonstration should not rely on fictional visual effects that could make the product behavior seem simulated.

## Suggested supporting copy

- **“The sleeve is the coffee.”**
- **“Drop it in. Watch it dissolve. Get your coffee.”**
- **“No machine. No pod. No disposable coffee sachet.”**
- **“Coffee that starts with the package.”**
- **“Your coffee cup just disappeared.”**

The claims remain tied to the brief: FoldBrew’s package dissolves into one serving of coffee, and preparation does not require a coffee machine, coffee pod, or disposable coffee sachet.

## Why the concept serves the objective

The campaign drives first-time trial by making the product easy to understand in a single visual sequence. Someone unfamiliar with FoldBrew does not need to decode a technical explanation: they see the sleeve placed in hot water, visibly dissolving, and becoming coffee.

That same transformation makes the product memorable. The unusual behavior is not treated as a secondary feature or explained in small print; it is the opening, middle, and payoff of every execution.

By placing the demonstration in ordinary office moments, the concept connects the product’s distinctive format to the audience’s stated need for a faster, lower-waste coffee option at work—using only short-form video and ordinary product photography within the stated production constraints.
