==================== USER INPUT 1/1 ====================
Brand:
SunTrace

Product:
A facial sunscreen that is transparent when applied normally. The package includes reusable UV-sensitive stickers. When a sticker is placed on the skin before sun exposure and removed later, the protected area remains unchanged while the surrounding exposed area temporarily reveals the sticker shape.

Target audience:
Young adults aged 18-30 who understand that sunscreen is important but frequently forget to reapply it during outdoor activities.

Objective:
Make the invisible protective effect of sunscreen visually understandable and motivate users to remember sunscreen application and reapplication.

Key product benefit:
The sunscreen provides SPF 50 protection, while the included UV-sensitive demonstration stickers make the effect of sun exposure easier to visualize.

Channels:
TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social media communities.

Budget:
Low to medium.

Constraints:
Use only the real sunscreen and included demonstration stickers. Do not claim that the temporary visual demonstration measures exact UV damage. Do not use celebrities or expensive locations. The idea must be understandable within a short video.

==================== AGENT OUTPUT ====================

=== Final Creative Concept ===
## Concept: **“The Reapply Receipt”**

### Core Idea

Young adults often treat sunscreen like a task they completed once in the morning. SunTrace turns that mistaken “done” feeling into a visible **receipt for an unfinished routine**.

The included UV-sensitive sticker is the receipt itself. Placed over freshly applied SunTrace SPF 50 before going outdoors, it stays with the person through an ordinary day. When they remove it later, the temporary sticker-shaped contrast appears: the protected area remains unchanged while the surrounding exposed area reveals the sticker shape.

The reveal is not a measurement of exact UV damage. It is a simple visual reminder:

**You applied sunscreen. You stayed outside. The routine is not finished yet.**

**Campaign line:**  
**“Still outside? Your sunscreen routine is still open.”**

### Main Execution: **“Receipt Check” Short Videos**

Every TikTok or Reel opens with the visual payoff, so the idea is understood immediately.

#### 1. The receipt appears — first two seconds

A close-up shows someone peeling off the SunTrace sticker. The sticker-shaped contrast becomes visible.

On-screen text:

**“Applied before class.”**  
**“Still outside after lunch.”**  
**“No reapply.”**

A second caption lands over the reveal:

**“Your reapply receipt.”**

The reaction is direct and familiar:

**“I thought I was done.”**

#### 2. Quick rewind to the start

The video cuts back to the beginning of the day. The person applies SunTrace SPF 50, places the reusable UV-sensitive sticker over the treated area, and leaves for class or an outdoor activity.

A simple status graphic appears in the corner:

**SUNSCREEN ROUTINE**  
**Started — Not finished**

The graphic is not a separate app or technology. It is a consistent receipt-style caption system created with ordinary text overlays.

#### 3. The everyday distraction

Fast cuts show the person continuing through a normal day: crossing campus, sitting outside, meeting friends, getting food, or watching an outdoor event.

The person does not forget sunscreen because they are careless; they forget because the day keeps moving. The sticker remains visible as a small reminder, but the person only acts when they pause and check it.

#### 4. The receipt closes

After the reveal, the person immediately reapplies SunTrace while still outdoors.

Final supers:

**“Applied before you went out.”**  
**“Still out? Reapply.”**

The pack and reusable sticker appear in the final frame with the clarification:

**“UV-exposure demonstration only. Does not measure exact UV damage.”**

### Product and Brand Role

SunTrace creates the entire demonstration.

- **SunTrace SPF 50** is applied beneath the sticker and provides the protection being represented.
- The **included reusable UV-sensitive sticker** creates the physical receipt: a temporary, sticker-shaped visual contrast between the sunscreen-treated area and surrounding exposed skin.
- The sticker’s removal is the campaign’s signature action. It reveals the reminder rather than merely displaying the product.
- Reapplication is shown as the clear behavioral payoff, so the sticker check never suggests that the demonstration replaces sunscreen use or reapplication.

The “receipt” is not a printed prop or a generic reminder. It is the visible trace created by using SunTrace as intended.

### Audience Connection and Objective

The format speaks to the specific habit of the audience: remembering sunscreen before leaving, then mentally closing the task while remaining outdoors.

The opening reveal creates an immediate “wait—what happened?” moment. The rewind makes the cause clear within seconds:

**Applied → stayed outside → forgot to reapply → noticed → reapplied.**

The emotional payoff is recognition rather than fear. Viewers see a familiar mistake—“I thought I was done”—and receive a memorable rule they can repeat:

**Still outside? Your sunscreen routine is still open.**

### Channel and Production

The concept is built for 15–20-second vertical videos using only:

- SunTrace SPF 50
- The included reusable UV-sensitive sticker
- A phone camera
- Ordinary campus, home, or outdoor locations
- Simple cuts and on-screen captions

Each version can begin with a different relatable situation—before class, before a walk, before lunch, or before meeting friends—while preserving the same recognizable format: **show the receipt first, rewind to the first application, then close the routine with reapplication**.

University social posts can use concise prompts such as:

**“Applied before class. Still outside? Check your receipt.”**

The visual demonstration remains consistent across TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university communities without requiring celebrities, paid locations, extra technology, or unsupported claims.
