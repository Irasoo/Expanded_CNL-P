==================== AGENT OUTPUT ====================

=== Final Creative Concept ===
**Concept: “The Invisible Reminder”**

SunTrace turns a temporary sticker-shaped reveal into a memorable prompt: sun exposure is not always visible while it is happening, just as transparent sunscreen protection cannot be seen after application. The creative makes that invisible relationship understandable without presenting the sticker as a measurement tool.

The principal execution is a 15-second vertical video filmed by a young adult in an ordinary outdoor setting, such as a campus lawn. The opening close-up shows SunTrace SPF 50 being applied normally, followed by one of the included reusable UV-sensitive demonstration stickers being placed on the skin before sun exposure. On-screen text reads: **“Sunscreen disappears. Sun exposure doesn’t.”**

A fast sequence shows the person continuing an outdoor activity. Midway through, they bring SunTrace back into frame and reapply before staying outside. The video then cuts to the sticker being removed later. The protected area remains unchanged while the surrounding exposed area temporarily reveals the sticker shape. The final frame pairs the product and sticker with: **“Make the invisible memorable. Apply. Reapply. SunTrace SPF 50.”**

A small, readable caption states: **“Temporary visual demonstration of sun exposure. Does not measure exact UV damage.”**

SunTrace remains the source of SPF 50 protection, while the included sticker provides the visual moment that makes otherwise unseen sun exposure easier to understand. The reveal is not positioned as a test of protection or damage; it is a reminder that exposure can occur without an immediate visible signal.

The short reveal structure is designed for TikTok and Instagram Reels, with the same video shared through university social media communities. It uses only the real product, included stickers, a phone camera, a peer-aged participant, and an accessible outdoor location, keeping production within a low-to-medium budget. For 18-to-30-year-olds who already understand sunscreen’s importance but forget to reapply during activities, the sticker shape gives the routine a concrete, repeatable memory cue.
