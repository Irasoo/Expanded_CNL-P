=== Final Creative Concept ===
**SunTrace: The Plan Has a Sunprint**

Young adults do not usually decide to skip sunscreen; they decide they will only be outside for a minute. Then the plan changes. SunTrace turns that exact change into a recognizable “sunprint” moment: a reusable UV-sensitive sticker acts as a visual bookmark for the outdoor plan, while SunTrace SPF 50 protects the skin around it.

Each short video begins with a specific, familiar plan: “cross campus before class,” “one game,” or “coffee for 20 minutes.” The person applies transparent SunTrace SPF 50 and places one included sticker on their skin before going outside. A simple phone lock-screen time jump or text message then changes the plan: “Want to stay for the match?” “Dinner outside?” “One more round?”

The moment the plan changes, the sticker becomes the cue. The person pauses, reapplies SunTrace, and continues outside. The video then cuts to the sticker being removed. The protected area remains unchanged while the surrounding exposed area temporarily reveals the sticker’s shape, creating a distinctive “sunprint” of the time spent outside. The reveal is identified as a visual demonstration of the difference between protected and exposed skin, not a measurement of exact UV damage.

The format’s signature is the same every time: a plan gets a new chapter, the SunTrace sticker marks the pause to reapply, and the skin reveals the “sunprint” afterward. The closing line is: **“When the plan gets a new chapter, give your protection one too.”** A concise prompt follows: “Apply before going out. Reapply when the plan runs long.”

SunTrace is essential to the idea: its transparent SPF 50 formula provides the invisible protection, while the included reusable sticker makes that protection visually understandable. The sticker is not presented as a sensor or damage meter; it is a memorable reminder to check protection when an outdoor plan changes. Each execution needs only the real sunscreen, one reusable sticker, a phone, and an ordinary campus or outdoor setting, making the repeatable format practical for TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social communities.
