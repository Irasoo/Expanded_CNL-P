Brand:
SunTrace

Product:
A facial sunscreen that is transparent when applied normally. The package includes reusable UV-sensitive stickers. When a sticker is placed on the skin before sun exposure and removed later, the protected area remains unchanged while the surrounding exposed area temporarily reveals the sticker shape.

Target audience:
Young adults aged 18-30 who understand that sunscreen is important but frequently forget to reapply it during outdoor activities.

Objective:
Make the invisible protective effect of sunscreen visually understandable and motivate users to remember sunscreen application and reapplication.

Key product benefit:
The sunscreen provides SPF 50 protection, while the included UV-sensitive demonstration stickers make the effect of sun exposure easier to visualize.

Channels:
TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social media communities.

Budget:
Low to medium.

Constraints:
Use only the real sunscreen and included demonstration stickers. Do not claim that the temporary visual demonstration measures exact UV damage. Do not use celebrities or expensive locations. The idea must be understandable within a short video.

=== Final Creative Concept ===
**SunTrace Check: Make the Invisible Part of the Routine**

SunTrace turns the moment young adults usually forget sunscreen into a simple, repeatable visual check. Every short video follows the same three-shot format: **apply, step outside, check and reapply**.

A vertical video opens on a close-up of two matching areas on the creator’s forearms, each with one of SunTrace’s reusable UV-sensitive stickers. The creator applies SunTrace SPF 50 to only one area, leaving the other without sunscreen. Fixed labels remain on screen: **“SunTrace applied”** and **“No sunscreen.”** The creator then leaves for an ordinary activity such as crossing campus, sitting outside, or meeting friends.

After the outdoor moment, the video returns to the same close-up and framing. The stickers’ visible UV responses create a straightforward contrast between the two areas. A compact caption reads: **“A visible UV response, not a measurement of damage or SPF.”** The creator looks at the comparison, then applies SunTrace to the area that needs reapplication.

The recurring visual is the **SunTrace Check**: the same paired sticker placement, the same fixed labels, and the same final action. It gives the product an ownable demonstration while keeping production simple enough for anyone to repeat with a phone and daylight. The opening line is: **“You remembered to go outside. Did you remember to reapply?”**

The closing message is: **“Make the invisible part of your routine. SunTrace SPF 50. Apply. Go. Check. Reapply.”**

The format is designed for TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social communities. It speaks directly to young adults who already know sunscreen matters but lose track of it during everyday outdoor plans, turning SunTrace’s included stickers into a practical reminder to apply before going out and reapply during the day.
