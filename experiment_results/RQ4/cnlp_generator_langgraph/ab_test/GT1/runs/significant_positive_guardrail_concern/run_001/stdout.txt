**Checkout Button Guardrail Test**

**Hypothesis:** Changing the checkout button from “Continue” to “Buy Now” will increase purchase conversion rate.

Over 14 days, Control had 10,000 users and a 10.0% conversion rate (1,000 conversions), while Variant had 10,000 users and a 12.0% conversion rate (1,200 conversions). The Variant improved conversion by 2.0 percentage points, a 20.0% relative lift. The difference is statistically significant (two-sided p-value: 0.00000619), with a 95% confidence interval for the absolute difference of approximately +1.13 to +2.87 percentage points.

The experiment is marked underpowered for the specified 1 percentage point minimum detectable effect at 80% power. This is a limitation, but it does not override the statistically significant observed result. No sample ratio mismatch was detected, so the allocation does not invalidate the experiment.

**Guardrail assessment:** There is a clear guardrail concern. Customer support contact rate increased from 2% to 5%, and the product team explicitly considers this a meaningful negative effect.

**Recommendation: Investigate.** Do not ship broadly yet. Although “Buy Now” produced a statistically significant conversion increase, the meaningful deterioration in the support-contact guardrail requires investigation before rollout.

**Next steps:** Identify the reasons for the additional support contacts, determine whether they are attributable to the button-text change, and assess whether the issue can be mitigated. Then rerun or extend validation with purchase conversion and support contact rate monitored together before making a shipping decision.
