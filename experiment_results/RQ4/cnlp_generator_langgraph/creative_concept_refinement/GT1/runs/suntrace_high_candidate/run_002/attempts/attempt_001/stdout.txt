Brand:
SunTrace

Product:
A facial sunscreen that is transparent when applied normally. The package includes reusable UV-sensitive stickers. Target audience:
Young adults aged 18-30 who understand that sunscreen is important but frequently forget to reapply it during outdoor activities.

Objective:

The sunscreen provides SPF 50 protection, while the included UV-sensitive demonstration stickers make the effect of sun exposure easier to visualize.

Low to medium.

==================== AGENT OUTPUT ====================
FINAL CREATIVE CONCEPT
“Trace Check” is a repeatable short-form video ritual that turns SunTrace’s temporary sticker reveal into an immediate cue to reapply, making an easy-to-forget sunscreen habit visible and memorable.

Each video opens mid-activity with a tight shot of a young adult lifting the edge of a familiar sticker shape on their skin. On-screen text asks, “Trace showing?” They remove the reusable UV-sensitive demonstration sticker, revealing that the covered area remains unchanged while the surrounding exposed area temporarily reveals the shape. The same quick sequence follows every time: reveal, reach for SunTrace, reapply. A concise caption remains visible during the reveal: “Visual demonstration of sun exposure. Not a measure of exact UV damage.”

The video then quickly rewinds to show the setup before the activity: transparent SunTrace SPF 50 applied to the face, followed by placement of the included sticker. It snaps back to the reapplication moment and ends with the sunscreen and sticker held together beside the revealed shape: “See the trace. Do the Trace Check. Reapply SunTrace.”

For TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social communities, every execution uses the same recognizable three-beat format: place it before going out, reveal it during the activity, then visibly reapply. Different students and ordinary outdoor activities provide variety without changing the ritual. SunTrace remains essential throughout: its SPF 50 sunscreen provides the protection, while its included reusable sticker creates the product-specific visual cue that turns invisible sun exposure into a practical reminder to apply and reapply.

Evaluation rationale: The concept gives SunTrace a strong, product-specific role by turning the included UV-sensitive sticker into a recognizable visual ritual rather than treating it as a one-time demonstration. The repeated three-beat structure is distinctive, easy to recognize, and directly connected to the invisible nature of sunscreen protection. It addresses the target audience's tendency to forget reapplication by creating a visible cue during an outdoor activity, while keeping SPF 50 and the sunscreen itself central. The reveal is likely to be immediately understandable and visually suitable for TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social channels. The format is also practical for a low-to-medium budget because it relies on ordinary activities, real users, the existing product, and the included stickers. The main limitation is that the visual reveal may demonstrate exposure without inherently proving that reapplication is required at that exact moment, so the behavioral connection depends on clear framing and repetition. The ritual could also become formulaic if every execution follows the same sequence without enough variation in situations or personalities.
Refinement suggestions: Make the reminder logic more precise by framing the Trace Check as a prompt to consider reapplication after time outdoors, sweating, swimming, or other relevant activity, rather than implying that the sticker precisely measures protection or UV damage. Keep the disclaimer visible but concise and ensure the video clearly shows that the sticker demonstration is separate from the SPF protection claim. Strengthen the short-form hook by showing the reveal in the first second and using a consistent visual or audio signature for the three beats: place, trace, reapply. Build variation around realistic university and outdoor moments while preserving the ritual, such as walking between classes, studying outdoors, or attending a recreational activity. End with a simple, repeatable reapplication action and a clear SunTrace product shot so the audience remembers both the cue and the brand.
