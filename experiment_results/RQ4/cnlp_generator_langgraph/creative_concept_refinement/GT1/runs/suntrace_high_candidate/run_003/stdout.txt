==================== USER INPUT 1/1 ====================
Brand:
SunTrace

Product:
A facial sunscreen that is transparent when applied normally. The package includes reusable UV-sensitive stickers. When a sticker is placed on the skin before sun exposure and removed later, the protected area remains unchanged while the surrounding exposed area temporarily reveals the sticker shape.

Target audience:
Young adults aged 18-30 who understand that sunscreen is important but frequently forget to reapply it during outdoor activities.

Objective:
Make the invisible protective effect of sunscreen visually understandable and motivate users to remember sunscreen application and reapplication.

Key product benefit:
The sunscreen provides SPF 50 protection, while the included UV-sensitive demonstration stickers make the effect of sun exposure easier to visualize.

Channels:
TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social media communities.

Budget:
Low to medium.

Constraints:
Use only the real sunscreen and included demonstration stickers. Do not claim that the temporary visual demonstration measures exact UV damage. Do not use celebrities or expensive locations. The idea must be understandable within a short video.

==================== AGENT OUTPUT ====================
FINAL CREATIVE CONCEPT
**“Trace the Moment”** turns SunTrace’s sticker reveal into a recognizable visual cue tied to the exact moments young adults tend to forget sunscreen.

Each short vertical video opens on a tight close-up of a SunTrace sticker shape already visible on the creator’s skin, paired with the hook: “This is the reminder I nearly forgot.” The video then jumps back to show the creator applying transparent SunTrace SPF 50, placing the reusable UV-sensitive sticker on the same area, and heading into a familiar outdoor activity such as a campus lunch, pickup game, festival queue, or park study session.

At a natural break in the activity, the creator removes the sticker on camera. The protected area remains unchanged while the surrounding exposed area temporarily reveals the shape. A clear overlay appears with the reveal: “Shows sun exposure. Not an exact UV damage reading.” The creator immediately uses the revealed shape as a behavioral cue, taking out SunTrace and reapplying before returning to the activity. No protection duration is stated or implied.

Every execution follows the same memorable sequence: **spot the trace, rewind the moment, reveal, reapply**. Different included sticker shapes and everyday settings keep the format varied, while the repeated close-up opening makes it identifiable as SunTrace content across TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social communities.

The closing shot pairs the sunscreen and reusable sticker with: **“When the trace appears, SunTrace reappears.”** The format uses only SunTrace SPF 50, its included stickers, ordinary outdoor locations, and simple close-up phone footage, making it practical for creators and university communities to repeat.

Evaluation rationale: The repeated 'spot the trace, rewind the moment, reveal, reapply' structure is a strong, memorable, product-specific use of SunTrace's real sticker demonstration rather than a generic sunscreen message, earning high originality. Strategic fit is strong because the concept directly targets forgotten reapplication during recognizable young-adult outdoor activities, makes otherwise invisible protection visually understandable, keeps both the SPF 50 sunscreen and sticker central, avoids duration claims, and explicitly states that the reveal is not an exact UV damage reading. Audience impact is high because the visible shape provides an immediate short-video hook, the rewind supplies context, and the reveal leads directly to a simple behavior; however, repeated executions could become predictable, and the opening line is somewhat familiar. Executability is strong because the format uses phone footage, ordinary locations, real product components, and a repeatable sequence suited to TikTok, Reels, and university communities. It still requires careful continuity between the opening reveal and rewind, consistent close-up visibility, and disciplined presentation so viewers do not mistake the sticker effect for a precise reapplication timer or UV-damage measurement.
Refinement suggestions: Preserve the recognizable sequence and product-led reveal, but make the reapplication trigger more precise: frame the trace as a memorable prompt during a natural activity break, not evidence that protection has expired. Keep the qualification legible during the reveal and reinforce it in captions without slowing the video. Develop a small set of standardized shot templates, lighting guidance, and approved overlay language so community creators can reproduce the effect clearly and consistently. To prevent repetition, vary the forgotten-moment hook, activity transition, sticker shape, and creator reaction while retaining the same branded opening close-up and reveal-to-reapplication behavior.
