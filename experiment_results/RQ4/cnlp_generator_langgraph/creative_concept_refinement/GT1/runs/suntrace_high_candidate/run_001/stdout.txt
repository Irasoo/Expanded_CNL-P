==================== USER INPUT 1/1 ====================
Brand:
SunTrace

Product:
A facial sunscreen that is transparent when applied normally. The package includes reusable UV-sensitive stickers. When a sticker is placed on the skin before sun exposure and removed later, the protected area remains unchanged while the surrounding exposed area temporarily reveals the sticker shape.

Target audience:
Young adults aged 18-30 who understand that sunscreen is important but frequently forget to reapply it during outdoor activities.

Objective:
Make the invisible protective effect of sunscreen visually understandable and motivate users to remember sunscreen application and reapplication.

Key product benefit:
The sunscreen provides SPF 50 protection, while the included UV-sensitive demonstration stickers make the effect of sun exposure easier to visualize.

Channels:
TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social media communities.

Budget:
Low to medium.

Constraints:
Use only the real sunscreen and included demonstration stickers. Do not claim that the temporary visual demonstration measures exact UV damage. Do not use celebrities or expensive locations. The idea must be understandable within a short video.

==================== AGENT OUTPUT ====================
FINAL CREATIVE CONCEPT
**“The Sun Leaves a Shape”**

SunTrace turns an invisible sunscreen reminder into a visual moment young adults can instantly understand and remember.

Each short video opens on the reveal, before viewers know what they are seeing: a close-up of a temporary sticker-shaped contrast on the creator’s skin. The creator asks, “Why is this shape still here?” A quick rewind shows the simple setup: they applied transparent SunTrace SPF 50 facial sunscreen, placed one of the included UV-sensitive stickers on their skin, and went outside for an everyday activity such as playing basketball, walking across campus, or sitting at an outdoor café.

When the sticker is removed, the area beneath it remains unchanged while the surrounding exposed area temporarily reveals the sticker shape. A small, fixed caption appears beside the reveal: **“Visual demonstration only. Not a UV-damage measurement or reapplication timer.”**

The reveal then becomes a behavioral cue tied to a natural activity transition. As the creator finishes the game, leaves the lawn, or returns from a break, they say, “That’s my reminder,” and immediately reapply SunTrace. The video ends with the product and reusable sticker in frame: **“See the shape. Reapply SunTrace.”**

SunTrace is essential to the idea: its transparent SPF 50 sunscreen is the protection people apply, while its included stickers create the distinctive visual reminder that makes otherwise invisible sun exposure easier to notice. The format is designed for TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social communities, using one person, one familiar outdoor location, natural daylight, and the real product. To keep the reveal clear and repeatable, creators use the same close-up framing, sticker placement, camera angle, and lighting direction for the before-and-after shots, with the reveal occupying the first seconds of the video and the qualification kept legible but unobtrusive.

Evaluation rationale: The concept has a strong, memorable product-specific device: the included UV-sensitive sticker makes an otherwise invisible sunscreen effect visible without requiring a new technology, celebrity, or expensive production. This gives it stronger originality than generic sunscreen reminders. Strategic fit is strong because the reveal directly supports the objective of making sun exposure understandable, keeps SunTrace SPF 50 central, and connects the demonstration to reapplication during realistic outdoor transitions. The fixed qualification appropriately avoids claiming that the effect measures exact UV damage or provides an exact reapplication timer. Audience impact should be high because the opening image creates immediate curiosity and the sticker-shaped reveal is easy to understand in a short vertical video. The behavioral line, "See the shape. Reapply SunTrace," provides a clear memory hook. Executability is practical for low-to-medium budgets, using one creator, familiar locations, natural light, the real product, and included stickers. However, consistent visibility of the temporary contrast may depend on exposure conditions, skin tone, timing, camera continuity, and lighting. The need to show a convincing reveal while keeping the disclaimer legible also adds production risk. The concept's reapplication cue is useful but somewhat broad, since it is tied to finishing an activity rather than a clearly defined product-use moment.
Refinement suggestions: Preserve the reveal-first structure and product-specific sticker demonstration, but make the behavior more repeatable by establishing a small set of explicit reapplication triggers such as leaving the court, returning from lunch outdoors, or starting the trip home. Use the same wording and end-frame treatment across videos so the trigger becomes a recognizable SunTrace habit. Test the demonstration in representative daylight and filming conditions before scaling creator content, and define a simple visual standard for sticker placement, framing, removal timing, and exposure so the reveal remains understandable without implying scientific precision. Keep the qualification visible long enough to be read while integrating it into the on-screen layout rather than making it feel like an afterthought. Consider opening with a slightly clearer visual or verbal cue after the initial mystery so viewers understand that the shape is a sunscreen demonstration within the first seconds.
