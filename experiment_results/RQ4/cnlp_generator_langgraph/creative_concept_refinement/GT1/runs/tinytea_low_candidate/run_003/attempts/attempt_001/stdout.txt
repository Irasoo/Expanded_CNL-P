==================== AGENT OUTPUT ====================
FINAL CREATIVE CONCEPT
**TinyTea: The Seven-Second Cold Break**

TinyTea turns its launch into one recognizable student ritual: chill the bottle, open it, take a sip, and hold up the bottle for a seven-second “cold break.” The action makes the product benefit visible and specific: a lightly sweetened iced tea designed for a quick, refreshing pause between classes, study sessions, and social plans.

During the first seven days, a globally famous celebrity appears in person at five live public events in five major international cities. At each event, the celebrity performs the same simple action with a cold TinyTea: opens the bottle, takes a sip, and leads the crowd through the seven-second countdown. Each custom interactive digital billboard responds to the event activation by showing the countdown and the locally filmed celebrity moment. The repeated format creates one campaign asset that can be understood immediately in every city and reused across Instagram, TikTok, and university-community channels.

The dedicated TinyTea augmented-reality application uses the bottle as its trigger. When students scan or point the application at a TinyTea bottle, it opens the seven-second countdown and records their own cold break. Prompts such as “cold, lightly sweet, ready for the next thing” connect the ritual to the product experience without adding a second campaign message. Students can post their clips to Instagram and TikTok, while local university communities receive compilations and short edits that frame the international moments as an invitation for students at home to join the same break.

The campaign’s central visual system remains deliberately narrow: one chilled bottle, one sip, one seven-second countdown, and one repeatable short-form edit. Professional cinematic production captures the five celebrity appearances in a consistent format, with lightweight vertical edits prepared for social distribution during the first month. The bottle stays in the center of every frame, and the celebrity functions as the launch trigger rather than the message itself.

Execution is organized as a fixed seven-day production sequence: confirm celebrity access, city-event permissions, billboard vendors, application development, and production resources first; capture the five appearances and billboard content next; then deploy the application, publish the launch film and short edits, and distribute participation materials through local university communities. The same countdown assets, bottle-triggered interaction, event format, and editing template are used across all five cities to limit unnecessary variation and support rapid delivery.

Because the brief requires a globally famous celebrity appearing in person, five international live events, five custom interactive billboards, a dedicated augmented-reality application, professional cinematic production, and completion within seven days, the concept is conditional on TinyTea securing the required funding, access, permissions, vendors, and production capacity before the seven-day schedule begins. The stated budget of below $1,000 cannot support those mandatory elements; without additional confirmed resources or an explicit amendment to the brief, production cannot responsibly proceed.

Evaluation rationale: The seven-second cold break is a clear, repeatable ritual that gives the chilled bottle and quick refreshment a meaningful role, although countdown challenges and short social rituals are familiar formats rather than exceptionally original ones. Strategic fit is strong at the message level: the bottle remains central, the lightly sweetened cold-tea benefit is explicit, and the short vertical format suits Instagram, TikTok, and student communities. However, the five international activations are poorly aligned with awareness for a small local beverage launch, even though the concept correctly includes them because the brief makes them mandatory. The action is immediately understandable and potentially engaging for students between classes, but requiring a dedicated application creates substantial participation friction compared with native social tools. Executability is critically weak: a sub-$1,000 budget and seven-day production window cannot credibly cover an in-person globally famous celebrity, five international events, custom interactive billboards, a dedicated augmented-reality application, permissions, travel, vendors, and professional cinematic production. Reusing one format reduces creative variation but does not resolve the fundamental cost, access, logistics, development, and scheduling conflicts. The concept appropriately identifies this impossibility instead of pretending the required resources exist.
Refinement suggestions: Preserve the seven-second ritual, centered bottle imagery, single product message, and reusable vertical edit structure. Before production, resolve the contradictory brief by securing documented funding, celebrity access, city permissions, billboard vendors, application capacity, and production resources, or by formally changing the mandatory constraints; no responsible execution plan exists under the stated budget and deadline. If the constraints remain unchanged, replace the speculative seven-day sequence with explicit feasibility gates, dependencies, lead times, and a no-go decision when resources are unconfirmed. Strengthen local strategic relevance by making the five international moments feed directly into local university distribution and participation, with clear campus-specific calls to action. Reduce audience friction within the mandatory application by minimizing installation and onboarding steps and making the bottle scan open directly into the seven-second recording experience.
