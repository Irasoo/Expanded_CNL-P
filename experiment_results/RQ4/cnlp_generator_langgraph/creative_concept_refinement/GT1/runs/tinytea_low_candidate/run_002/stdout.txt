==================== AGENT OUTPUT ====================
FINAL CREATIVE CONCEPT
**TinyTea Cold Front: Campus to the World, Back to Campus**

TinyTea launches from one local university with a single, recognizable action: one student hands a cold TinyTea to another, each vertical video ending on the bottle label and the line, “Lightly sweet. Served cold.” The handoff begins on campus, travels through five synchronized international appearances, and returns to the same university, making the global activity a route back to TinyTea’s actual launch audience rather than a spectacle detached from the local brand.

A globally famous celebrity appears in person as the designated link in the handoff during five short live public events in five major international cities. Every event follows the same compact format: the celebrity receives a chilled TinyTea, holds it toward a custom interactive digital billboard to trigger one cold-front sweep, then passes the bottle onward as the screen names the local university and prompts students there to post the next handoff. There are no separate performances or city-specific creative builds; one bottle-triggered interaction, one event cue, and one visual template are repeated in all five cities.

The dedicated TinyTea AR mobile application performs one function only. When two people frame a real bottle during a handoff, it adds condensation to the bottle and sends a brief frost wave from one person to the other. The effect resolves to the TinyTea pack, “Lightly sweet. Served cold,” and a prompt to share the vertical clip to TikTok or Instagram with the local university community tagged. The bottle remains necessary to the interaction, keeping TinyTea central to every celebrity, billboard, app, and student post.

Professional cinematic production is confined to one tightly specified master sequence assembled from the celebrity handoffs, the identical billboard reaction in each city, and close shots of cold TinyTea. The required deliverables are one concise launch film and reusable vertical cuts for TikTok, Instagram, and local university community accounts throughout the first month. Student participation then carries the campaign locally through the same low-complexity handoff format, requiring only a cold bottle, two people, and a phone.

Production begins only after written confirmation of the celebrity’s five in-person appearances, all five public venues and permits, interactive billboard access, AR development capacity, professional production staffing, travel logistics, and sufficient funding. Because those mandatory requirements cannot be procured and completed within seven days for a total production budget below $1,000, the campaign cannot responsibly enter production under the brief as written. Launch therefore requires a formal revision of the budget or deadline, or confirmed provision of the required resources outside the stated production budget; once that gate is cleared, the standardized event, billboard, AR, and filming specifications keep execution as narrow and repeatable as the mandatory scope permits.

STANDARDIZED EVALUATION

Weakest criteria: executability
Evaluation rationale: The repeated bottle handoff, cold-front visual language, and return to the originating university create a distinctive product-specific structure rather than a generic launch video. TinyTea remains visible and functionally necessary across the celebrity appearances, billboards, AR effect, and student posts, which gives the product a clear role. However, the concept is strategically weakened by the scale mismatch: a very low-budget local launch for university students is built around five international events, a globally famous celebrity, custom billboards, an AR application, and professional cinematic production. The handoff is understandable and potentially engaging on social channels, but the global spectacle may feel detached from the local student audience, and the product benefit remains mostly a repeated line rather than a particularly persuasive reason to try the tea. Executability is effectively nonviable under the explicit brief because the mandatory requirements cannot realistically be procured, produced, permitted, and delivered within seven days and below $1,000. The concept correctly identifies this problem, but acknowledging infeasibility does not make the campaign workable.
Refinement suggestions: Preserve the strong handoff mechanic, the cold-front visual cue, and the product-centered bottle interaction, but make the local university the primary narrative and conversion destination. Define a specific student action and payoff, such as posting a handoff to unlock a campus sampling moment or limited local challenge, so awareness connects more directly to trial. Make each international appearance serve a clear route back to the launch campus rather than functioning as an end in itself, and simplify the message around the lightly sweetened, cold-serving benefit. Most importantly, the brief requires a formal resource change before refinement can produce an executable plan: the budget, seven-day deadline, or external provision of celebrity, venue, billboard, AR, travel, and production resources must be revised. Without that change, no creative refinement can resolve the core feasibility failure while retaining every mandatory element.
