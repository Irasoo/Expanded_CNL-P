SunTrace

Young adults aged 18-30 who understand that sunscreen is important but frequently forget to reapply it during outdoor activities.

==================== AGENT OUTPUT ====================
Final Creative Concept
**Concept: “Don’t Let Protection Disappear”**

A short-form video makes SunTrace’s invisible SPF 50 protection visible through a simple before-and-after sticker reveal, then turns that reveal into a memorable reapplication cue.

The video opens with a young adult preparing for an everyday outdoor activity. They place one included UV-sensitive demonstration sticker on their cheek, apply transparent SunTrace facial sunscreen normally, and hold the product to camera. On-screen text reads: **“Sunscreen is invisible. Its job isn’t.”**

After time outdoors, a quick transition returns to the same person and setting. They remove the sticker, revealing its temporary shape against the surrounding exposed area while the protected area remains unchanged. A clear caption states: **“A visual demonstration of sun exposure, not a measurement of UV damage.”**

The reveal immediately prompts the person to reapply SunTrace. The closing product shot carries the line: **“See the reminder. Reapply the protection.”** A final caption identifies **“SunTrace SPF 50 facial sunscreen + reusable demonstration stickers.”**

Designed for TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social communities, the execution uses a repeatable vertical-video structure: place, apply, go outside, reveal, reapply. Fast cuts and the recognizable sticker shape make the idea understandable within seconds, while the product remains central to both the SPF 50 protection and the visual reminder. Young adults can recreate the format during ordinary outdoor activities without celebrities, expensive locations, or anything beyond the sunscreen and included stickers.

Evaluation reasons: The concept earns strong scores because it turns SunTrace's distinctive included stickers into a memorable, product-specific visual device rather than relying on generic sunscreen messaging. Strategically, it directly addresses the brief by making an invisible benefit visible, keeping the SPF 50 product central, prompting reapplication, using the specified short-form channels, and including the required limitation that the demonstration does not measure UV damage. The place-apply-reveal-reapply sequence is immediately understandable and gives young adults a clear behavioral cue. Execution is practical for a low-to-medium budget and needs no celebrities, expensive locations, or additional demonstration materials. The main weakness is that the reveal demonstrates exposure but does not inherently show when protection should be reapplied, so the connection between seeing the sticker shape and reapplication is asserted rather than fully established. The wording about the surrounding area being exposed may also create visual or procedural ambiguity if sunscreen has been applied normally around the sticker.
Refinement suggestions: Preserve the simple reveal and repeatable vertical-video format, but clarify the application sequence so viewers understand exactly which areas receive sunscreen and what the sticker is demonstrating. Avoid implying that the sticker detects protection wearing off or determines the correct reapplication time. Strengthen the behavioral cue with a concise, accurate transition tied to the outdoor activity, such as showing the reveal as a memorable reminder to follow the product's reapplication directions. Keep the disclaimer legible during the reveal rather than presenting it too quickly, and use a bold, recognizable sticker shape that remains easy to read on a small mobile screen.
