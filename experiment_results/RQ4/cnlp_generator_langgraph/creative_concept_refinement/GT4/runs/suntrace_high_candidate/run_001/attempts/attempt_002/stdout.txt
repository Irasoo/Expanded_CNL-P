==================== AGENT OUTPUT ====================
Final Creative Concept
**Concept Name: “Still Outside?”**

SunTrace turns a familiar young-adult habit, staying outside longer than planned, into a memorable sunscreen reapplication cue. The included UV-sensitive sticker creates a temporary visual check-in after outdoor exposure; it does not measure UV damage or determine protection levels.

Each short vertical video opens on a plan that sounds quick:

**“Just walking to class.”**

The creator applies SunTrace SPF 50, places the reusable sticker on their skin, and heads out. Rapid cuts show the plan extending in recognizable ways:

**“Coffee?”**  
**“Sit outside?”**  
**“Walk home?”**

With each change, the recurring on-screen prompt returns:

**“Still outside?”**

After the extended outdoor activity, the creator removes the sticker in close-up. The protected area remains unchanged while the surrounding exposed area temporarily reveals the sticker shape. Instead of treating the reveal as a measurement or “receipt,” the creator recognizes it as the SunTrace check-in: their quick trip became much more time outdoors than expected.

SunTrace SPF 50 immediately returns to the center of the action. The creator checks the product directions and reapplies accordingly before continuing with the day.

The repeatable format is:

**Apply. Plans change. Check in. Reapply as directed.**

Different videos feature common university-day detours, such as a short lunch becoming an afternoon on the lawn, one errand becoming a long walk, or a study break moving outside. The changing plans keep each execution fresh, while **“Still outside?”** becomes a simple, ownable prompt that viewers can associate with SunTrace.

On-screen text keeps the demonstration accurate and concise:

**“SunTrace SPF 50 applied before heading out.”**  
**“Plans lasted longer?”**  
**“Use the included sticker as a visual reminder only.”**  
**“It does not measure UV damage or protection.”**  
**“Reapply SunTrace according to product directions.”**

The final frame shows the sunscreen and reusable sticker together:

**“SunTrace. Still outside? Check in.”**

SunTrace provides the SPF 50 protection; the included sticker makes an otherwise invisible outdoor moment noticeable. The concept directly addresses forgetful 18–30-year-olds by attaching reapplication to something they already experience: plans changing and outdoor time adding up. Every video can be filmed on a phone in ordinary campus settings and repeated across TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social communities.

Evaluation reasons: The concept gives the included UV-sensitive sticker a clear role in making invisible sunscreen protection visually noticeable without claiming that it measures UV damage or protection levels. The repeated "Still outside?" prompt is distinctive, easy to remember, and closely connected to the target audience's habit of extending casual outdoor plans. The product remains central because the videos begin with SunTrace SPF 50 application and end with checking the product directions and reapplying accordingly. The format is immediately understandable in a short vertical video, supports relatable university scenarios, and can be produced with a phone, the real product, and ordinary campus settings. The main limitation is that the visual reveal occurs after exposure, so it is more naturally a retrospective reminder than a precise trigger for when reapplication is due. The repeated disclaimer language may also compete slightly with the simple behavioral hook in very short videos.
Refinement suggestions: Make the behavioral sequence even more explicit: show the creator noticing that plans have extended, checking the product directions, and reapplying before continuing, while making clear that the sticker is only a visual reminder and not a timing or protection gauge. Keep "Still outside?" as the primary recurring line, but move detailed accuracy language into brief captions or a final frame so the videos stay fast and memorable. Establish a consistent visual signature for the check-in, such as the same close-up reveal and a simple reminder action, while varying only the university detour. Include scenarios where the creator remembers to reapply during the day, rather than relying mainly on the post-exposure reveal, to make the concept more directly motivate the intended behavior.
