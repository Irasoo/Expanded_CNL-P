==================== USER INPUT 1/1 ====================
Brand:
SunTrace

Product:
A facial sunscreen that is transparent when applied normally. The package includes reusable UV-sensitive stickers. When a sticker is placed on the skin before sun exposure and removed later, the protected area remains unchanged while the surrounding exposed area temporarily reveals the sticker shape.

Target audience:
Young adults aged 18-30 who understand that sunscreen is important but frequently forget to reapply it during outdoor activities.

Objective:
Make the invisible protective effect of sunscreen visually understandable and motivate users to remember sunscreen application and reapplication.

Key product benefit:
The sunscreen provides SPF 50 protection, while the included UV-sensitive demonstration stickers make the effect of sun exposure easier to visualize.

Channels:
TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social media communities.

Budget:
Low to medium.

Constraints:
Use only the real sunscreen and included demonstration stickers. Do not claim that the temporary visual demonstration measures exact UV damage. Do not use celebrities or expensive locations. The idea must be understandable within a short video.

==================== AGENT OUTPUT ====================
FINAL CREATIVE CONCEPT
**SunTrace: “The Sun Leaves a Trace. Make Reapplication Part of Your Route.”**

Each short video opens with a close-up of a reusable UV-sensitive sticker being peeled from a forearm. The protected shape remains unchanged while the surrounding exposed skin temporarily reveals the sticker’s outline. On-screen text immediately clarifies: **“The sticker shows sun exposure around a protected shape. It does not measure exact UV damage.”**

The video rewinds to the start of an ordinary campus route. Before leaving class, the student applies transparent SunTrace SPF 50 to their face according to the package directions, then places the included demonstration sticker on clean skin on their forearm. The sticker is a **visual bookmark for the time spent outdoors**, not a timer or test of when reapplication is required.

The student then follows the route: class to the quad, lunch to an outdoor activity, or campus to the trip home. At the next natural transition, the sticker enters the frame as the cue to repeat the same action: stop, take out SunTrace, and reapply according to the package directions before continuing outside. The product application is shown clearly and prominently, while the sticker remains visibly separate so the roles are unmistakable: the sticker makes exposure easier to see; SunTrace helps protect the face when used as directed.

The final shot returns to the peel reveal, with the student’s face protected by SunTrace and the sticker’s temporary outline visible on the forearm. The end line is: **“Leave a trace. Don’t leave out reapplication.”** Supporting text reads: **“SunTrace SPF 50. Sticker demonstration only. Does not measure exact UV damage. Use SunTrace as directed.”**

The repeatable format is **peel, rewind, apply, route, transition, reapply**. Each video uses one recognizable campus transition as its opening cue, making the behavior specific to how young adults actually move through an outdoor day. The sticker gives the videos their distinctive visual signature, while the repeated SunTrace application makes the product—not the demonstration—the central solution to forgetting reapplication.

Production requires only the real SunTrace sunscreen, its included reusable UV-sensitive stickers, a phone camera, and ordinary campus locations. The peel reveal is filmed under consistent outdoor conditions and edited to the opening so the result is immediate for viewers without implying that it happens instantly.

Weak criteria: originality, strategic_fit, audience_impact, executability
Evaluation reasons: The concept gives SunTrace a strong, product-specific visual device: the reusable sticker creates a temporary contrast between a protected shape and surrounding exposure, making an otherwise invisible sunscreen benefit understandable in a short video. The peel-and-reveal opening is distinctive and provides an effective recurring signature for TikTok and Reels. Strategic fit is strong because the route structure connects the demonstration to a real forgetting problem and turns campus transitions into prompts to reapply, while the sunscreen remains the central protective product and the sticker is explicitly framed as a demonstration rather than a measurement tool. Audience impact is likely high because the reveal is immediate, visual, and easy to understand, and the campus-route behavior is relatable to the stated audience. Executability is also strong: the format uses the supplied product and stickers, a phone camera, and ordinary locations, and can be adapted across multiple campus scenarios. The main limitations are that the repeated disclaimer and distinction between the sticker and sunscreen may make the videos feel information-heavy, and the sticker could still be interpreted by some viewers as a precise reapplication timer or proof of skin damage despite the clarification. The demonstration also requires sufficiently consistent outdoor conditions and editing to make the reveal readable without overstating its speed or significance.
Refinement suggestions: Keep the peel-reveal structure and route-based cue, but simplify the communication hierarchy so the first seconds prioritize the visual reveal and the reapplication behavior. Use a brief, persistent or end-frame disclaimer rather than allowing explanatory text to compete with the opening action, while retaining clear wording that the sticker demonstrates exposure and does not measure exact UV damage. Make the behavioral instruction more precise by presenting the sticker as a reminder to check and follow the sunscreen label's reapplication directions, not as the trigger that determines when reapplication is due. Show the face application and reapplication consistently, and use the forearm sticker only for the visual demonstration so the roles cannot be confused. Establish a repeatable filming setup and include varied route transitions, lighting conditions, and skin tones to keep the effect credible and readable. Consider ending each video with a simple campus-specific action cue, such as applying before the next outdoor segment, so the memorable reveal leads directly to the intended habit.
