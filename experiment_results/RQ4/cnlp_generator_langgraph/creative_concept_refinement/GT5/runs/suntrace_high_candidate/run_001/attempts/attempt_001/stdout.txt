==================== USER INPUT 1/1 ====================
Brand:
SunTrace

Product:
A facial sunscreen that is transparent when applied normally. The package includes reusable UV-sensitive stickers. When a sticker is placed on the skin before sun exposure and removed later, the protected area remains unchanged while the surrounding exposed area temporarily reveals the sticker shape.

Target audience:
Young adults aged 18-30 who understand that sunscreen is important but frequently forget to reapply it during outdoor activities.

Objective:
Make the invisible protective effect of sunscreen visually understandable and motivate users to remember sunscreen application and reapplication.

Key product benefit:
The sunscreen provides SPF 50 protection, while the included UV-sensitive demonstration stickers make the effect of sun exposure easier to visualize.

Channels:
TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social media communities.

Budget:
Low to medium.

Constraints:
Use only the real sunscreen and included demonstration stickers. Do not claim that the temporary visual demonstration measures exact UV damage. Do not use celebrities or expensive locations. The idea must be understandable within a short video.
================== END USER INPUT 1/1 ==================

==================== AGENT OUTPUT ====================
FINAL CREATIVE CONCEPT
**SunTrace: “Before Outside. Again While Outside.”**

The short video opens in a fixed close-up as two SunTrace stickers are peeled from adjacent, coin-sized demonstration areas on a creator’s forearm. The first area temporarily reveals the sticker shape; the second remains visually unchanged. Two simple on-screen labels appear directly beneath them: **“Untreated demo”** and **“SunTrace SPF 50.”** The hook asks: **“Same sun. What changed?”**

A rapid rewind shows the setup without changing the camera angle. One sticker was placed in each demonstration area. SunTrace was applied around the **“SPF 50”** sticker, while the small **“Untreated demo”** area was left untreated solely for comparison. A single wider shot shows the creator applying SunTrace normally to their face before returning to an everyday outdoor activity on campus.

The video returns to the opening close-up for the peel and side-by-side reveal. The product enters the same frame beside the two areas, immediately connecting the visible difference to SunTrace. A legible disclaimer remains on screen throughout the reveal: **“Visual demonstration only. Not an exact measure of UV damage.”**

A natural break in the activity becomes the memory cue: the creator sits back down after getting water, sees SunTrace beside their bag, and reapplies it to their face. The closing pack shot carries one line: **“Before outside. Again while outside.”** The demonstration does not suggest an exact reapplication time; it turns SunTrace’s otherwise invisible protection into a visual reminder to apply and remember it again during outdoor activity.

Each version uses the same repeatable production setup: two small adjacent areas, one locked close-up for setup and reveal, the same camera distance and exposure, and one continuous period of ordinary daylight before both stickers are removed together. Creators film the reveal before the other shots and proceed only when the temporary contrast is clearly visible on camera. This keeps the edit to four beats: **reveal, rewind, explanation, reapply**, using only SunTrace, its included reusable stickers, a familiar outdoor location, and simple on-screen graphics.

Evaluation reasons: The concept earns strong marks for originality because the paired sticker reveal turns SunTrace's invisible SPF 50 benefit into a memorable, product-specific visual rather than relying on generic sunscreen messaging. Strategic fit is strong: it directly addresses forgetful 18-30-year-olds, gives both the sunscreen and included stickers central roles, creates a natural reapplication cue, fits short-form social channels, and explicitly avoids presenting the demonstration as an exact measure of UV damage. Audience impact is high because the opening contrast and 'Same sun. What changed?' hook are immediately legible, while the rewind supplies a clear explanation and the campus activity feels relevant to the audience. Executability is good but not exceptional. The fixed setup, ordinary location, limited props, and four-beat edit suit a low-to-medium budget and are repeatable, but the concept depends on daylight, camera exposure, application consistency, and a clearly visible temporary contrast. The instruction to film the reveal first reduces publishing risk but does not eliminate failed or visually ambiguous demonstrations, and a persistent disclaimer may compete with labels and action in a short mobile video.
Refinement suggestions: Preserve the side-by-side reveal, rewind structure, product-in-frame proof, and natural reapplication moment. Improve execution by defining a simple creator shot guide for framing, exposure lock, sticker placement, sunscreen application around the protected comparison shape, minimum label size, and a clear stop condition when contrast is not visible. Keep the untreated area as small as possible and describe it consistently as a limited visual comparison. Reduce screen congestion by reserving fixed zones for the two labels and disclaimer, then test them for legibility in vertical mobile viewing. Strengthen the reminder behavior by making the water break visually consistent across versions and linking the pack's reappearance directly to the closing line, without implying a precise reapplication interval. Consider a few tightly controlled opening variants that retain the same proof, such as leading with the unchanged SunTrace area before revealing the untreated comparison, to identify the clearest hook without adding production complexity.
