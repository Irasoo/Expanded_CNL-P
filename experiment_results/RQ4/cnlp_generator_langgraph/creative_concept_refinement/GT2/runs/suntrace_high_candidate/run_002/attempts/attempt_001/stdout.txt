==================== USER INPUT 1/1 ====================
Brand:
SunTrace

Product:
A facial sunscreen that is transparent when applied normally. The package includes reusable UV-sensitive stickers. When a sticker is placed on the skin before sun exposure and removed later, the protected area remains unchanged while the surrounding exposed area temporarily reveals the sticker shape.

Target audience:
Young adults aged 18-30 who understand that sunscreen is important but frequently forget to reapply it during outdoor activities.

Objective:
Make the invisible protective effect of sunscreen visually understandable and motivate users to remember sunscreen application and reapplication.

Key product benefit:
The sunscreen provides SPF 50 protection, while the included UV-sensitive demonstration stickers make the effect of sun exposure easier to visualize.

Channels:
TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social media communities.

Budget:
Low to medium.

Constraints:
Use only the real sunscreen and included demonstration stickers. Do not claim that the temporary visual demonstration measures exact UV damage. Do not use celebrities or expensive locations. The idea must be understandable within a short video.

==================== AGENT OUTPUT ====================
Final Creative Concept
“SunTrace: See What You Usually Miss”

A short-form social video campaign that makes sunscreen’s invisible protection visible using only SunTrace sunscreen and its included reusable UV-sensitive stickers. In each video, a young adult applies SunTrace SPF 50 facial sunscreen, places a sticker on their cheek or forearm, and goes about a normal outdoor activity such as walking across campus, sitting outside, or meeting friends. After sun exposure, they remove the sticker: the protected area remains unchanged while the surrounding exposed area temporarily reveals the sticker’s shape. On-screen text clarifies that the demonstration shows the contrast between protected and exposed skin, not an exact measurement of UV damage.

The reveal is followed by a simple reminder: “Sunscreen works even when you can’t see it. Reapply before you forget.” The creator then applies SunTrace again, turning the visual reveal into a memorable reapplication cue. Videos use a quick setup, exposure, sticker removal, and reveal within a short TikTok or Instagram Reel, with versions suitable for university social media communities. The concept gives SunTrace a clear role: SPF 50 protection that remains transparent on application, paired with reusable stickers that help users understand the invisible benefit and remember to apply and reapply during outdoor activities.

Evaluation: The concept has strong originality because it turns SunTrace's included UV-sensitive stickers into the central creative device, making an otherwise invisible sunscreen benefit visually demonstrable in a product-specific way. It is strategically well aligned with the brief: the demonstration uses the real product, reinforces SPF 50 protection, addresses forgetfulness around reapplication, and makes clear that the effect is illustrative rather than an exact measure of UV damage. Audience impact is likely to be high because the sticker removal creates a simple, surprising reveal that is well suited to short-form video and relevant to ordinary university and social activities. The concept is highly executable within the stated constraints, requiring only the sunscreen, reusable stickers, a phone camera, and low-complexity everyday locations. Its main limitations are that repeated executions could become formulaic, and the visual reveal may be interpreted as evidence of precise skin damage despite the disclaimer. The reapplication message is relevant but currently somewhat generic and may not fully solve the behavioral problem of remembering later.
Refinement suggestions: Preserve the sticker reveal as the core asset, but vary the situations and storytelling so the campaign does not become a repetitive before-and-after format. Make the reapplication cue more behaviorally specific by tying it to recognizable moments such as leaving class, arriving at an outdoor event, or returning from a break, and use concise recurring language that links the reveal directly to the habit. Keep the disclaimer close to the reveal and phrase it plainly so viewers understand that the contrast demonstrates protection visibility rather than measuring exact UV damage. Consider showing the creator setting a simple reapplication reminder immediately after the reveal, while keeping the product application prominent and natural. The short videos should establish the sticker, sunscreen, and exposure sequence quickly so the product benefit remains central rather than letting the visual effect become the entire story.
