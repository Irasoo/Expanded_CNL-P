==================== USER INPUT 1/1 ====================
Brand:
SunTrace

Product:
A facial sunscreen that is transparent when applied normally. The package includes reusable UV-sensitive stickers. When a sticker is placed on the skin before sun exposure and removed later, the protected area remains unchanged while the surrounding exposed area temporarily reveals the sticker shape.

Target audience:
Young adults aged 18-30 who understand that sunscreen is important but frequently forget to reapply it during outdoor activities.

Objective:
Make the invisible protective effect of sunscreen visually understandable and motivate users to remember sunscreen application and reapplication.

Key product benefit:
The sunscreen provides SPF 50 protection, while the included UV-sensitive demonstration stickers make the effect of sun exposure easier to visualize.

Channels:
TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social media communities.

Budget:
Low to medium.

Constraints:
Use only the real sunscreen and included demonstration stickers. Do not claim that the temporary visual demonstration measures exact UV damage. Do not use celebrities or expensive locations. The idea must be understandable within a short video.

==================== AGENT OUTPUT ====================
Final Creative Concept
**“The Sticker-Off SPF Reset”** is a repeatable short-form SunTrace ritual built for days outside on campus. Each video opens on an immediate close-up of a temporary sticker-shaped reveal with the hook: “You can’t see sunscreen. So make reapplying visible.”

The video quickly rewinds to show the responsible setup: a student places the included UV-sensitive demonstration sticker on their skin, applies SunTrace SPF 50 normally to all exposed skin according to the label, and heads into an everyday outdoor activity such as studying on the quad, walking to class, or meeting friends. No skin is intentionally left without sunscreen to create the effect.

Later, the student removes the sticker for the visual reveal. That action triggers a consistent transition: the sticker passes in front of the camera, and when it clears, the student is reapplying SunTrace. On-screen text reads, “Sticker off. SunTrace back on.” A second line directs viewers to “Reapply as directed on the label, especially while staying outdoors.” The reveal is presented as a memorable demonstration and behavioral cue, not as a timer or measurement.

Every video ends with the sunscreen and reusable stickers together: “SunTrace SPF 50. Make the invisible step unforgettable.” A persistent, legible disclosure reads, “Visual demonstration only. Does not measure exact UV damage.”

The same sticker-cover transition becomes a recognizable campus format that students can reproduce using only SunTrace, its included stickers, a phone, and an ordinary outdoor setting. Fast setup shots, one clear reveal, and one repeatable reapplication action keep the idea practical for TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social communities while making SunTrace central to both the visual proof and the habit it is designed to reinforce.

Evaluation: The concept gives SunTrace a distinctive, product-specific visual device by turning the included UV-sensitive sticker into both a reveal and a recognizable reapplication cue. The sticker-off transition is simple enough to become a repeatable short-form format, and the line "Sticker off. SunTrace back on" connects the demonstration directly to the desired behavior. Strategic fit is especially strong: the idea addresses young adults who forget to reapply, keeps SPF 50 and the sunscreen central, works in ordinary campus settings, and clearly respects the constraints by using only the product and included stickers. The disclosure and framing as a visual demonstration avoid claiming that the reveal measures exact UV damage. The concept should earn attention because the opening reveal is immediately legible and the rewind creates a clear before-and-after structure. Execution is practical for TikTok, Reels, and university social channels, although consistency of the visual reveal, lighting, timing, and skin placement may vary across user-generated videos. The idea is strong and workable, but it is not yet an unusually powerful emotional platform beyond the demonstration and reminder mechanic.
Refinement suggestions: Make the behavioral payoff more specific and repeatable by tying the sticker-off moment to a clear reapplication occasion, such as leaving an outdoor study session, after sweating, or before continuing an afternoon activity, while keeping the wording aligned with the product label. Establish a small set of standardized video beats and framing rules so the reveal remains recognizable across campus and user-created executions. Consider showing the reusable sticker being placed again or moved to a new area to reinforce continued use without implying that the sticker measures protection. Strengthen audience participation with a simple recreateable format built around the same transition and a consistent caption prompt, but avoid making participation dependent on unusual locations or extra materials. Keep the disclosure prominent but visually brief, and ensure every execution shows normal sunscreen application to all exposed skin so the demonstration cannot be mistaken for intentionally exposing unprotected skin.
