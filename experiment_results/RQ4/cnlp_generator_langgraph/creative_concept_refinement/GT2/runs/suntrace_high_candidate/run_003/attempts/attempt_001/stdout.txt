==================== USER INPUT 1/1 ====================
Brand:
SunTrace

Product:
A facial sunscreen that is transparent when applied normally. The package includes reusable UV-sensitive stickers. When a sticker is placed on the skin before sun exposure and removed later, the protected area remains unchanged while the surrounding exposed area temporarily reveals the sticker shape.

Target audience:
Young adults aged 18-30 who understand that sunscreen is important but frequently forget to reapply it during outdoor activities.

Objective:
Make the invisible protective effect of sunscreen visually understandable and motivate users to remember sunscreen application and reapplication.

Key product benefit:
The sunscreen provides SPF 50 protection, while the included UV-sensitive demonstration stickers make the effect of sun exposure easier to visualize.

Channels:
TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social media communities.

Budget:
Low to medium.

Constraints:
Use only the real sunscreen and included demonstration stickers. Do not claim that the temporary visual demonstration measures exact UV damage. Do not use celebrities or expensive locations. The idea must be understandable within a short video.

==================== AGENT OUTPUT ====================
Final Creative Concept
**“The SunTrace Check-In”** is a repeatable short-form format that turns ordinary campus transitions into memorable sunscreen reminders, using the included UV-sensitive sticker to make an invisible benefit visible.

Each Reel or TikTok opens with a participant peeling off a SunTrace sticker to reveal its temporary shape, immediately followed by the line: **“You remembered this. Did you remember to reapply?”** A quick matched before-and-after close-up makes even a subtle reveal easy to understand. Brief on-screen text reads **“Visual demonstration only,”** while the caption clarifies that the result does not measure exact UV damage or SPF performance.

The video jumps back to show how the check-in began. Before a normal outdoor activity, the participant applies SunTrace SPF 50 normally across their full face. On a small, separate demonstration area, they place the included sticker, then apply SunTrace over both the sticker and the surrounding skin so the demonstration setup is unmistakable.

The sticker case is placed beside an item already involved in the participant’s day, such as a water bottle or lunch container. At the next natural transition, like stopping for water, eating lunch, or heading into a building, seeing the case prompts the **SunTrace Check-In**: a fast, genuine reapplication moment captured in one shot. The recurring three-beat structure is **“Place. Go. Check in.”**

After normal outdoor exposure, the participant removes the sticker and films the reveal without filters, color enhancement, or extended time in the sun. The product, sticker, and repeated check-in remain visually linked throughout: the sticker makes sun exposure easier to notice, while SunTrace is the action the audience is prompted to repeat.

The closing shot shows SunTrace and the reusable sticker case returning beside the participant’s everyday item, with the line: **“See it. Check in. Reapply.”** University social communities can reproduce the format in familiar campus settings by choosing their own real transition, creating a recognizable SunTrace ritual that turns moments already built into young adults’ days into practical reapplication cues.

Evaluation: The concept has a differentiated, product-specific format: the included UV-sensitive sticker turns an otherwise invisible sunscreen benefit into a repeatable visual check-in. The recurring structure, campus transitions, and phrase “See it. Check in. Reapply.” fit young adults who forget sunscreen during active days and give the product a central role. The before-and-after reveal is likely to earn attention in short-form video, although the effect may be subtle and the audience may focus more on the visual trick than on the reapplication behavior. Executability is practical for low-to-medium budgets and the specified channels, but the setup requires several shots, clear explanation of sticker placement and sunscreen application, authentic outdoor exposure, and a visible reveal that may vary by conditions. The connection between the sticker demonstration and the need to reapply could also be misunderstood as proof of exact UV damage or SPF performance despite the disclaimer.
Refinement suggestions: Make the behavioral cue more immediate and simpler by centering each video on one recognizable transition, one short reapplication action, and one reveal. Demonstrate the sticker procedure in a consistent, easy-to-copy way and show the sunscreen application clearly enough that viewers understand the sticker is a visual demonstration rather than a measurement of skin damage or product performance. Keep the disclaimer brief but prominent in the video itself, not only in the caption. Strengthen the reapplication message by showing the participant noticing the check-in cue, applying SunTrace again, and then explaining that the reveal visualizes exposure while the sunscreen is the protective action. Use close framing, consistent lighting, and a small number of repeatable campus scenarios to improve clarity and reduce production complexity while preserving the ritual format.
