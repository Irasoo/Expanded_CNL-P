==================== USER INPUT 1/1 ====================
Brand:
SunTrace

Product:
A facial sunscreen that is transparent when applied normally. The package includes reusable UV-sensitive stickers. When a sticker is placed on the skin before sun exposure and removed later, the protected area remains unchanged while the surrounding exposed area temporarily reveals the sticker shape.

Target audience:
Young adults aged 18-30 who understand that sunscreen is important but frequently forget to reapply it during outdoor activities.

Objective:
Make the invisible protective effect of sunscreen visually understandable and motivate users to remember sunscreen application and reapplication.

Key product benefit:
The sunscreen provides SPF 50 protection, while the included UV-sensitive demonstration stickers make the effect of sun exposure easier to visualize.

Channels:
TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social media communities.

Budget:
Low to medium.

Constraints:
Use only the real sunscreen and included demonstration stickers. Do not claim that the temporary visual demonstration measures exact UV damage. Do not use celebrities or expensive locations. The idea must be understandable within a short video.

==================== AGENT OUTPUT ====================
FINAL CREATIVE CONCEPT
**Concept: “The SunTrace Pause”**

SunTrace turns the moment people usually forget sunscreen into a visible pause in their day.

Each short vertical video begins with a familiar outdoor routine: a swim, a game on campus, a bike ride, or sitting outside between classes. Before heading out, the person applies transparent SunTrace SPF 50 to their face and places one reusable UV-sensitive sticker on their skin. The sticker acts as a visual bookmark for the activity, marking the point at which they remembered protection.

At the next natural pause in the routine, they remove the sticker. The protected area remains unchanged while the surrounding exposed area temporarily reveals the sticker shape. The reveal is not presented as a measurement of skin damage; it shows, in one unmistakable image, that sun exposure can happen while protection feels invisible.

The person immediately reapplies SunTrace before returning outdoors. Every video follows the same recognizable signature:

**Apply. Pause. Reapply.**

On-screen copy reads:

**“Your day has pauses. Make one for SPF.”**  
**“See the SunTrace Pause.”**  
**“Reapply SunTrace SPF 50.”**

The recurring format makes the sticker more than a one-time demonstration: it becomes a branded cue attached to real moments when young adults naturally stop, such as after swimming, after sweating through an activity, or before heading back outside. The videos can be filmed by students and creators in ordinary locations, with the sticker reveal providing the consistent visual payoff across different routines.

SunTrace remains central because its transparent SPF 50 application explains why protection is easy to overlook, while the included sticker makes the invisible context of sun exposure visible. The product pack and application appear at the start and the reapplication appears at the end of every version. Near the reveal, a brief disclaimer reads: **“Temporary visual demonstration only. Does not measure exact UV damage.”**

Evaluation reasons: The concept is strongly product-specific: it turns SunTrace's transparent SPF 50 and included UV-sensitive sticker into a repeatable visual reveal rather than relying on generic sunscreen reminders. The 'Apply. Pause. Reapply.' structure connects ordinary breaks in outdoor activities to the desired reapplication behavior, directly addressing young adults who understand sunscreen but forget to reapply. The reveal is immediate and suitable for short vertical video, while the product pack, initial application, and reapplication keep SunTrace central. The concept also respects the stated constraints by using ordinary locations, student or creator filming, the real product and stickers, and an explicit disclaimer that the demonstration does not measure exact UV damage. Its main limitation is that the underlying reveal-demonstration format is inherently tied to the existing product mechanism, so the added creative leap is strong but not exceptionally novel. Audience impact may also depend on showing the reveal clearly and quickly; if exposure conditions produce a subtle result or the disclaimer is too brief to read, the message could lose clarity. Execution is practical at the stated budget, although continuity, safe exposure framing, sticker placement, lighting, and reliable visibility of the temporary shape require a controlled filming protocol.
Refinement suggestions: Preserve the recurring pause-and-reapply structure, but sharpen the first two seconds with the activity and sticker setup immediately visible, then cut directly to the natural pause and reveal. Establish a consistent close-up framing, lighting approach, sticker placement, and on-screen timing so the reveal reads clearly across every video without implying an exact measure of UV damage. Keep the disclaimer legible for long enough to understand and avoid language or visuals that suggest viewers should remain unprotected to produce a stronger result. Make each scenario identify a specific reapplication trigger, such as after swimming, after sweating, or before returning outside, so the behavioral cue is more actionable. Use one distinctive sticker shape or branded visual treatment consistently across student-created versions to strengthen recognition, while keeping the product pack and the final reapplication action prominent.
