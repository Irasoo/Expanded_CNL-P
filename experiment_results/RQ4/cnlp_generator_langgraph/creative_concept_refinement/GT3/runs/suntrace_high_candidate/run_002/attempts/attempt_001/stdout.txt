==================== USER INPUT 1/1 ====================
Brand:
SunTrace

Product:
A facial sunscreen that is transparent when applied normally. The package includes reusable UV-sensitive stickers. When a sticker is placed on the skin before sun exposure and removed later, the protected area remains unchanged while the surrounding exposed area temporarily reveals the sticker shape.

Target audience:
Young adults aged 18-30 who understand that sunscreen is important but frequently forget to reapply it during outdoor activities.

Objective:
Make the invisible protective effect of sunscreen visually understandable and motivate users to remember sunscreen application and reapplication.

Key product benefit:
The sunscreen provides SPF 50 protection, while the included UV-sensitive demonstration stickers make the effect of sun exposure easier to visualize.

Channels:
TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social media communities.

Budget:
Low to medium.

Constraints:
Use only the real sunscreen and included demonstration stickers. Do not claim that the temporary visual demonstration measures exact UV damage. Do not use celebrities or expensive locations. The idea must be understandable within a short video.

FINAL CREATIVE CONCEPT
**SunTrace: “Plans Changed. Check Your SunTrace.”**

**Core idea**  
Young adults often apply sunscreen for the plan they started with, then forget when one outdoor activity unexpectedly becomes another. SunTrace turns that familiar “plans changed” moment into a recognizable sunscreen habit: when the day gains another outdoor block, check your routine and reapply according to the product directions.

The included reusable UV-sensitive sticker creates the memorable visual payoff. Placed on a separate clean area of skin before exposure and removed later, it temporarily reveals a shape that makes otherwise invisible sun exposure noticeable. It shows exposure, not damage, SPF performance, or an exact time to reapply.

**Main execution: “I Was Only Going To…”**

Each short phone-shot video opens mid-action with a relatable confession:

> “I was only going to walk to class.”

A fast sequence shows SunTrace SPF 50 being applied according to its directions and a sticker placed on a small, separate clean area of skin. The original plan appears on screen, then changes:

> WALK TO CLASS  
> became  
> LUNCH OUTSIDE

Other episodes use equally familiar shifts:

> QUICK COFFEE  
> became  
> AFTERNOON ON THE QUAD

> ONE GAME  
> became  
> STAYING FOR PRACTICE

At the transition between plans, the person removes the sticker. Its temporary shape is revealed in close-up, immediately followed by the person looking at the next outdoor plan, picking up SunTrace, and checking the label before reapplying as directed.

A small, persistent caption during the reveal reads:

> “Shows exposure, not damage or sunscreen performance.”

Each video ends on the same repeatable three-beat sequence: changed plan, revealed signal, SunTrace reapplied.

> “Plans changed? Check your SunTrace.”  
> “Reapply as directed before you go again.”

**Series Format**  
Every episode uses a sticker shape tied to the day’s original plan, making the reveal both personal and immediately readable: a book for class, a cup for coffee, a note for music, or a simple sports shape. University social accounts can adapt the same format to real campus routines using ordinary locations, students’ own plans, SunTrace, and the included stickers.

The opening confession changes with each scenario, but the visual structure stays recognizable enough to become a social series: “I was only going to…” followed by the plan change viewers know often causes sunscreen to be forgotten.

**Product and Brand Role**  
SunTrace SPF 50 is the action that begins and continues the routine; the sticker is the brand’s visual demonstration tool that makes exposure noticeable. They appear together in the opening seconds, but their roles remain distinct: sunscreen provides protection when used as directed, while the sticker sits on separate clean skin and demonstrates that sun exposure occurred. It does not test the sunscreen, measure UV damage, or determine a precise reapplication interval.

**Audience and Objective Connection**  
The concept focuses on the unpredictable, social nature of university life rather than asking viewers to remember sunscreen at an arbitrary moment. It attaches reapplication to a concrete behavior young adults can recognize immediately: before another outdoor part of the day, check the product directions and renew the routine. The temporary sticker reveal supplies a strong visual hook for TikTok and Reels, while “Plans changed? Check your SunTrace” turns that reveal into a concise, repeatable reminder.
