==================== USER INPUT 1/1 ====================
Brand:
SunTrace

Product:
A facial sunscreen that is transparent when applied normally. The package includes reusable UV-sensitive stickers. When a sticker is placed on the skin before sun exposure and removed later, the protected area remains unchanged while the surrounding exposed area temporarily reveals the sticker shape.

Target audience:
Young adults aged 18-30 who understand that sunscreen is important but frequently forget to reapply it during outdoor activities.

Objective:
Make the invisible protective effect of sunscreen visually understandable and motivate users to remember sunscreen application and reapplication.

Key product benefit:
The sunscreen provides SPF 50 protection, while the included UV-sensitive demonstration stickers make the effect of sun exposure easier to visualize.

Channels:
TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social media communities.

Budget:
Low to medium.

Constraints:
Use only the real sunscreen and included demonstration stickers. Do not claim that the temporary visual demonstration measures exact UV damage. Do not use celebrities or expensive locations. The idea must be understandable within a short video.

==================== AGENT OUTPUT ====================
FINAL CREATIVE CONCEPT
**SunTrace: “The Shape You Forgot”**

The short video opens on the payoff: a student removes a SunTrace demonstration sticker after an afternoon outside, revealing a temporary sticker-shaped contrast on the skin. The opening caption makes the forgotten behavior immediate:

**“You remembered sunscreen for the first plan. What about the next one?”**

The video rewinds to a single locked close-up taken before the activity. The sticker is placed on a small area of skin, with the surrounding demonstration area divided simply between **SUNTRACE SPF 50** and **NO SUNSCREEN**. SunTrace is applied normally to its labeled side. The framing, placement, and labels remain unchanged when the video cuts back to the reveal, making the difference easy to understand without a complicated multi-shot comparison.

The reveal holds long enough to register, accompanied by a clearly legible caption:

**“Temporary visual demonstration only. Does not measure UV damage.”**

The sticker shape then becomes a memory cue, not a timer. The student finishes one outdoor activity and starts another, such as moving from a pickup game to sitting outside with friends. A match cut from the revealed shape to the reusable sticker beside the SunTrace bottle prompts one simple action: check SunTrace and reapply according to the product directions before continuing outdoors.

The closing shot shows the sunscreen and included reusable stickers together:

**“New outdoor plan? Check SunTrace.”**

Each version follows the same repeatable structure: reveal, rewind, locked demonstration, then a recognizable transition between two everyday outdoor activities. Students can recreate it in campus courtyards, parks, or sports areas using only SunTrace, its included stickers, one phone camera, persistent labels, and consistent close-up framing and lighting. The striking shape makes invisible protection easier to visualize, while the change of activity provides a practical cue to remember SunTrace again.
