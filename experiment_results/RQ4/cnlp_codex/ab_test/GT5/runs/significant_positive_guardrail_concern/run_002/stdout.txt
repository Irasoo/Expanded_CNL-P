## Checkout Button Guardrail Test

- **Hypothesis:** Changing the checkout button text will increase purchase conversion rate.
- **Change tested:** Control uses **“Continue”**; Variant uses **“Buy Now.”**
- **Primary metric:** Purchase conversion rate
- **Duration:** 14 days; duration alone does not establish experiment maturity.

### Results

| Arm | Sample size | Purchases | Conversion rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Control | 10,000 | 1,000 | 10.00% |
| Variant | 10,000 | 1,200 | 12.00% |

- **Absolute difference:** **+2.00 percentage points**
- **Relative lift:** **+20.00%**
- **Two-sided p-value:** **6.19 × 10⁻⁶**
- **95% confidence interval, Variant minus Control:** **+1.13 to +2.87 percentage points**
- **Statistical significance:** Statistically significant at the 0.05 level
- **Power status:** Underpowered; this diagnostic does not override the statistically significant result

### Guardrail assessment

There is a **guardrail concern**. Customer support contact rate increased from **2% to 5%**, and the product team explicitly considers this a meaningful negative effect.

### Recommendation: Investigate

The Variant produced a statistically significant increase in purchase conversion, but the meaningful increase in customer support contacts prevents a straightforward rollout decision.

**Next steps:**
1. Investigate the source and content of the additional support contacts.
2. Determine whether **“Buy Now”** creates confusion, incorrect expectations, or downstream checkout issues.
3. Assess whether the guardrail impact can be mitigated while retaining the conversion improvement.
4. After addressing the issue, run an appropriate follow-up evaluation and reassess both purchase conversion and customer support contact rate.
