=== Final Creative Concept ===
# FoldBrew: **This Package Goes in the Mug**

**Core idea**

Turn the instinct to discard coffee packaging into a moment of surprise. Every execution asks one immediate visual question: **Where does the FoldBrew sleeve go?**

A clearly branded FoldBrew sleeve is held between two possible destinations: an office bin and a clear mug of hot water. The hand moves toward the bin, pauses, then drops the sleeve into the mug. In one uninterrupted close-up, the package dissolves and becomes the coffee.

The campaign line is:

**“Not the bin. In your brew.”**

The existing usage mnemonic closes each execution:

**“Fold it. Drop it. Drink it.”**

This gives FoldBrew a distinctive, repeatable social mechanism built around the product truth: unlike the packaging associated with an ordinary office coffee, this package becomes the drink.

**Main execution**

Each short-form video opens directly on the choice, without a conventional office-problem setup. A branded FoldBrew sleeve appears in the foreground, with a bin on one side and a clear mug of hot water on the other. On-screen text asks:

**“Where does this go?”**

The hand initially moves toward the bin, matching the viewer’s expected answer. Before releasing the sleeve, it changes direction and places it into the mug. The camera stays close and the action remains continuous, making it clear that the intact sleeve entering the water is the same sleeve visibly dissolving into coffee.

As the transformation completes, the bin moves out of frame and the finished coffee remains beside the FoldBrew branding. The close is:

**“Not the bin. In your brew.”**  
**“Fold it. Drop it. Drink it.”**  
**“Try FoldBrew at your desk.”**

The hovering choice creates anticipation in the first seconds, while the uninterrupted dissolve resolves the disbelief. Viewers are not simply shown how FoldBrew works; they are invited to make a prediction and then see that prediction overturned.

**Social format**

Instagram Reels, TikTok, and short-form digital video use the same recognizable opening frame so the campaign becomes identifiable before the dissolve begins.

The primary edit plays the complete choice and transformation in real time. A shorter loop begins on the finished coffee, reverses until it becomes the intact sleeve, then plays forward as the sleeve is dropped back into the mug. The loop always retains the FoldBrew name and the clear sequence from intact package to finished drink, avoiding any impression that ordinary packaging waste has been placed in coffee.

Still and carousel executions preserve the same decision:

1. **Where does this go?** The branded sleeve between the bin and mug.
2. **Not the bin.** The sleeve entering the hot water.
3. **In your brew.** The sleeve dissolving into coffee.
4. **Fold it. Drop it. Drink it.** The finished FoldBrew at the desk.

**Product and brand role**

FoldBrew creates both the question and the answer. The idea only works because its edible, water-soluble sleeve contains the premium instant coffee concentrate and becomes one serving of coffee when placed in hot water.

The visible transformation demonstrates that preparing FoldBrew requires no coffee machine, coffee pod, or separate disposable coffee sachet. The branded sleeve remains clearly legible before it enters the water, and the resulting coffee stays on screen long enough for viewers to connect the unusual action to FoldBrew rather than interpreting it as a visual effect.

**Audience and objective**

The desk-level setting places FoldBrew inside the working day of urban office workers aged 22-35. The bin-or-mug choice uses a familiar workplace action but gives it an unexpected outcome designed for fast-moving social feeds.

The moment of mistaken expectation makes the dissolving package easier to remember, while the final invitation, **“Try FoldBrew at your desk,”** converts curiosity into a direct first-trial prompt. Repeating the same choice, dissolve, and mnemonic across placements builds recognition without adding extra messages or mechanics.

**Production approach**

The campaign uses ordinary product photography and practical short-form video: a branded FoldBrew sleeve, clear mug, hot water, office desk, and office bin. The dissolve is filmed against a clean, light background with close framing so the sleeve edges, movement through the water, and finished coffee remain legible on a mobile screen.

No celebrity, installation, complex visual effect, or unsupported environmental claim is required. The product’s real transformation provides the hook, demonstration, and payoff.

Evaluation reasons:
The concept turns FoldBrew's distinctive product behavior into a simple, memorable bin-versus-mug decision that would not work for an ordinary coffee product. This gives it strong originality while keeping the brand and product central. It addresses the brief directly: the intact branded sleeve is visibly placed in hot water, the dissolve is shown continuously, the desk setting is relevant to office workers, and the trial prompt supports the objective. The prediction-and-reversal structure should earn attention quickly in short-form feeds and make the unusual preparation behavior easier to remember. The repeated opening frame and concise mnemonic also support recognition across formats. Execution is practical within the stated constraints because it relies on ordinary product photography, a small office setup, and the real product transformation. The main weaknesses are that the bin association could briefly make the product feel like waste being put into a drink, and a complete real-time dissolve may lose momentum if the transformation is not fast and visually legible on a mobile screen. The concept also encourages trial verbally but does not yet make the first-use experience feel especially desirable beyond its novelty and convenience.

Refinement suggestions:
Preserve the bin-or-mug choice and uninterrupted product demonstration, but strengthen cues that this specific FoldBrew sleeve is intentionally made to become coffee. Keep the FoldBrew name and a concise preparation cue visible before the drop, and frame the bin clearly as the expected but incorrect destination rather than allowing it to dominate the shot. Test the dissolve at actual social-feed size and build the edit around the most visually active portion of the transformation while retaining enough continuity to prove that the intact sleeve becomes the drink. Make the finished coffee look premium and appealing so the payoff is not only reduced packaging and surprise, but also a desirable coffee moment. Consider replacing the reverse loop if it makes the process look digitally manipulated; a clean forward loop or tightly repeated drop-and-dissolve sequence may protect credibility. Give the final trial prompt greater visual priority and connect it directly to the office use case, while avoiding additional claims or mechanics not supplied in the brief.
