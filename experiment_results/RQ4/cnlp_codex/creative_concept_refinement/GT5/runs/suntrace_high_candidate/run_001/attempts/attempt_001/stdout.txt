=== Final Creative Concept ===
**Concept: “New Plan. Same Sun.”**

SunTrace turns the temporary sticker shape into a visual continuity mark that follows a young adult from one outdoor plan to the next. Activities, friends, and locations can change, but the revealed trace remains fixed in the frame, showing that sun exposure does not reset just because the plan does. That uninterrupted visual becomes the cue to reapply SunTrace before the day continues.

**Main Execution**

Each TikTok or Instagram Reel opens with a rapid series of match cuts. The same close-up of the temporary sticker shape remains in exactly the same position while the background changes between two familiar outdoor moments: campus becomes a park, lunch becomes a walk, or one group of friends becomes another.

On-screen text reads:

**“New plan. Same sun.”**

The video cuts briefly to the start of the day. Two distinct close-ups establish the product roles without additional explanation:

**“Protect”**  
The person applies transparent SunTrace SPF 50 to their face.

**“Visualize”**  
The person places the included demonstration sticker on their skin.

The first outdoor activity appears with a simple label: **“Plan 1.”** A single message or visual cue then introduces the next activity: **“Plan 2?”**

At the transition, the person removes the sticker. The temporary shape is revealed and held in frame as the location match-cuts from the first plan to the second. A concise limitation appears beside it:

**“Visual reminder only. Not an exact measure of UV damage.”**

Before joining the second activity, the person reapplies SunTrace. The product and application remain clearly visible as the final line appears:

**“Your plans reset. Sun exposure doesn’t.”**  
**“Reapply SunTrace SPF 50 when the day keeps going.”**

**Repeatable Format**

Every video follows one recognizable four-beat sequence:

**Protect and place. Plan 1. Same trace, new plan. Reapply.**

The locked close-up of the revealed shape is the campaign’s signature transition. Only the ordinary outdoor activities and locations change, allowing students and university communities to recreate the format while keeping it immediately recognizable.

Captions can pair familiar plan changes in a compact form:

**“Library lawn → outdoor dinner.”**  
**“Coffee walk → afternoon game.”**  
**“Campus event → park hangout.”**

Each execution shows only one transition, keeping the story clear and short.

**SunTrace’s Essential Role**

SunTrace SPF 50 provides the transparent facial sunscreen applied at the beginning and reapplied when the outdoor day continues. The included reusable UV-sensitive sticker creates the temporary visual trace that connects both plans in a single image.

The sticker is presented consistently as a visual demonstration of sun exposure, not as a precise test of UV damage or sunscreen performance. Its role is to make an otherwise invisible reason to reapply easier to see and remember.

**Audience and Production**

“New Plan. Same Sun.” connects reapplication to the spontaneous way young adults aged 18–30 move between outdoor activities. The fixed trace against changing backgrounds delivers the idea instantly: a new plan may feel like a fresh start, but it is still part of the same time outside.

The videos can be filmed vertically on smartphones with the real SunTrace sunscreen, included stickers, ordinary outdoor settings, simple text overlays, and match cuts. No celebrities, expensive locations, special effects, or additional demonstration tools are required, making the format practical for TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social media communities.

Evaluation reasons:
The locked sticker-shape match cut is a memorable, product-specific device rather than a generic sunscreen demonstration, and the line "New plan. Same sun." gives the format a clear organizing idea. Strategically, it connects reapplication to the audience's spontaneous movement between outdoor plans, keeps both SunTrace SPF 50 and the included sticker essential, and carefully avoids presenting the demonstration as an exact measure of UV damage. The contrast between changing plans and an unchanged trace creates an immediate visual hook suited to short-form social video. Execution is broadly practical for the stated budget and channels, using ordinary locations, smartphones, the real product, and simple edits. However, the opening match cuts, return to the start of the day, two product-role close-ups, two activity beats, sticker removal, limitation copy, reapplication, and final message may create too many narrative and text elements for one short video. Precisely locking the trace across changing backgrounds also requires consistent framing and editing, which may make community recreations less effortless than the concept suggests.

Refinement suggestions:
Simplify the sequence so it reads in one chronological pass: show application and sticker placement, Plan 1, the incoming Plan 2 cue, sticker removal and the locked match cut, then reapplication. Avoid opening with a montage that must later be explained. Reduce the number of overlays and give the revealed trace enough screen time to register; retain "New plan. Same sun." as the primary line and integrate the required limitation as concise, legible on-screen copy or a persistent caption. Create a simple framing rule for recreations, such as keeping the trace aligned to a fixed screen marker, so university contributors can reproduce the signature transition reliably. Keep the final reapplication visibly tied to SunTrace and avoid wording that could imply the sticker determines exactly when reapplication is required.
