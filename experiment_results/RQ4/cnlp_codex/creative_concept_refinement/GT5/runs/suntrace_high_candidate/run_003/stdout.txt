=== Final Creative Concept ===
**SunTrace: Leave a Trace**

**Core idea and creative mechanism**

SunTrace makes an invisible act visible through a simple “before and after” reveal. A reusable UV-sensitive sticker is placed on the skin before an outdoor activity, over sunscreen-protected skin. Later, the sticker is removed. The area beneath it remains unchanged, while the surrounding exposed area temporarily reveals the sticker’s shape.

The memorable mechanism is the **SunTrace reveal**: the shape of the sticker becomes a visual reminder of the protection that was applied, while the contrast around it shows why sunscreen should remain part of the activity, including reapplication.

**Principal execution**

Short vertical videos begin with a familiar outdoor moment: someone is about to leave for a university activity, meet friends outdoors, or spend time in the sun. They apply SunTrace SPF 50, place one of the included reusable stickers on their skin, and continue with the activity.

The video then cuts to the later reveal:

- The sticker is removed.
- The protected area remains unchanged.
- The surrounding exposed area temporarily reveals the sticker’s shape.
- The person looks at the result, then applies SunTrace again before continuing outdoors.

The video ends with a clear line such as:

**“You can’t always see sunscreen. SunTrace helps you see why it matters.”**

A final prompt connects the demonstration to memory:

**“Apply. Trace. Reapply.”**

The demonstration is presented as a visual indication of the difference between protected and exposed areas, never as a measurement of exact UV damage.

**Channel use**

On **TikTok**, the format is a fast, repeatable reveal: application, sticker placement, outdoor activity, removal, and the temporary shape appearing on the skin. The reveal is the payoff and should happen within the short video.

On **Instagram Reels**, the same structure can use a clean split between the “before” and “later” moments, with the sticker shape making the transition immediately understandable.

In **university social media communities**, the videos can be posted as practical outdoor reminders around activities where students may spend extended time outside. The same simple visual format makes the concept easy to understand without additional explanation.

**Essential role of SunTrace**

SunTrace is both the protection being used and the proof-of-concept that makes its normally transparent effect easier to visualize. The sunscreen provides SPF 50 protection, while the included UV-sensitive stickers create the visible contrast between the protected area and the surrounding exposed area.

The product remains central throughout the video: it is applied before the sticker is placed, shown again during reapplication, and linked directly to the reveal.

**Audience and objective**

Young adults already understand that sunscreen is important but may forget to apply or reapply it during outdoor activities. The reveal gives them a memorable physical cue: instead of relying only on a reminder or instruction, they see a temporary trace of the area that was protected.

By turning an invisible protective action into a short, shareable moment, the idea helps make sunscreen application more memorable and encourages users to include reapplication in their outdoor routine.

**Budget and constraints**

The concept uses only the real SunTrace sunscreen and the included reusable demonstration stickers. It can be filmed with a phone in ordinary outdoor or university settings, without celebrities, paid locations, complex production, or additional visual effects. Its meaning comes from the real sticker demonstration, and the short sequence remains understandable through the visible before-and-after contrast.

Evaluation reasons:
The concept has a strong, memorable product-specific mechanism: the reusable UV-sensitive sticker creates a visible trace from an otherwise transparent sunscreen application. This gives SunTrace a clear role in the central idea rather than using the product as a superficial brand appearance. The before-and-after reveal is well suited to short-form video, immediately understandable, and relevant to young adults who already know sunscreen matters but forget to apply or reapply it. The repeated structure can work across TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social media communities, and the phone-shot production approach fits the low to medium budget and stated restrictions. The main limitation is that the demonstration most directly shows the difference between one protected area and surrounding exposed skin; it does not by itself prove that the viewer reapplied sunscreen correctly or at the right time. The proposed line about seeing why sunscreen matters could also be interpreted as a claim about measured UV damage unless the visual and wording remain carefully qualified. The execution is practical, but it depends on clearly showing the sunscreen application, sticker placement, elapsed outdoor exposure, removal, and reveal within a very short runtime.

Refinement suggestions:
Make the reapplication behavior more concrete without adding unsupported claims: structure each video around a clear outdoor interruption or return moment, then show the reveal as the cue to apply SunTrace again before going back outside. Use concise on-screen wording such as "A visual demonstration, not a UV damage measurement" and avoid language implying that the sticker quantifies damage. Keep the product visible at the start and during reapplication, while making the sticker reveal the single payoff. Establish the sequence with simple time or activity cues so viewers understand that exposure occurred between application and removal. Develop a repeatable visual template with a consistent close-up of the protected area, sticker removal, reveal, and final "Apply. Trace. Reapply." prompt; this preserves the concept's strongest asset while improving behavioral clarity and reducing the risk of viewer confusion.
