==================== AGENT OUTPUT ====================

=== Final Creative Concept ===
**Concept: “Leave a SunTrace”**

SunTrace makes invisible sunscreen protection visible through a simple before-and-after reveal. The memorable creative mechanism is a UV-sensitive sticker that creates a temporary “trace” of sun exposure: after the sticker is placed on the skin and the sunscreen is applied according to the package directions, the user spends time outdoors, removes the sticker, and reveals the contrast between the protected area and the surrounding exposed skin.

**Principal Execution**

Create a repeatable 15-second vertical video format for TikTok and Instagram Reels:

1. **Before going outside:** A user holds up a SunTrace sticker and applies it according to the package demonstration. On-screen text: “Sunscreen on. Sticker on. Let’s see the trace.”
2. **Outdoor activity:** Short cuts show an everyday activity such as walking across campus, sitting outside, or meeting friends.
3. **The reveal:** The user removes the sticker. The sticker shape becomes temporarily visible around the area that was exposed, while the protected area remains unchanged.
4. **The reminder:** The user applies SunTrace again before continuing outdoors. End frame:  
   **“You can’t see sunscreen working. You can remember to reapply.”**  
   **“SunTrace SPF 50.”**

The visual reveal is the recurring hook: every video begins with an ordinary, invisible application and ends with a visible SunTrace. Different users can create their own sticker shapes and reveal moments, keeping the format recognizable while allowing varied content across university social media communities.

Each post should include a clear note that the sticker is a visual demonstration of sun exposure and does not measure exact UV damage.

**Brand and Product Role**

SunTrace is the mechanism that turns an invisible protective benefit into something users can understand immediately. Its transparent SPF 50 sunscreen remains central to the demonstration, while the included reusable UV-sensitive stickers provide the visual proof point. The product is not presented as preventing the visible trace entirely or as measuring damage; the contrast simply helps users understand that sun exposure is happening even when they cannot see it.

**Audience and Objective**

Young adults who already know sunscreen matters but forget to reapply are given a memorable physical cue: the SunTrace reveal. Seeing the temporary sticker shape appear after an outdoor activity connects an invisible process with a visible reminder to apply sunscreen again. The short, self-contained format is easy to understand without explanation and is suited to everyday campus and outdoor situations.

**Budget and Constraints**

The concept uses only the real SunTrace sunscreen and the included demonstration stickers. Videos can be filmed vertically in ordinary outdoor locations such as a campus walkway or outdoor seating area, using natural light and a phone camera. The format requires no celebrities, special locations, partnerships, complex effects, or additional product claims, making it practical for a low-to-medium budget.

Evaluation reasons:
The concept has a strong, memorable, and product-specific visual mechanism: the included UV-sensitive sticker makes an otherwise invisible sunscreen and sun-exposure story visible in a short before-and-after reveal. This gives the product a central role and directly addresses the objective of helping young adults understand protection and remember to reapply. The everyday outdoor situations are relevant to the target audience, and the reapplication beat provides a clear behavioral prompt rather than ending with a product demonstration alone. The repeatable 15-second structure is well suited to TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social media communities, and it respects the low-to-medium budget and production constraints. The main risks are that the order and method of applying the sunscreen and sticker may be unclear in a fast edit, and viewers could interpret the visual trace as an exact measurement of UV damage despite the disclaimer. The idea is highly effective, though its core reveal format may become familiar across repeated executions and its impact depends on the contrast being immediately legible on camera.

Refinement suggestions:
Make the demonstration steps visually unambiguous: show the sticker placement, sunscreen application to the surrounding exposed skin, removal, and reapplication in distinct, quick shots. Keep the disclaimer integrated into the reveal or caption in concise language such as "A visual demonstration of sun exposure, not a UV-damage measurement." Make the behavioral connection stronger by showing the reapplication immediately after the reveal and before the user resumes the activity, with a simple recurring line such as "See the trace. Reapply." Use a consistent close-up reveal and lighting approach so the temporary sticker shape reads clearly on mobile screens. For variation, use only the sticker shapes actually included with the product rather than implying that users can create or choose additional shapes unless the brief confirms that capability. Preserve the recognizable before-and-after format, ordinary campus settings, and product-centered proof point.
