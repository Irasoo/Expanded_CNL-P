=== Final Creative Concept ===
**Concept: “The Sticker Timestamp”**

SunTrace turns the moment a short plan becomes unexpected extra time outdoors into a visible reminder to reapply. The included UV-sensitive sticker acts as a “timestamp” for the decision to stay out: it marks the point when a quick activity quietly becomes longer than intended.

Each 10–15 second video opens on the temporary sticker-shaped contrast left after exposure. The sticker shape then becomes the visual transition into a rapid rewind:

- **Present:** The user looks at the visible shape and asks, **“When did this stop being a quick plan?”**
- **Rewind:** A familiar moment appears: a study session moves outside to the quad, a quick walk becomes an afternoon with friends, or a short break continues longer than expected.
- **Timestamp moment:** As the plan changes, the user applies transparent SunTrace SPF 50 and places the included sticker on their skin. The sticker shape briefly appears as a visual marker in the edit, identifying the moment when more outdoor time begins.
- **Return to present:** After exposure, the sticker is removed and the surrounding exposed area temporarily reveals its shape, while the protected area remains unchanged. The on-screen line reads: **“A reminder of time outside, not a measurement of UV damage.”**
- **Behavioral payoff:** Before continuing outdoors, the user clearly reapplies SunTrace. The video closes with: **“Plans change. Reapply.”**

The sticker is not a decorative prop or a claim of exact UV measurement. It is SunTrace’s recurring visual timestamp: a product-specific cue that makes an easily forgotten transition visible. By connecting the reveal to the moment a routine plan extends, the idea speaks directly to young adults who know sunscreen matters but lose track of reapplication during changing outdoor plans.

The same repeatable structure works across TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social media communities, using real sunscreen, the included reusable stickers, ordinary everyday settings, and simple editing. Every execution includes the readable caption: **“Temporary visual demonstration only. Does not measure exact UV damage.”**

Evaluation reasons:
The concept is distinctive because it turns the included sticker into a recurring, product-specific storytelling device rather than using it only for a standard before-and-after demonstration. Connecting the sticker shape to the moment a quick plan extends is relevant to young adults who forget to reapply during changing outdoor activities. SunTrace SPF 50, transparent application, the sticker demonstration, and reapplication all have clear roles, while the disclaimer respects the restriction against presenting the result as an exact measure of UV damage. The rewind structure, familiar university scenarios, and concise closing line are well suited to short-form social channels. Audience impact is slightly weaker because the meaning of 'timestamp' may not be immediately clear: the sticker records a visible contrast after exposure but does not indicate when the plan changed or how much time passed. Opening on the unexplained shape and moving rapidly through a rewind, disclaimer, reapplication, and closing message could also overload a 10-15 second execution. Production is achievable with real products, ordinary settings, and simple editing, but consistently capturing a clear temporary reveal, maintaining visual continuity across the rewind, and keeping the required disclaimer readable introduce some execution risk.

Refinement suggestions:
Preserve the changing-plans insight, product-specific sticker reveal, and 'Plans change. Reapply.' payoff, but simplify the story so the timestamp metaphor is understood without explanation. Establish the original short plan with one immediate cue, show one unmistakable extension moment, and use the sticker-shape transition at that exact point. Limit each video to a single scenario and reduce dialogue so the reveal, SunTrace application, reapplication, and disclaimer remain legible within the short runtime. Avoid any clock, countdown, or elapsed-time graphic that could imply the sticker measures exposure duration or exact UV damage. Make the product demonstration mechanically precise: clearly distinguish the transparent sunscreen application from the sticker-protected area and describe the resulting contrast only as a temporary visualization of sun exposure. Test the first seconds without sound to ensure viewers can identify the sticker shape, understand that the plan lasted longer than expected, and see reapplication as the intended action.
