==================== AGENT OUTPUT ====================

=== Final Creative Concept ===
**Concept: “Follow the SunTrace”**

Each short video opens with the most distinctive product moment: a young adult removes a SunTrace demonstration sticker, temporarily revealing its shape against the surrounding exposed skin. A concise hook appears immediately: **“Exposure leaves a trace.”**

The video snaps backward to show how the moment began. Before an everyday outdoor activity, the creator visibly applies transparent SunTrace SPF 50 across their face, then places the included reusable UV-sensitive sticker on their skin. Tight close-ups make the two roles unmistakable: **“SunTrace protects. The sticker makes exposure visible.”**

Quick cuts follow the creator through a familiar outdoor activity. At a natural pause, such as taking a break or changing activities, the recurring sticker shape appears as a visual cue in the edit. The creator takes out SunTrace and reapplies before continuing. The sticker is never presented as detecting when reapplication is required; its recognizable shape prompts the behavior this audience often forgets.

The video returns to the opening reveal and completes the three-beat sequence:

**APPLY. REAPPLY. REVEAL.**  
**“See the trace. Remember the layer.”**

A clean end frame shows SunTrace SPF 50 beside the reusable sticker with the clarification: **“SunTrace provides SPF 50 protection. The sticker visually demonstrates sun exposure; it does not measure exact UV damage.”**

For TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social media communities, every participant follows the same short, repeatable structure: prominent SunTrace application, reapplication during a natural activity break, and the final sticker reveal. The recurring shape creates immediate recognition across videos, while the rewind makes the invisible protection easier to understand: the sticker supplies the visible demonstration, and SunTrace is the protective layer users are prompted to apply and remember to reapply.

Evaluation reasons:
The concept uses the included UV-sensitive sticker as a product-specific visual proof device, making an otherwise transparent sunscreen benefit easy to show in a short-form format. The reveal-first structure and reverse sequence add a clear storytelling device, though sticker-reveal content and reminder messaging are familiar social-video mechanics rather than highly unexpected creative territory. Strategically, it closely answers the brief: SunTrace SPF 50 is visibly applied and reapplied, while the sticker is correctly framed as a demonstration of exposure rather than an indicator of when reapplication is needed or a measure of exact UV damage. The three-beat sequence is understandable for young adults and gives the audience a practical behavior cue during outdoor activity breaks. The opening reveal is likely to earn attention, but the emotional payoff may remain somewhat instructional unless the execution makes the activity context and reveal visually compelling. It is practical for TikTok, Reels, and university communities because it relies only on the product, included stickers, everyday outdoor settings, and a repeatable creator format.

Refinement suggestions:
Preserve the reveal-first structure, explicit SPF 50 role, reapplication moment, and required clarification. Strengthen audience impact by making the recurring sticker shape function as a sharper visual interruption at the activity break, so the reapplication cue feels immediate rather than merely appearing in the edit. Create a small set of distinct, everyday outdoor activity scenarios relevant to the stated audience, while retaining the identical APPLY. REAPPLY. REVEAL. structure, to prevent repeated videos from feeling interchangeable. Tighten the opening hook so the visible reveal, product role, and action are comprehensible before the rewind begins; this will reduce reliance on the end frame for meaning while keeping the sticker's demonstration claim appropriately limited.
