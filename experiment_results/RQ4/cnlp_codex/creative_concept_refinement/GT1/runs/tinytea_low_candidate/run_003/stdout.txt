==================== AGENT OUTPUT ====================

=== Final Creative Concept ===
**TinyTea: The Launch That Must Fit**

TinyTea turns the impossible scale of its launch brief into a product-centered feasibility concept. A cold TinyTea bottle sits on a student desk beside a laptop, textbooks, and a backpack. In a short, professionally defined pre-production film, the mandatory launch elements are tested against the bottle one by one: an in-person globally famous celebrity, simultaneous public events in five international cities, custom interactive billboards, a dedicated AR application, cinematic production, and delivery within seven days. Each appears as a labeled physical object or storyboard card that visibly cannot fit inside the bottle. The closing line is: “TinyTea fits student life. This launch does not fit the brief.”

The film uses recognizable university moments to establish the intended campaign direction: opening TinyTea between classes, taking a cold bottle from a shared refrigerator, and passing it across a study table. These scenes keep the lightly sweetened iced tea meaningful to local students and provide a clear social hook for Instagram, TikTok, and university community accounts.

This is explicitly a pre-production feasibility film, not a substitute for the mandated campaign. During the seven-day window, the team will also produce the campaign storyboard, five-city event plan, billboard interaction specification, AR application scope, celebrity appearance requirement, production schedule, and itemized resource request. The full awareness campaign proceeds only when confirmed funding, celebrity participation, international venues, billboard access, AR development capacity, travel, and professional production resources are supplied. Under the current sub-$1,000 budget, no compliant audience-facing execution can truthfully be promised.

=== Evaluation ===
Originality: 7/10
Strategic fit: 3/10
Audience impact: 5/10
Executability: 2/10
Overall score: 4.4/10
Weak criteria: executability
Evaluation reasons: The bottle-based idea gives the concept a clear, memorable device and makes the scale mismatch product-specific rather than merely complaining about the brief. The student desk, between-class, refrigerator, and study-table moments connect TinyTea to university life and preserve the cold, lightly sweetened tea benefit. However, the concept primarily communicates that the mandated campaign cannot be delivered instead of increasing awareness through that campaign. It does not provide the required celebrity appearance, five-city live events, interactive billboards, AR application, professional cinematic production, or compliant seven-day execution. The feasibility film may be understandable to industry or university-community audiences, but its self-referential message is less likely to create strong consumer interest in a new bottled tea, and the required global-scale elements remain unresolved.
Refinement suggestions: Keep the strong bottle-as-scale-device and student-life scenes, but make the bottle the organizing idea for every mandatory element rather than presenting those elements as rejected storyboard objects. Define a tightly scoped awareness execution in which the celebrity, five-city events, billboards, and AR experience all use the same simple TinyTea interaction or visual challenge, with the cold bottle and lightly sweetened taste central to participation. Clarify how the concept can be adapted to Instagram, TikTok, and university communities while preserving the required live and digital components. Because the stated budget, seven-day schedule, and mandatory production requirements conflict, explicitly separate the creative idea from a required feasibility or funding escalation; otherwise the concept should be repositioned as a brief-challenge film rather than a compliant launch campaign.
