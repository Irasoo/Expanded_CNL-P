Brand:
SunTrace

Product:
A facial sunscreen that is transparent when applied normally. The package includes reusable UV-sensitive stickers. When a sticker is placed on the skin before sun exposure and removed later, the protected area remains unchanged while the surrounding exposed area temporarily reveals the sticker shape.

Target audience:
Young adults aged 18-30 who understand that sunscreen is important but frequently forget to reapply it during outdoor activities.

Objective:
Make the invisible protective effect of sunscreen visually understandable and motivate users to remember sunscreen application and reapplication.

Key product benefit:
The sunscreen provides SPF 50 protection, while the included UV-sensitive demonstration stickers make the effect of sun exposure easier to visualize.

Channels:
TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social media communities.

Budget:
Low to medium.

Constraints:

==================== AGENT OUTPUT ====================

=== Final Creative Concept ===
**SunTrace: Before You Head Back Out**

Each 10- to 15-second vertical video begins with the most compelling moment: a close-up of a SunTrace demonstration sticker being removed to reveal its temporary shape against the surrounding exposed area. On-screen text immediately clarifies: “A visual demonstration, not a timer or UV damage measurement.”

The shot widens to a familiar break in one outdoor plan: friends leaving lunch to return to the quad, a student heading back outside after studying, or someone restarting an afternoon at the park. The young adult picks up SunTrace and reapplies according to the product directions before going back out. The video ends with the concise routine cue: “Heading back out? Reapply as directed.”

A quick opening flashback shows how the reveal was created: before the activity, the person applied SunTrace SPF 50 normally and placed one included reusable UV-sensitive sticker on their skin. The sticker makes otherwise invisible sun exposure easier to visualize, while SunTrace provides the SPF 50 protection. The reveal never determines when to reapply; the return outdoors is the behavioral cue.

Every video follows the same repeatable sequence: reveal, return-outside moment, reapply, then a brief apply-and-place flashback. One activity and one cue keep the message clear and memorable within a short video. The closing product shot reads: “SunTrace SPF 50 + reusable demonstration stickers.”

Designed for TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social media communities, the series uses real young adults, ordinary campus or neighborhood locations, natural close-up lighting, and only the actual SunTrace sunscreen and included stickers. Captions and pinned comments carry the fuller clarification: the sticker creates a temporary visual demonstration of exposure and does not measure exact UV damage or indicate when sunscreen protection has expired.

Weak criteria: originality
Evaluation reasons: The concept is clearly differentiated by making SunTrace's otherwise invisible benefit visible through the included stickers, and the reveal-first structure gives it a strong product-specific hook, although demonstration reveals and routine-reminder videos are recognizable social formats rather than exceptionally novel territory. Strategic fit is strong: it directly addresses forgetful 18-30-year-olds, keeps SPF 50 and the stickers central, uses returning outdoors as a practical reapplication cue, and explicitly avoids presenting the sticker as a timer or exact UV-damage measurement. The immediate reveal, familiar campus situations, and concise 'Heading back out?' cue should be quickly understood and remembered in short-form feeds. Execution is practical and repeatable with real users, ordinary locations, and only the supplied product and stickers. The main weaknesses are that the flashback after the reapplication moment may make the chronology harder to follow within 10-15 seconds, and the prominent opening disclaimer could compete with the visual hook or be difficult to read at feed speed. Producing a consistently clear reveal may also require careful framing, exposure timing, and continuity, though this remains compatible with the stated budget.
Refinement suggestions: Preserve the reveal-first hook and return-outdoors cue, but simplify the timeline so viewers cannot confuse the sticker reveal with an instruction to reapply. Use a very brief, visually distinct 'Earlier' insert immediately after the reveal, then move cleanly to the present-time return outdoors and reapplication. Shorten the opening clarification to a legible phrase such as 'Visual demo only' while retaining the full limitation in the end frame, captions, and pinned comment. Establish one repeatable close-up setup and test sticker visibility, lighting, text size, and pacing before producing variants. Keep each video to one location and one return-outside moment, and make the SunTrace pack and SPF 50 designation clearly visible during application and on the final frame.
