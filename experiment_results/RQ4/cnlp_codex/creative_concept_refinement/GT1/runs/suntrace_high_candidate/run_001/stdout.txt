==================== USER INPUT 1/1 ====================
Brand:
SunTrace

Product:
A facial sunscreen that is transparent when applied normally. The package includes reusable UV-sensitive stickers. When a sticker is placed on the skin before sun exposure and removed later, the protected area remains unchanged while the surrounding exposed area temporarily reveals the sticker shape.

Target audience:
Young adults aged 18-30 who understand that sunscreen is important but frequently forget to reapply it during outdoor activities.

Objective:
Make the invisible protective effect of sunscreen visually understandable and motivate users to remember sunscreen application and reapplication.

Key product benefit:
The sunscreen provides SPF 50 protection, while the included UV-sensitive demonstration stickers make the effect of sun exposure easier to visualize.

Channels:
TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social media communities.

Budget:
Low to medium.

Constraints:
Use only the real sunscreen and included demonstration stickers. Do not claim that the temporary visual demonstration measures exact UV damage. Do not use celebrities or expensive locations. The idea must be understandable within a short video.

==================== AGENT OUTPUT ====================

=== Final Creative Concept ===
**SunTrace: “The Plan Changed”**

A short TikTok or Reel opens with a young adult saying, “I’m staying inside today,” while applying transparent SunTrace SPF 50 and placing one included UV-sensitive sticker on their arm. A message arrives: “Meet outside?” They go.

The pattern repeats through the day. “Quick coffee?” becomes an outdoor table. “Come watch this?” becomes the campus lawn. Each unexpected change of plan cuts to the same visual cue: the phone notification appears, the person glances at the sticker, and the words **“The plan changed. Check your sunscreen.”**

At the end of the day, they remove the sticker. The temporary sticker-shaped contrast appears against the surrounding skin, making invisible sun exposure suddenly visible. The person looks at the next outdoor plan, applies SunTrace again, and places the reusable sticker back on their arm.

The reveal is captioned: **“This shows that exposure happened. It does not measure exact UV damage or SPF performance.”** The closing line is: **“Plans change. Make reapplying part of the plan.”**

SunTrace is central to the behavior: its transparent SPF 50 formula is applied before outdoor time, while the included reusable sticker creates a visual reminder that exposure can be easy to forget. The recurring “plan changed” moment turns spontaneous university invitations into a recognizable reapplication cue for 18-30-year-olds. Shot vertically on a phone across ordinary campus locations, the format is inexpensive, easy to understand within seconds, and simple for university communities to recreate with their own unexpected “meet outside?” moments.

Weak criteria: originality, strategic_fit, audience_impact, executability
Evaluation reasons: The concept gives SunTrace a strong, product-specific demonstration rather than treating sunscreen as a generic outdoor-care message. The repeated unexpected-plan structure is relatable for university-age audiences and creates a clear behavioral cue: a spontaneous invitation should trigger a sunscreen check and possible reapplication. The sticker reveal makes an invisible benefit visible and provides a memorable end payoff. The concept also follows the brief closely: it uses only the sunscreen and included stickers, works in short vertical video, avoids expensive talent and locations, and explicitly states that the demonstration does not measure exact UV damage or SPF performance. The main limitations are that the underlying insight of remembering sunscreen when plans change is familiar, and the sticker may be interpreted as a real-time indicator or proof of protection if the disclaimer is not prominent and well-timed. The repeated sequence could also become predictable if every video uses the same campus-invitation pattern.
Refinement suggestions: Make the sticker an unmistakable memory device rather than implying that it diagnoses exposure: show the person using it as a visible prompt to check whether sunscreen was applied or needs reapplication, while reserving the sticker-shaped reveal for the final demonstration. Bring the SPF 50 and transparent-formula benefits into the action or on-screen copy without suggesting that the sticker measures performance. Strengthen originality by varying the unexpected plan moments across creator executions, such as a quick errand becoming a long walk or an indoor event moving outside, while keeping the same recognizable cue. Tighten the short-video structure so the notification, sunscreen check, and final reveal are immediately legible, and have the closing behavior show reapplication before the next outdoor plan rather than relying mainly on the end caption.
