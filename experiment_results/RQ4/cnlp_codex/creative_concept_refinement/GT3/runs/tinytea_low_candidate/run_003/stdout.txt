=== Final Creative Concept ===
## TinyTea: The Launch That Fits

**Core idea:** TinyTea refuses to let an impossible spectacle become more important than the drink. The launch opens with the cold bottle and a direct truth: **“TinyTea is ready. This campaign isn’t.”** The brand publicly postpones promotion until the launch brief is made executable, turning its small-company honesty into the central statement without pretending that mandatory elements have been delivered.

**Main execution:** Within seven days, TinyTea creates one simple launch-hold asset for Instagram, TikTok, and local university communities. A phone-shot vertical video begins on a clearly branded TinyTea bottle being taken from a refrigerator, opened, and served cold. On-screen text then states:

**“Lightly sweetened. Served cold. Made locally.”**

The video cuts to the mandatory production list: an in-person globally famous celebrity, live events in five major international cities, custom interactive digital billboards in each city, a dedicated AR mobile application, and professional cinematic production. The final screen reads:

**“Required in seven days for under $1,000. TinyTea won’t fake it.”**

The post caption explains that the launch is on hold until the mandatory scope, budget, and timeline are formally reconciled. No celebrity involvement, international event, billboard, application, or professional production is represented as secured or completed. TinyTea does not solicit speculative student content or add another participation mechanic; the bottle, benefit, and honest decision remain the entire message.

**Product and brand role:** TinyTea appears first, remains visually central, and provides the payoff. Its recognizable bottle, lightly sweetened taste proposition, cold serving cue, and local origin are presented before the impossible requirements, ensuring the product is remembered more clearly than the production dispute. The refusal to manufacture a false launch reflects the reality of a small local company operating with limited resources.

**Audience and objective connection:** The concise, self-aware video is designed for the vertical social channels and university communities specified in the brief. Its immediate cold-bottle opening gives students a clear product cue, while **“TinyTea won’t fake it”** supplies a sharper and more memorable hook than asking them to resolve an internal budget decision. The asset can create initial awareness honestly for less than $1,000, but the full campaign cannot proceed or satisfy its objective as commissioned until the mutually incompatible mandatory requirements are formally changed or adequately funded.

=== Evaluation ===
Originality: 7/10
Strategic fit: 2/10
Audience impact: 5/10
Executability: 8/10
Weighted overall score: 5.30/10
Weak criteria: strategic_fit
Reasons: The concept has a differentiated, product-connected premise: it converts the impossible production mandate into an honest small-brand statement, keeps the cold bottle visible first, and makes the lightly sweetened/local cues clear. That gives it solid originality without relying on a generic launch trope. Strategic fit is very weak because the brief makes the celebrity, five international live events, custom billboards, AR app, and cinematic production mandatory; the concept explicitly declines to deliver all of them. It therefore cannot fulfill the commissioned campaign or reliably achieve first-month awareness at the intended scale. For local university students, the direct, short-form social treatment is understandable and may earn attention through candor, but a message focused on a production dispute risks feeling internal and less compelling than a product-led reason to try TinyTea. Execution is strong only for the proposed substitute asset: a phone-shot vertical video, branded bottle footage, and distribution through the named social/community channels are plausible within seven days and below $1,000. It is not executable as the full mandatory campaign, which is inherently incompatible with the stated constraints.
Refinement suggestions: Preserve the concise bottle-first execution and the honest local-company voice, but shift the main message from the unsatisfied brief to a student-relevant product moment, such as a cold, lightly sweetened option between classes or during study breaks. Make the final call to action concrete for awareness, for example identifying where students can find TinyTea locally, provided that distribution is actually available. The concept should clearly document the conflict between mandatory scope, budget, and timeline as a production escalation rather than position non-delivery as the campaign solution. No creative refinement can make the required celebrity, international events, custom billboards, dedicated AR app, and cinematic production achievable under the supplied seven-day, sub-$1,000 constraints; the brief must be formally revised or funded before a compliant campaign can be produced.
