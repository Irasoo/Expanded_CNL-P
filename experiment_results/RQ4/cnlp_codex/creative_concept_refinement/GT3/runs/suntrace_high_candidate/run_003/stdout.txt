=== Final Creative Concept ===
**Concept: “SunTrace: Mark Your Next Exit”**

SunTrace turns the ordinary moment of leaving campus into a visual “exit mark” that reminds young adults to reapply sunscreen whenever they head back outdoors.

Each short video opens with the most distinctive image: a close-up of a reusable UV-sensitive sticker being removed, revealing its temporary shape on the skin. The caption asks: **“First exit marked. What about your next one?”** The reveal is immediately framed as a visual demonstration, not a measurement of UV damage.

The video then rewinds to the start of the day. A student applies transparent SunTrace SPF 50 to the sticker-adjacent area, places the included sticker on the same clearly framed patch of skin, and leaves for a familiar campus activity. Every outdoor transition uses the sticker’s exact shape as the visual cut: leaving class, crossing campus, finishing exercise, or meeting friends. The repeated shape makes the product’s demonstration the video’s editing signature rather than a generic sunscreen before-and-after.

After the activity, the student removes the sticker in the matching close-up. The protected area remains unchanged while the surrounding exposed area temporarily reveals the sticker shape. The student then gets ready to leave again, applies SunTrace once more, and says or captions: **“Going out again? Mark it. SunTrace again.”**

The behavior is the central payoff: the sticker creates a memorable visual checkpoint, but the action it prompts is reapplication before the next outdoor transition. The trace is never presented as an exact reading of UV damage.

SunTrace is essential to the idea in two ways. Its transparent SPF 50 formula is shown as part of a normal, repeatable routine, while the included reusable sticker gives invisible protection and exposure a distinctive visual form. The sticker becomes a recognizable “mark” for the next exit and a consistent transition device across the series.

The format is built for low- to medium-budget TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social media. Each video follows the same short structure: trace-first hook, first application, one campus outing, matched sticker reveal, and visible reapplication before going out again. Students can vary the outdoor situation while keeping the same close-up, sticker placement, camera angle, and transition shape. Only SunTrace, the included sticker, a phone, and ordinary campus locations are required. The disclaimer remains concise and legible during the reveal: **“Visual demonstration only. Not a measurement of UV damage.”**

Weak criteria: originality, audience_impact, executability
Reasons: The concept gives the included UV-sensitive sticker a distinctive strategic role: it becomes both the visual reveal and the editing signature, rather than functioning as a one-off product demonstration. The repeated 'exit mark' idea is memorable and strongly connected to the product benefit. Strategic fit is especially strong because the concept targets a specific forgetful behavior, places reapplication before the next outdoor transition at the center of the story, and is designed for short-form campus content using ordinary locations and limited resources. It also respects the constraint against presenting the sticker as an exact UV-damage measurement by including a concise disclaimer. Audience impact should be strong because the sticker removal provides an immediate visual hook and the repeated question about the next exit creates a clear behavioral cue. However, the visual reveal could still be misread as showing actual skin damage, and the phrase 'mark' may require careful framing to avoid making the demonstration feel alarming or punitive. Executability is good because the structure is reusable and requires only the sunscreen, stickers, a phone, and campus settings. The main practical risks are maintaining a consistent close-up and sticker placement across transitions, making the temporary effect visible in variable lighting, and fitting the full sequence plus disclaimer into a very short video.
Refinement suggestions: Keep the trace-first opening, matched transition, and reapplication behavior, but make the demonstration framing unmistakable in the first reveal: pair the visual with concise on-screen language such as 'A visual demonstration of covered vs. exposed areas' and keep the existing disclaimer readable without slowing the video. Test alternate wording to ensure 'exit mark' feels like a useful reminder rather than evidence of damage. Build a simple repeatable shooting template with a locked camera distance, consistent body location, sticker placement, and lighting guidance so the reveal remains legible across student-created videos. Show the reapplication action immediately after the reveal and before the next outdoor scene, making the intended habit more prominent than the visual effect. Use a small set of campus scenarios with the same visual grammar, and confirm that each version can communicate the hook, demonstration status, and reapplication cue within the short-video format.
