=== Final Creative Concept ===
I’ll shape this as one repeatable short-form format: the sticker creates the visual payoff, while the reapplication moment becomes the behavioral prompt. I’ll keep the demonstration language strictly within the brief and specify the filming flow so the idea is easy to assess and produce.**Concept: “The Reminder You Can See”**

**Core idea**  
SunTrace turns an invisible sunscreen habit into a visible end-of-outdoor-activity check-in. The reusable UV-sensitive sticker creates a temporary visual reminder of the difference between a protected area and surrounding exposed skin, prompting the user to apply SunTrace again before returning outside.

**Principal execution: “SunTrace Check-In” vertical videos**

Each TikTok or Instagram Reel follows one short outdoor session:

1. A young adult says, “I never forget sunscreen. I forget to reapply.”
2. They apply SunTrace SPF 50 normally, showing that the facial sunscreen is transparent when applied.
3. They place one included reusable UV-sensitive sticker on the skin before going outdoors.
4. After the activity, they remove the sticker on camera. The protected area remains unchanged while the surrounding exposed area temporarily reveals the sticker shape.
5. The creator states: “This makes the difference visible. It does not measure exact UV damage.”
6. Before heading back outside, they reapply SunTrace and close with: “SunTrace check-in: apply again before the next round.”

The reveal should be a tight, uncut close-up, making the product demonstration understandable within seconds. The video does not describe the visual as a precise measurement of UV exposure or skin damage.

**Brand and product role**  
SunTrace is central to both parts of the story: its transparent SPF 50 formula provides the protection, while the included reusable sticker makes the normally invisible contrast between protected and exposed skin easier to visualize. The product is not merely shown in the video; its packaging feature creates the behavioral prompt.

**Audience connection**  
The format reflects a familiar young-adult situation: applying sunscreen before an outdoor activity but forgetting the second application. The check-in happens at a natural transition point, such as leaving a campus field, pool, sports area, or outdoor gathering, when reapplication is most relevant.

**Objective connection**  
The sticker reveal makes sunscreen’s protective effect visually understandable without claiming to measure exact UV damage. Ending with a reapplication immediately after the reveal links understanding to action.

**Channel and production approach**  
Produce a repeatable vertical format using one creator, a phone camera, natural outdoor light, the SunTrace sunscreen, and the included stickers. Versions can be posted to TikTok and Instagram Reels, then shared through university social media communities using a simple prompt such as: **“Show your SunTrace check-in.”** This keeps production low to medium cost without celebrities or expensive locations.

Reasons: The concept is distinctive because the reusable UV-sensitive sticker creates a clear, memorable visual payoff for an otherwise invisible sunscreen habit. It gives SunTrace a central and ownable role, connects the demonstration to the specific problem of forgetting to reapply, and uses a repeatable short-form format suited to TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social communities. The close-up reveal and transition-point reapplication prompt should be easy for the stated audience to understand and may encourage immediate behavior. Production is practical for a low-to-medium budget because it requires only a phone, one creator, the real product, the included stickers, and ordinary outdoor settings. The main weakness is interpretive: viewers could mistakenly conclude that the sticker reveal directly proves the sunscreen's protection or measures UV damage, especially because the script describes the sticker area as protected while also positioning SunTrace as the source of protection. The explanation that it is not an exact measurement helps, but the causal relationship still needs to be made more precise. The reveal may also depend on suitable exposure, skin visibility, and timing for the visual contrast to appear clearly.
Refinement suggestions: Make the demonstration mechanics explicit in the video and captions: explain that the sticker blocks UV on the covered area and creates a visual contrast with surrounding exposed skin, while SunTrace SPF 50 is the sunscreen habit being reinforced. Avoid wording that implies the sticker independently proves the exact performance of the sunscreen. Replace the potentially confusing sequence with a simple visual structure: show the sticker placement, show SunTrace application around the relevant area as directed, present the reveal, then state that the demonstration makes sun exposure easier to visualize rather than measuring damage or SPF performance. Use a consistent on-screen disclaimer such as "Visual demonstration only; not a UV-damage measurement" without letting it interrupt the reveal. Strengthen the reapplication behavior by making the check-in a recurring cue at natural activity transitions and ending with a direct, brief prompt such as "Back outside? Reapply SunTrace." Test a few close-up filming conditions and exposure durations so the sticker shape is reliably visible, and keep the visual reveal consistent across creator videos.
