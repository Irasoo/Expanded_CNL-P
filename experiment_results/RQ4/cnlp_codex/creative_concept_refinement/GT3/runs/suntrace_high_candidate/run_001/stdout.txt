=== Final Creative Concept ===
## Concept: “Trace the Gap”

### Core idea

The moment sunscreen is forgotten is usually not a decision. It is the gap between two outdoor plans: leaving the lawn for a walk, moving from lunch to a game, or staying outside longer than expected.

**“Trace the Gap”** turns SunTrace’s temporary sticker-shaped exposure contrast into a visual marker for that forgotten gap. Each short video opens with the reveal, then traces backward to the exact change of plan when sunscreen disappeared from the creator’s attention. The video returns to that transition and shows the creator reapplying before continuing outdoors.

The repeatable behavior is:

**“Plan changed? Trace it back. Reapply.”**

### Main execution

Each 15-second vertical video uses one creator, one outdoor setting, one real SunTrace bottle, and one included reusable UV-sensitive sticker.

1. **Open on the result:**  
   The creator removes the sticker in close-up. The temporary shape is visible against the surrounding exposed area. On-screen text reads:

   **“The gap I forgot sunscreen in.”**

2. **Trace backward:**  
   The edit quickly reverses to the one clear transition that caused the lapse. For example, the creator finishes sitting on a campus lawn, then decides to walk to an outdoor court. The product was applied before the first activity, but the new plan made reapplication easy to forget.

3. **Mark the transition:**  
   The video briefly freezes on the moment the plan changes. A simple on-screen line identifies it:

   **“Lawn ended. Outside continued.”**

   The sticker reveal is presented as a temporary visual demonstration of exposure contrast, not as a timer or measurement of exact UV damage.

4. **Return to the present:**  
   The edit snaps back to the creator removing the sticker and seeing the temporary shape. They take out SunTrace SPF 50 and reapply before starting the next outdoor activity.

5. **End frame:**  
   The real sunscreen and reusable sticker remain visible with the campaign line:

   **“Trace the gap. Reapply before the next plan.”**

   A high-contrast disclaimer stays on screen long enough to read:

   **“Temporary visual demonstration of exposure contrast. Does not measure exact UV damage.”**

### Product and brand role

SunTrace is the mechanism of the story, not just its sponsor. The transparent SPF 50 sunscreen represents protection that is normally invisible. The included UV-sensitive sticker creates the temporary shape that reveals the otherwise unseen contrast and gives the creator something concrete to trace back to.

The sticker does not determine when sunscreen must be reapplied. It provides a memorable visual cue for the behavioral problem in the brief: the creator applied sunscreen once, changed plans, and lost track of reapplication.

### Audience and objective connection

Young adults often move through several outdoor contexts in one day without formally deciding to extend their sun exposure. The concept focuses on that recognizable behavioral moment rather than presenting sunscreen as a general warning.

The hook is immediate because the audience sees a real, unusual result before receiving the explanation. The “gap” gives the result a sharper meaning: it shows where attention moved from one plan to the next. Reapplication is then attached to a practical memory cue:

**when the plan changes, bring SunTrace into the new plan.**

### Channel and production fit

The format is designed for TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social channels. Each execution can be filmed in one accessible outdoor location with one creator and a single activity transition. It needs only the real SunTrace sunscreen, one included sticker, natural light, and simple cuts or reverse playback.

The production should capture the sticker result in advance under conditions where the actual temporary contrast is clearly visible. Footage should be selected only when the result reads naturally on the creator’s skin, without filters, color grading, or exaggerated lighting. The same three-beat structure can be repeated across campus routines:

**result, forgotten plan change, reapply before continuing.**

This makes the concept distinctive through the sticker’s role as a visual record of a forgotten transition while keeping production simple, credible, and repeatable.

Weak criteria: originality, executability
Reasons: The concept gives the included UV-sensitive sticker a meaningful role beyond a generic product demonstration: the visible reveal becomes a narrative device for identifying a forgotten transition in an outdoor day. This is differentiated and product-connected, though reverse-telling a lapse from a reveal is a familiar short-video structure rather than an exceptionally novel one. It fits the brief strongly because it targets the stated behavioral problem, makes normally invisible SPF 50 protection visually understandable, keeps SunTrace central, and is built for fast vertical social video. The plan-change cue is practical and relevant to young adults' campus and outdoor routines, but it should not imply that a change of plans alone determines when reapplication is needed. The opening reveal is an immediate, visually unusual hook, and the short phrase 'Plan changed? Trace it back. Reapply.' is likely memorable and action-oriented. Execution is feasible at a low-to-medium budget using a creator, accessible outdoor settings, real product, and simple editing. Its main practical risk is that the temporary contrast must read clearly and credibly on camera within a short video; relying on naturally visible results may limit usable footage and require careful pre-testing across skin tones and exposure conditions.
Refinement suggestions: Keep the reveal-first structure and plan-change mnemonic, but make the reapplication message more precise: position a changed plan as a prompt to check and reapply before additional outdoor time, rather than as the sole rule for reapplication. Tighten the first seconds so the visual reveal, product/sticker identification, and the phrase 'the gap I forgot' are understandable without needing the reverse sequence to carry all of the explanation. Build several repeatable campus scenarios around distinct transition moments, such as leaving lunch for a walk or moving from studying outside to a game, while retaining the same three-beat format. Pre-test unfiltered close-up footage to confirm that the temporary contrast is legible, and use clear framing and lighting rather than exaggerated visuals. Preserve the exact-damage disclaimer, but place it where it remains readable without crowding the product reveal or end frame.
