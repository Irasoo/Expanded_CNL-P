=== Final Creative Concept ===
**Concept: “The Pack Goes in the Mug”**

**Core idea**  
Interrupt an office worker’s automatic “use it, bin it” reflex with FoldBrew’s distinctive behavior: this coffee package is not opened or thrown away. The whole branded sleeve goes directly into hot water and becomes the coffee.

The repeatable campaign gesture is a hand starting to move the FoldBrew sleeve toward a desk bin, then changing direction and dropping it into a mug instead.

**Main execution**  
Create a series of 6–10 second vertical videos built around recognizable office-pressure moments: arriving late, leaving one meeting for another, or finding the communal coffee area busy.

Each video opens in the first second with a clearly branded FoldBrew sleeve held above a desk bin beside a mug of hot water. The hand begins the familiar throwing-away motion, then stops.

On-screen text: **“Not in the bin.”**

The hand redirects the entire sleeve into the mug. A tight macro shot shows it dissolving completely and the water becoming coffee. The final shot widens just enough to reveal the finished drink and an uncluttered preparation area: no machine, opened sachet, pod, or discarded pack. The worker takes the mug and moves straight into their next task.

The action is edited as a smooth visual loop: the empty hand moving away from the mug transitions back to the opening hand holding a new FoldBrew sleeve. This encourages repeat viewing while keeping the dissolve as the campaign’s unmistakable centerpiece.

End frame:

**“Drop the whole thing in.”**  
**The pack becomes your coffee.**  
**Try FoldBrew.**

**Product and brand role**  
FoldBrew creates both the interruption and the payoff. Its branded sleeve is visible before the drop, during the dissolve, and as the reason nothing is left beside the finished coffee. The concept demonstrates the product rather than relying on a general lower-waste message: the viewer sees that the pack itself becomes one serving, with no machine, pod, or disposable sachet required.

**Audience connection**  
The near-bin gesture is based on a habit urban office workers already recognize, making FoldBrew’s difference immediately surprising and easy to remember. Busy office moments connect the product to situations where this audience wants coffee without joining a queue or dealing with shared equipment. The redirect from bin to mug turns an unusual preparation instruction into one simple, repeatable behavior.

**Objective and channel connection**  
The opening fake-out provides a fast scrolling hook, while the complete dissolve supplies a clear visual payoff and practical reason to try the product. The concise instruction, **“Drop the whole thing in,”** tells first-time viewers exactly what to do. The same bin-to-mug gesture can be repeated across Instagram, TikTok, and other short-form video placements using ordinary desks, mugs, hot water, close-up product photography, and a small set of office scenarios.

Reasons: The concept gives FoldBrew a distinctive, memorable action: redirecting the branded sleeve from the bin into the mug. The near-bin fake-out is familiar as a visual device, but the product-connected payoff makes it feel fresh and clearly demonstrates the unusual dissolve behavior. Strategic fit is especially strong because the concept directly addresses first-time trial, shows exactly how to use the product, targets recognizable office-pressure moments, and is designed for Instagram, TikTok, and short-form video. The product remains central before, during, and after preparation rather than being added as an end-frame explanation. Audience impact should be high because the opening creates immediate curiosity and the visible transformation provides a satisfying payoff, while the simple instruction is easy to remember. However, the bin gesture could initially create confusion or make the product seem waste-related before the reveal. Execution is practical for medium-budget vertical video using ordinary office settings, product photography, close-ups, and repeatable scenarios. The dissolve must be filmed clearly and consistently enough that viewers understand the sleeve fully becomes the drink, and the loop format should not compromise that explanation.
Refinement suggestions: Preserve the bin-to-mug interruption and the macro dissolve, but make the product behavior unmistakable earlier by briefly showing the full branded sleeve and a clean hot-water setup before the fake-out. Test alternate opening text such as "Wait. The whole pack." if "Not in the bin" causes confusion or overemphasizes disposal. Vary the office situations and the worker reactions so the campaign does not become mechanically repetitive, while keeping the same redirect gesture as the mnemonic. Give the dissolve enough screen time and use a clear before-and-after composition so viewers can verify that no wrapper or sachet remains beside the finished coffee. Keep the final instruction concise, but consider pairing "Drop the whole thing in" with a visually prominent confirmation that the sleeve dissolves into one serving, strengthening trial confidence without introducing unsupported environmental claims.
