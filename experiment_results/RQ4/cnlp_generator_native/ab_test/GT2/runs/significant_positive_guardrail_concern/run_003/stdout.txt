**Checkout Button Guardrail Test**

**Hypothesis:** Changing the checkout button text from “Continue” to “Buy Now” will increase purchase conversion rate.

**Experiment summary**
- Duration: 14 days
- Traffic allocation: 50% Control / 50% Variant
- Control: 1,000 conversions from 10,000 users, a 10.0% conversion rate
- Variant: 1,200 conversions from 10,000 users, a 12.0% conversion rate
- Absolute difference: +2.0 percentage points
- Relative lift: +20.0%
- Two-sided p-value: 0.00000619
- 95% confidence interval for the absolute difference: +1.13 to +2.87 percentage points

**Statistical assessment:** The Variant produced a statistically significant increase in purchase conversion because the p-value is below 0.05 and the confidence interval excludes zero. The result is marked underpowered against the specified 1 percentage-point minimum detectable effect at 80% power. This is a diagnostic limitation, but it does not override the observed statistical significance.

**Experiment validity:** No sample ratio mismatch was detected. The observed equal sample sizes are consistent with the planned 50/50 allocation.

**Guardrail assessment:** There is an explicit guardrail concern. Customer support contact rate increased from 2% to 5%, and the product team considers this a meaningful negative effect.

**Recommendation: Investigate; do not ship broadly yet.** Although “Buy Now” significantly improved purchase conversion, the reported support-contact increase is an important negative side effect that prevents an unqualified rollout.

**Next steps:** Investigate the reasons for increased support contacts and determine whether the button text creates confusion or changes customer expectations. Validate the support-contact measurement and assess the affected contact reasons. If the underlying issue can be addressed, test a revised variant while continuing to monitor both purchase conversion and the support guardrail.
