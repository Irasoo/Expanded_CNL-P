**Checkout Button Guardrail Test**

**Hypothesis:** Changing the checkout button text from “Continue” to “Buy Now” will increase purchase conversion rate.

**Experiment summary**
- Duration: 14 days
- Traffic allocation: 50% Control / 50% Variant
- Control: 1,000 conversions from 10,000 users, a 10.0% conversion rate
- Variant: 1,200 conversions from 10,000 users, a 12.0% conversion rate
- Absolute difference: +2.0 percentage points
- Relative lift: +20.0%
- Two-sided p-value: 6.19 × 10⁻⁶
- 95% confidence interval for the absolute difference: +1.13 to +2.87 percentage points
- Statistical significance: Yes, at the 0.05 threshold

**Validity and power:** No sample ratio mismatch was detected, so the observed allocation is consistent with the intended 50/50 split. The test is classified as underpowered for the specified 1-percentage-point minimum detectable effect at 80% power. This is a diagnostic limitation, but it does not override the statistically significant observed result.

**Guardrail assessment:** The customer support contact rate increased from 2% to 5%. The product team explicitly considers this a meaningful negative effect, so the guardrail is a confirmed concern.

**Recommendation: Investigate.** The variant produced a statistically significant improvement in purchase conversion, but it should not be shipped broadly while the material support-contact increase remains unexplained. The primary metric result is positive, but the guardrail prevents a straightforward ship decision.

**Next steps:** Investigate why “Buy Now” increased support contacts, validate the support-contact measurement and affected user segments, and determine whether the negative effect can be mitigated without losing the conversion gain.
