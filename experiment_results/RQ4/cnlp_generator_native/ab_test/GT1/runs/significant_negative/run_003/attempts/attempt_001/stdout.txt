**Checkout Negative Test**

**Hypothesis:** The new checkout button will increase purchase conversion rate.

Over 14 days, Control recorded 1,000 purchases from 10,000 users, a **10.0% conversion rate**. Variant recorded 800 purchases from 10,000 users, an **8.0% conversion rate**. The Variant decreased conversion by **2.0 percentage points**, a **20.0% relative decline**.

The result is **statistically significant**: two-sided **p = 7.75 × 10⁻⁷**, with a 95% confidence interval for the absolute difference of **-2.79 to -1.21 percentage points**. The interval is entirely below zero, providing strong evidence that the new button reduces purchase conversion.

The experiment is marked **underpowered** against the 1-percentage-point minimum detectable effect at 80% power. This is a diagnostic limitation, but it does not override the observed statistically significant result. There is **no sample ratio mismatch**: the equal sample sizes align with the planned 50/50 allocation. No additional meaningful guardrail degradation was reported.

**Recommendation: Do not ship the new checkout button.** Retain the Control experience. Investigate why the new button reduced conversion, including its presentation and checkout interaction, then develop a revised variant and test it in a new, adequately sized experiment.
