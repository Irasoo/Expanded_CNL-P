==================== USER INPUT 1/1 ====================
Brand:
SunTrace

Product:
A facial sunscreen that is transparent when applied normally. The package includes reusable UV-sensitive stickers. When a sticker is placed on the skin before sun exposure and removed later, the protected area remains unchanged while the surrounding exposed area temporarily reveals the sticker shape.

Target audience:
Young adults aged 18-30 who understand that sunscreen is important but frequently forget to reapply it during outdoor activities.

Objective:
Make the invisible protective effect of sunscreen visually understandable and motivate users to remember sunscreen application and reapplication.

Key product benefit:
The sunscreen provides SPF 50 protection, while the included UV-sensitive demonstration stickers make the effect of sun exposure easier to visualize.

Channels:
TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social media communities.

Budget:
Low to medium.

Constraints:
Use only the real sunscreen and included demonstration stickers. Do not claim that the temporary visual demonstration measures exact UV damage. Do not use celebrities or expensive locations. The idea must be understandable within a short video.

==================== AGENT OUTPUT ====================
FINAL CREATIVE CONCEPT
======================
**Concept: “Invisible Until You See It”**

A short-form video turns SunTrace’s invisible SPF 50 protection into a simple visual reminder that viewers can understand at a glance. The video opens on a young adult applying the transparent facial sunscreen, with on-screen text: “Sunscreen protection can be hard to see.” They place an included UV-sensitive demonstration sticker on their skin according to the product instructions before heading outdoors.

After sun exposure, the sticker is removed to reveal the temporary visible shape: the protected area remains unchanged while the surrounding exposed area makes the shape apparent. The reveal is shown in a tight close-up with the line: “Invisible protection. A visible reminder.”

The person immediately reapplies SunTrace, connecting the demonstration to the audience’s common habit of forgetting. The closing message reads: “See the reminder. Remember to reapply.” The product and reusable stickers appear together in the final shot with the SunTrace name and “SPF 50.”

Designed for TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social communities, the execution uses quick cuts, natural outdoor light, the real sunscreen, and its included stickers. A small on-screen clarification states: “Temporary visual demonstration only. Does not measure exact UV damage.”

Evaluation reasons:
The concept gives SunTrace a strong, product-specific visual idea: the included UV-sensitive sticker makes normally invisible sunscreen protection understandable in a short-form reveal. The message is closely connected to the objective and audience, especially because the reveal leads directly to reapplication rather than stopping at product demonstration. The line "Invisible protection. A visible reminder." is clear, memorable, and easy to adapt across TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social channels. The concept uses only the real sunscreen and included stickers, avoids unsupported claims about measuring UV damage, and can be produced with natural light, close-ups, and a simple outdoor setting at low to medium cost. Its main limitation is that the visual effect may be interpreted as a precise measurement of protection or skin damage despite the clarification, and the basic demonstration format could become predictable if presented without a strong opening or relatable reapplication situation.

Refinement suggestions:
Keep the visual reveal and direct reapplication message, but make the forgetting behavior more specific and relatable through a short setup such as leaving class for an outdoor activity, finishing a run, or spending the afternoon outside. State the product role precisely at the moment of reveal: the unchanged area demonstrates where sunscreen was applied, while the surrounding sticker response shows exposure, not an exact measure of UV damage or SPF performance. Make the reapplication prompt more behaviorally useful by showing the person carrying or placing the compact sunscreen somewhere visible and applying it after the activity. Test several opening hooks and reveal timings so the concept earns attention before the explanation, while keeping the final frame simple and consistently branded with SunTrace, SPF 50, and the reusable stickers.
