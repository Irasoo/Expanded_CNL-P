==================== USER INPUT 1/1 ====================
Brand:
SunTrace

Product:
A facial sunscreen that is transparent when applied normally. The package includes reusable UV-sensitive stickers. When a sticker is placed on the skin before sun exposure and removed later, the protected area remains unchanged while the surrounding exposed area temporarily reveals the sticker shape.

Target audience:
Young adults aged 18-30 who understand that sunscreen is important but frequently forget to reapply it during outdoor activities.

Objective:
Make the invisible protective effect of sunscreen visually understandable and motivate users to remember sunscreen application and reapplication.

Key product benefit:
The sunscreen provides SPF 50 protection, while the included UV-sensitive demonstration stickers make the effect of sun exposure easier to visualize.

Channels:
TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social media communities.

Budget:
Low to medium.

Constraints:
Use only the real sunscreen and included demonstration stickers. Do not claim that the temporary visual demonstration measures exact UV damage. Do not use celebrities or expensive locations. The idea must be understandable within a short video.

==================== AGENT OUTPUT ====================
FINAL CREATIVE CONCEPT
======================
**Concept: “The Sticker Reminder”**

A short vertical video opens with a close-up of a young adult peeling a SunTrace UV-sensitive sticker from a clearly labeled forearm **“demo area.”** The temporary sticker-shaped contrast is visible against the surrounding exposed area. On-screen copy reads: **“The sun leaves reminders.”**

The video cuts back to the beginning of the same outdoor moment. Before leaving for a campus activity, the person applies SunTrace SPF 50 to their face as directed. They then place one included reusable sticker on the small, labeled demonstration area on their forearm. The video makes the separation clear: the sticker is being used only as a visual demonstration, while SunTrace is applied to the face for protection.

The person spends time outdoors with friends in one ordinary location. A single close-up of the sticker becomes the reminder: they notice it, take SunTrace from their bag, and reapply the facial sunscreen as directed. The action is shown clearly and without interrupting the story with additional messages. On-screen copy reads: **“See the cue. Reapply as directed.”**

The video ends with the person removing the sticker, echoing the opening reveal. The SunTrace sunscreen and reusable stickers appear together in the final shot. The closing line is: **“Protection goes on clear. Keep the reminder with you.”** A concise call to action reads: **“Apply. Keep it with you. Reapply as directed.”**

The reveal is presented as a temporary visual demonstration only, with a legible on-screen disclaimer: **“Does not measure exact UV damage.”** The video does not suggest intentionally leaving skin unprotected; the labeled forearm is a controlled demo area, while the product’s protective role is shown through facial application and reapplication.

The execution uses one person, one familiar outdoor setting, one small demo area, one reusable sticker, and the real SunTrace product. Its simple sequence, **place the sticker, apply SunTrace, notice the cue, reapply, reveal**, can be filmed as a repeatable 15- to 20-second format for TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social channels. Different everyday campus activities can change between versions, while the sticker close-up remains the consistent SunTrace reminder.

Evaluation reasons:
The sticker-shaped reveal is distinctive, visually tied to the included product feature, and more memorable than generic sunscreen education, although the reverse-timeline reveal and reminder-device structure are familiar short-form techniques. The concept clearly features SunTrace SPF 50, demonstrates its transparent application, targets forgetful young adults in a credible campus setting, and includes the required limitation that the demonstration does not measure exact UV damage. However, the sticker does not visibly indicate when reapplication is needed, so the moment when the wearer notices it and reapplies may feel narratively convenient rather than behaviorally persuasive. There is also some tension between showing surrounding exposed skin to produce the reveal and stating that the video does not suggest leaving skin unprotected. The opening contrast, simple sequence, and repeated sticker motif should be easy to understand and reasonably attention-grabbing, but multiple labels, instructions, and a disclaimer within 15 to 20 seconds could reduce immediacy. Execution is strong: it uses only the real product and sticker, one person, an ordinary location, and a repeatable vertical-video structure suited to all specified channels and the low-to-medium budget.

Refinement suggestions:
Make the sticker a clearly established memory cue rather than implying that it detects reapplication timing: for example, show the wearer associating noticing or touching the sticker with checking the product directions and reapplying when due. Clarify exactly how the controlled demo area is prepared and keep it very small so the reveal does not appear to encourage deliberate unprotected exposure. Simplify the on-screen copy to one opening hook, one reapplication instruction, and the required disclaimer, giving each enough screen time to remain legible. Preserve the reverse reveal, ordinary campus setting, visible facial application, and final product-and-sticker shot, since these are the concept's strongest branded and executable elements.
