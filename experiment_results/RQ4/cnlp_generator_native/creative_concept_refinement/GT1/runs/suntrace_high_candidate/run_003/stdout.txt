==================== USER INPUT 1/1 ====================
Brand:
SunTrace

Product:
A facial sunscreen that is transparent when applied normally. The package includes reusable UV-sensitive stickers. When a sticker is placed on the skin before sun exposure and removed later, the protected area remains unchanged while the surrounding exposed area temporarily reveals the sticker shape.

Target audience:
Young adults aged 18-30 who understand that sunscreen is important but frequently forget to reapply it during outdoor activities.

Objective:
Make the invisible protective effect of sunscreen visually understandable and motivate users to remember sunscreen application and reapplication.

Key product benefit:
The sunscreen provides SPF 50 protection, while the included UV-sensitive demonstration stickers make the effect of sun exposure easier to visualize.

Channels:
TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social media communities.

Budget:
Low to medium.

Constraints:
Use only the real sunscreen and included demonstration stickers. Do not claim that the temporary visual demonstration measures exact UV damage. Do not use celebrities or expensive locations. The idea must be understandable within a short video.

==================== AGENT OUTPUT ====================
FINAL CREATIVE CONCEPT
======================
**Concept: “SunTrace Replay”**

A short-form video series uses SunTrace’s sticker reveal as a visual “replay button,” turning a forgettable sunscreen moment into a memorable reapplication cue.

Each 10–15 second video opens with the most arresting image: a young adult removes an included UV-sensitive sticker after an everyday outdoor activity, revealing its temporary shape. The shape triggers a rapid reverse replay of the moments when sunscreen slipped their mind: joining a campus game, settling into a park, or spending time outside with friends. The replay lands on SunTrace SPF 50, and the action switches forward as they apply or reapply according to the product directions.

On-screen text connects the product demonstration to the habit: **“You can’t replay sun time. You can remember SunTrace.”** The video closes with the sunscreen and reusable stickers beside: **“Apply. Reapply. Follow label directions.”**

The sticker is explicitly framed as a visual demonstration of sun exposure, not a timer or test. A clear mobile-safe closing line states: **“Visual demonstration only. Does not measure UV damage or tell you when to reapply.”** Users are never shown waiting for a reveal before reapplying.

The reverse-to-forward edit becomes the campaign’s recognizable device across TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social communities. Different student activities, sticker shapes, and missed moments keep the series varied, while the same distinctive sequence remains easy to repeat using real SunTrace products, accessible locations, and simple phone editing. SunTrace stays central as the transparent SPF 50 protection users need to remember, while its included stickers make an otherwise invisible reason to build that habit visible.

Evaluation reasons:
The concept turns SunTrace's distinctive sticker reveal into a memorable replay device, giving a familiar reverse-edit format a strong, product-specific role. It directly addresses forgetful reapplication among young adults by connecting recognizable outdoor moments to a clear corrective action, while keeping the SPF 50 sunscreen and demonstration stickers central. Opening on the reveal provides an immediate visual hook suited to short-form feeds, and the repeated reverse-to-forward structure should make the message easy to understand and recognize across episodes. Execution is practical for a low-to-medium budget because it uses real products, everyday campus or park settings, young adult participants, and simple phone editing. It also respects the important constraints by avoiding unsupported measurement claims, stating that the sticker is only a visual demonstration, showing reapplication according to label directions, and not implying that users should wait for a reveal. The main limitation is that reverse replay is an established social-video technique, so distinction depends heavily on consistently staging the sticker reveal clearly. The amount of required closing text may also be difficult to read within 10-15 seconds and could weaken the final behavioral cue.

Refinement suggestions:
Preserve the opening reveal and reverse-to-forward campaign structure, but standardize a highly legible visual transition in which the revealed sticker shape becomes the replay symbol or edit trigger without implying that it measures exposure. Simplify the narrative in each video to one missed reapplication moment so the product, corrective action, and disclaimer all have enough screen time. Use large mobile-safe text, keep the primary habit cue separate from the compliance line, and hold the final product and disclaimer frame long enough to read. Develop a small set of repeatable campus scenarios tied to natural reapplication occasions, while ensuring every episode shows application or reapplication based on label directions rather than on the sticker result. Test whether viewers understand both that the sticker demonstrates exposure visually and that it is not a timer or UV-damage test; revise wording or sequencing if either point is unclear.
