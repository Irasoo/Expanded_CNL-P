==================== USER INPUT 1/1 ====================
Brand:
FoldBrew

Product:
A premium instant coffee concentrate packaged inside an edible, water-soluble coffee cup sleeve. When the sleeve is placed in hot water, it dissolves and becomes one serving of coffee.

Target audience:
Urban office workers aged 22-35 who regularly buy takeaway coffee but want a faster and lower-waste option at work.

Objective:
Drive first-time trial of FoldBrew and make the product's unusual "dissolve the packaging to make the coffee" behavior memorable.

Key product benefit:
The package itself becomes the coffee, so the user does not need a coffee machine, coffee pod, or disposable coffee sachet.

Channels:
Instagram, TikTok, and short-form digital video.

Budget:
Medium.

Constraints:
Use ordinary product photography and short-form video production. Do not use celebrities, large public installations, or fictional environmental claims. The central idea must visibly demonstrate the package dissolving into the drink.

==================== AGENT OUTPUT ====================
FINAL CREATIVE CONCEPT
======================
**Concept: “Don’t Open It. Brew It.”**

FoldBrew turns the instinct to open coffee packaging into a product-specific visual surprise: the sleeve is not something to tear and throw away. It is the coffee.

Each short-form video opens within the first two seconds on an office worker holding a clearly branded FoldBrew sleeve over a cup. Just as they begin to tear it open, on-screen copy interrupts:

**“Don’t open it.”**

They notice the queue at the office coffee machine, a meeting countdown, or a colleague preparing to make a coffee run. Instead of joining in, they drop the whole FoldBrew sleeve into hot water. A tight, uninterrupted close-up shows the folded sleeve dissolving and the water becoming coffee. Nothing is removed or discarded.

The payoff appears over the transformation:

**“Brew the whole thing.”**  
**“The sleeve is the coffee.”**

The worker takes the finished drink back to their desk while the queue, countdown, or coffee run continues in the background. This keeps the familiar office pressure from the original concept but makes the unexpected product behavior, rather than a generic time-saving claim, the central hook.

Every execution repeats the same recognizable sequence: the interrupted tear, the whole sleeve entering hot water, the visible dissolve, and the finished coffee. Different office situations provide variety without adding production complexity. Macro product shots and ordinary desks, cups, kettles, meeting screens, and coffee-machine areas keep the format practical for Instagram, TikTok, and short-form digital video.

The final frame shows the FoldBrew pack beside the finished drink with one clear instruction:

**“FoldBrew. Don’t open it. Drop it in hot water.”**

By asking office workers to do the opposite of what packaging normally demands, the campaign makes first trial feel simple, surprising, and worth watching. FoldBrew remains the proof at the center: no machine, pod, or disposable sachet is needed because the package itself becomes the coffee.

CREATIVE EVALUATION
===================
Originality: 8.0
Strategic fit: 9.0
Audience impact: 8.0
Executability: 9.0
Weighted overall score: 8.5
Weak criteria: originality, audience_impact

Evaluation reasons:
The concept has a strong, product-specific reversal: viewers expect the coffee sleeve to be opened or discarded, but the instruction is to place the entire sleeve in hot water. That gives the unusual product behavior a clear visual hook and earns the 8 for originality, although the 'do the opposite of what you expect' structure is familiar in advertising. Strategic fit is especially strong because the concept directly serves first-time trial, targets recognizable office moments, and makes the package becoming the coffee the central proof rather than relying on a generic convenience or waste-reduction claim. The repeated interruption, visible dissolve, and finished drink should be immediately understandable and memorable to the target audience, supporting an 8 for audience impact. The concept is practical for the stated channels and medium budget: ordinary office settings, product photography, macro shots, and short-form video can support multiple executions without requiring celebrities, installations, or unsupported environmental claims. The main execution risk is that the transformation must be unmistakably visible and quick enough for short-form viewing, which keeps executability at 9 rather than 10.

Refinement suggestions:
Preserve the interrupted-tear opening and the uninterrupted dissolve demonstration, but develop a few sharper variations so the campaign does not become a repetitive coffee-machine queue scenario. Tie each situation more directly to the product behavior, such as showing the worker instinctively searching for a bin before realizing there is nothing to discard, while keeping the claim focused on what FoldBrew visibly does rather than making broader environmental promises. Make the first frame clearly show the branded sleeve, cup, and hot water, and use a consistent transformation sequence with a tight close-up, a clear before-and-after view, and enough time for viewers to understand that the sleeve has dissolved into the drink. Test concise wording for the final instruction so the action is impossible to misread: 'Drop the whole sleeve in hot water.' The finished pack and drink should remain together in the closing frame to reinforce trial, product recognition, and the central behavioral change.
