==================== AGENT OUTPUT ====================
FINAL CREATIVE CONCEPT
======================
**TinyTea: Open Cold, Wherever You Are**

During TinyTea’s first launch month, one simple action connects university students around the world: chill a bottle, open it, and take the first sip at the same moment as students in other university communities. The campaign turns TinyTea’s defining product benefit, a lightly sweetened iced tea served cold, into a recognizable social ritual rather than a generic beverage message.

A globally famous celebrity appears in person at the central launch event and performs the ritual on camera, inviting students to “open cold” with them. Live public events in five major international cities recreate the same moment with local university communities. Each event uses the same low-complexity visual system: a chilled TinyTea bottle, a synchronized countdown, and student reactions captured vertically for Instagram and TikTok. Custom interactive digital billboards in each city show the countdown and respond to the public opening their bottles. A dedicated augmented-reality mobile application lets participants point their phones at a TinyTea bottle to unlock the shared countdown, city locations, and a digital “cold opened” mark for social posts.

The campaign’s cinematic film and short-form edits focus on one repeated image: different students, in different cities, opening the same cold TinyTea together. Local university communities are the primary participation engine, with student groups and campus channels directing attention to the launch moment and encouraging students to post their own cold-opening videos throughout the month. TinyTea remains central because the bottle, the cold serving ritual, and the first sip are the entire mechanism that unites the campaign.

This concept is production-ready only when the brief provides confirmed access to the celebrity, five-city events, interactive billboards, AR development, professional production, and a realistic budget and schedule. Under the stated budget of less than $1,000 and seven-day deadline, the mandatory execution cannot be truthfully delivered.

CREATIVE EVALUATION
===================
Originality: 7.0
Strategic fit: 2.0
Audience impact: 6.0
Executability: 1.0
Weighted overall score: 4.1
Weak criteria: executability

Evaluation reasons:
The synchronized cold-opening ritual is clearly differentiated from generic iced-tea advertising and gives TinyTea a product-specific role through the bottle, chilled serving occasion, and first sip. It could create a memorable social behavior for university students and adapt well to short-form video. However, Strategic Fit is severely weakened because the concept depends on a globally famous celebrity, five international live events, interactive billboards, an AR application, cinematic production, and completion within seven days, all while the brief sets a total budget below $1,000. These mandatory requirements are not realistically compatible, so the concept cannot meet the launch-awareness objective as proposed. Audience impact is promising in theory, but the scale and complexity may distract from the simple product message and participation is uncertain without credible access and promotion. Executability is effectively infeasible under the explicit budget, schedule, and resource constraints.

Refinement suggestions:
Preserve the central cold-opening ritual and synchronized social behavior, but redesign the execution around the actual constraints. Use student creators, campus groups, and a single local launch moment or fully digital campus challenge, with phone-shot vertical content, simple countdown graphics, and a reusable branded posting template. Replace the celebrity appearance with a locally relevant student host or reveal that can be produced within the available resources, and replace international events, billboards, and a dedicated AR app with Instagram and TikTok effects or QR-linked mobile content. If the mandatory celebrity, five-city events, billboards, AR, cinematic production, budget, and seven-day deadline cannot change, the concept should be rejected as unworkable rather than presented as production-ready.
