==================== AGENT OUTPUT ====================
Final Creative Concept
======================
**SunTrace Checkpoint: “One More Thing”**

A repeatable short-form video format built around the moment young adults realize a quick outdoor plan has turned into a much longer one.

The video opens with a familiar promise: “We’ll only be outside for 20 minutes.” A young adult applies SunTrace SPF 50, places one of the included UV-sensitive stickers on their skin, and joins an ordinary activity such as studying outdoors, playing a casual game, or meeting friends for coffee. Each time someone says “one more,” the video cuts forward: one more round, one more conversation, one more stop. The original plan quietly becomes an extended afternoon.

At the first natural pause, an on-screen prompt appears: “Still out?” The person removes the sticker. Its temporary shape remains visible against the surrounding exposed area, creating the visual interruption that the sunscreen itself cannot. The video immediately connects the reveal to behavior with: “If your plans changed, your sunscreen routine should too.” The person reapplies SunTrace before returning to the activity.

The closing shot shows the transparent sunscreen and reusable sticker with the line: **“Plans change. Reapply.”** A small clarification reads: **“Visual demonstration only. Does not measure exact UV damage.”**

The sticker functions as a visual bookmark for the time spent outdoors, while SunTrace is the practical action at every “one more” moment. The format is distinctive, easy to repeat across relatable university and social settings, and uses only the real product, included stickers, everyday locations, quick cuts, and text overlays for TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social media communities.

Evaluation reasons: The concept has a strong, memorable product-specific device: the reusable UV-sensitive sticker makes an otherwise invisible sunscreen benefit visible without claiming to measure exact UV damage. The repeated "one more" structure is familiar and relatable for university-age audiences, but it is meaningfully connected to the real behavioral problem of unexpectedly extended outdoor plans. Strategic fit is strong because the idea links the sticker reveal directly to the need to reapply, keeps SPF 50 and the sunscreen central, and works within the stated channels, budget range, locations, and production constraints. The short-video format, quick progression of time, and immediate visual reveal should make the message easy to understand and likely to earn attention. Execution is especially practical: it requires only the product, included stickers, ordinary activities, simple edits, and text overlays. The main limitation is that the basic "plans changed, reapply" reminder is not completely novel, and the visual reveal may vary in clarity depending on exposure time, skin, lighting, and filming conditions. The concept also depends on viewers understanding that the sticker demonstration is illustrative rather than a precise indicator of protection or damage.
Refinement suggestions: Preserve the "one more" mechanic and product reveal, but give the format a sharper recurring signature so it feels more ownable than a general sunscreen reminder. For example, make each additional commitment visibly update a simple on-screen outdoor-time counter or transform the original "20 minutes" promise into a recognizable SunTrace checkpoint, while avoiding any suggestion that the sticker measures exact UV exposure. Make the reapplication moment unmistakable by showing the person pause, apply the sunscreen visibly and correctly, and replace or reposition the reusable sticker before continuing. Test several everyday scenarios and filming conditions to ensure the temporary shape reads clearly in a short video; use a close-up and consistent lighting rather than relying on the reveal alone. Keep the disclaimer concise but legible, and repeat the core line "Plans change. Reapply." at the moment of behavior change as well as in the end frame. These refinements would strengthen distinctiveness, comprehension, and reliability without adding expensive production requirements.
