==================== USER INPUT 1/1 ====================
Brand:
SunTrace

Product:
A facial sunscreen that is transparent when applied normally. The package includes reusable UV-sensitive stickers. When a sticker is placed on the skin before sun exposure and removed later, the protected area remains unchanged while the surrounding exposed area temporarily reveals the sticker shape.

Target audience:
Young adults aged 18-30 who understand that sunscreen is important but frequently forget to reapply it during outdoor activities.

Objective:
Make the invisible protective effect of sunscreen visually understandable and motivate users to remember sunscreen application and reapplication.

Key product benefit:
The sunscreen provides SPF 50 protection, while the included UV-sensitive demonstration stickers make the effect of sun exposure easier to visualize.

Channels:
TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social media communities.

Budget:
Low to medium.

Constraints:
Use only the real sunscreen and included demonstration stickers. Do not claim that the temporary visual demonstration measures exact UV damage. Do not use celebrities or expensive locations. The idea must be understandable within a short video.

==================== AGENT OUTPUT ====================
Final Creative Concept
======================
**Concept: “The Sun Doesn’t Pause. SunTrace Back On.”**

Each 10–15 second video opens with a student absorbed in a familiar campus activity while quick cuts mark the moments that make reapplication easy to forget: “One more set.” “One more chapter.” “One more song.” The activity continues, but the sunscreen bottle gradually slips out of attention.

A sharp rewind returns to the start of the outing. In one clear close-up, the student places an included demonstration sticker on clean skin, then applies SunTrace SPF 50 across the relevant exposed area as directed. Back in the activity, a natural transition becomes the memory trigger: halftime at pickup basketball, finishing a chapter on the quad, or the music changing at an outdoor event. The action freezes on-screen with the line: “You paused. The sun didn’t.” The student picks up SunTrace and reapplies as directed before continuing.

At the end, the sticker is removed to reveal its temporary shape, making otherwise invisible sun exposure visible without presenting it as a timer or measurement. The reveal lands with the repeatable cue: “See the sun. SunTrace back on.” The closing frame shows SunTrace and the sticker together with: “SPF 50. Apply as directed. Remember to reapply.”

Across TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social communities, students can recreate the same sequence using their own real “lost track of time” moments. Every version uses an ordinary outdoor setting, one activity-specific pause, a close-up reapplication, and the sticker reveal. A persistent caption states: “Visual demonstration only; not a timer or measure of exact UV damage.”

Evaluation reasons: The concept uses a familiar short-form video device, rewind, but gives it a clear SunTrace-specific role: showing how ordinary moments cause users to lose track of reapplication. The repeated activity cues such as “one more set” and “one more chapter” are highly relevant to young adults and make the behavioral problem recognizable. The product is central through the SPF 50 sunscreen, the reapplication moment, and the included UV-sensitive sticker reveal. The line “You paused. The sun didn’t.” provides a concise, memorable trigger, while the final sticker demonstration makes an otherwise invisible benefit easier to understand without explicitly claiming to measure UV damage. The format is practical for TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university communities, uses ordinary locations and real product components, and supports user recreations. The main limitations are that the rewind structure is not entirely new, the sequence may require careful visual explanation to make the sticker and sunscreen relationship immediately clear, and the sticker reveal could still be misinterpreted as an exact indicator of exposure despite the disclaimer.
Refinement suggestions: Preserve the rewind structure and behavioral cue, but make the demonstration mechanics unmistakable within the first few seconds: show the sticker being placed, sunscreen being applied as directed to the relevant skin area, and the sticker being removed in a visually consistent close-up. Use on-screen wording such as “visual demonstration only” at the reveal itself, not only in the caption, and avoid language that implies the sticker measures damage or functions as a timer. Strengthen the reapplication behavior by making the activity pause the recurring campaign device across every execution, with a simple repeatable cue such as a phone reminder, halftime, or a completed chapter. Keep the number of beats tightly limited so the video does not spend too long on the rewind before showing the product and the visible result.
