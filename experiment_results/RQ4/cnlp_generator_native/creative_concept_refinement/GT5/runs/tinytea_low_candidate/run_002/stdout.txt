==================== AGENT OUTPUT ====================
Final Creative Concept
======================
**TinyTea: The Cold Sip Check Relay**

TinyTea turns its defining serving benefit into a recognizable visual ritual: the **Cold Sip Check**. Every chilled bottle carries a visible condensation ring. After taking a sip, the celebrity places the bottle on a simple marked surface, lifts it, and reveals the ring. The message is: **“Cold enough. Sweet enough. TinyTea.”**

During the first launch month, the globally famous celebrity appears in person at one compact public Cold Sip Check event in each of five major international cities. Each event uses the same seven-second sequence: the celebrity opens a cold TinyTea, takes a sip, places it on the marked surface, reveals the condensation ring, and invites university students to make their own check. No stage, set, performances, or additional product claims are required. Students receive the same prompt: **“Show us your ring.”**

Custom interactive digital billboards in the five cities use one reusable interaction. When a passerby activates the display, a virtual TinyTea bottle appears, leaves a condensation ring, and triggers the celebrity saying, **“Cold enough. Sweet enough. TinyTea.”** Each billboard then displays a local university-community prompt and the relevant TinyTea availability message supplied by the company.

The dedicated TinyTea AR mobile application opens directly to one effect: a virtual chilled TinyTea bottle leaves a condensation ring wherever the user taps. Students record their own Cold Sip Check, post it to Instagram or TikTok, and tag their university community. The effect is built once and reused in every city, event, and social edit.

Professional cinematic production captures the same short sequence at all five events, creating one launch film and five vertical city edits. Each edit opens with the celebrity’s bottle ring, cuts to students repeating the gesture, and ends with the local university prompt. TinyTea remains central in every frame, while the ring gives students a simple, repeatable behavior tied specifically to the product being served cold.

The seven-day production uses one script, one shot list, one compact event layout, one billboard interaction, one AR effect, and one edit template. Day one locks the celebrity schedule, city permissions, billboard files, app build, and university-community distribution. Days two and three produce the shared bottle-ring mark, billboard interaction, and single AR effect. Days four through six capture the identical event sequence in the five cities using the same minimal setup. Day seven delivers the master film, city edits, and social assets.

All required celebrity, event, billboard, application, travel, and production resources must be secured as confirmed in-kind support before day one; TinyTea’s sub-$1,000 cash budget is reserved for the reusable bottle-ring materials, local coordination, and final digital delivery. Without those confirmed resources, the campaign does not proceed.

Creative Evaluation
===================
Originality: 7.0
Strategic fit: 4.0
Audience impact: 7.0
Executability: 2.0
Weighted overall score: 5.1
Weak criteria: executability
Evaluation reasons: The Cold Sip Check is a clear, product-linked ritual: the condensation ring makes the cold-serving benefit visible and gives students a simple action to repeat in short-form social content. This is more distinctive than a generic iced-tea launch, although condensation imagery and challenge-style participation are familiar devices, limiting originality below exceptional territory. The concept supports launch awareness on Instagram, TikTok, and university communities, and TinyTea remains visible throughout the proposed behavior. However, strategic fit is materially weakened by the mismatch between a small local beverage company's likely launch context and the mandatory international scope; the concept does not explain how five major international-city events translate into awareness or availability for the stated local university audience. Audience impact is solid because the seven-second action is easy to understand, visually demonstrable, and shareable, but the line 'Show us your ring' may be ambiguous or invite unintended interpretations without stronger visual framing. Executability is very low: securing a globally famous celebrity, five in-person international events, custom billboards, a dedicated AR application, cinematic production, travel, permissions, and seven-day delivery cannot credibly be treated as feasible under a sub-$1,000 total budget merely by stating that all resources must be in-kind. The mandatory constraints make the plan operationally dependent on multiple unverified, high-complexity commitments.
Refinement suggestions: Preserve the Cold Sip Check as the central asset, but make the audience connection and local availability explicit in every social asset, event prompt, billboard message, and AR posting flow. Replace the potentially unclear 'Show us your ring' wording with a clearly branded instruction such as 'Make the Cold Sip Check' while retaining the visible bottle-and-condensation demonstration. Build a detailed dependency plan for the mandatory elements: written confirmation deadlines for the celebrity, each venue and permit, billboard operators, travel, AR development, production crew, product supply, and university distribution; define a single accountable owner and delivery gate for each. Since the requirements prohibit removing elements, identify which confirmed in-kind partners are providing each major resource before claiming the seven-day schedule is viable. Use the one reusable visual sequence, event layout, interaction design, and edit template to limit complexity, but acknowledge that unconfirmed in-kind support leaves the campaign non-executable.
