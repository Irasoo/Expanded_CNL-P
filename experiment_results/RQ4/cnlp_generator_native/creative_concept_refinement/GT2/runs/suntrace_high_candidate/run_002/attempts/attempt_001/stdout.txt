==================== USER INPUT 1/1 ====================
Brand:
SunTrace

Product:
A facial sunscreen that is transparent when applied normally. The package includes reusable UV-sensitive stickers. When a sticker is placed on the skin before sun exposure and removed later, the protected area remains unchanged while the surrounding exposed area temporarily reveals the sticker shape.

Target audience:
Young adults aged 18-30 who understand that sunscreen is important but frequently forget to reapply it during outdoor activities.

Objective:
Make the invisible protective effect of sunscreen visually understandable and motivate users to remember sunscreen application and reapplication.

Key product benefit:
The sunscreen provides SPF 50 protection, while the included UV-sensitive demonstration stickers make the effect of sun exposure easier to visualize.

Channels:
TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social media communities.

Budget:
Low to medium.

Constraints:
Use only the real sunscreen and included demonstration stickers. Do not claim that the temporary visual demonstration measures exact UV damage. Do not use celebrities or expensive locations. The idea must be understandable within a short video.

==================== AGENT OUTPUT ====================
FINAL CREATIVE CONCEPT
**SunTrace: The Reminder That Shows Up Later**

Every TikTok and Reel follows one simple, repeatable sequence: **reveal, rewind, reapply.**

It opens with a one-second close-up of a student peeling off a SunTrace UV-sensitive demonstration sticker after a real outdoor campus activity. The temporary sticker-shaped contrast is the immediate hook. On-screen copy reads: **“This is your reminder.”** A small persistent line clarifies: **“Sticker demonstrates sun exposure only. It does not measure UV damage.”**

The video rewinds to the beginning of the activity. In a clear, standardized close-up, the student places the reusable sticker on a small area of skin, then applies SunTrace transparent SPF 50 facial sunscreen normally across their face and exposed skin, including around the sticker. The sticker is shown as a visual exposure demonstration, while SunTrace is shown as the protection step.

The rewind stops at the familiar forgetting point: midway through an outdoor study session, after a pickup game, while leaving a park hangout, or during a long walk between campus buildings. A time cue appears, such as **“Still outside?”** The student takes out SunTrace, reapplies, and returns to the moment without breaking the activity. The closing frame lands the behavior cue: **“When the day keeps going, reapply.”**

Each video uses the same camera sequence: sticker reveal close-up, rewind to initial application, one activity shot, one reapplication close-up, and the closing line. University communities can reproduce it with real students, ordinary daylight, one outdoor location, the SunTrace product, and its included sticker, varying only the activity and the moment reapplication is forgotten.

SunTrace makes invisible protection easier to remember by turning the sticker’s temporary trace into a visible prompt, while the product shot makes the desired action unmistakable: apply SPF 50 before heading out, and reapply when time outdoors continues.

Weak criteria: strategic_fit
Evaluation reasons: The reveal, rewind, reapply structure is distinctive, repeatable, and well suited to short-form video. The delayed visual reveal creates a clear hook and gives the audience a memorable reason to think about sunscreen after an outdoor activity. The concept also uses the product and included reusable stickers directly, includes SPF 50, supports ordinary student-created executions, and addresses the specific behavior of forgetting to reapply when plans continue. However, the central demonstration risks being misunderstood: the visible sticker-shaped contrast is primarily caused by the sticker blocking exposure, so it does not clearly prove or visualize the sunscreen's transparent protective effect. Applying sunscreen around the sticker may further blur what the sticker demonstrates and what SunTrace does. The persistent disclaimer is responsible and follows the constraint, but it may compete with the hook in a one-second opening. The reapplication cue is useful and strategically relevant, although the concept is closer to a strong reminder format than an exceptionally original brand idea.
Refinement suggestions: Make the product role unambiguous by framing the sticker as a reminder device and visual exposure demonstration, not as evidence that the sunscreen itself created the protected shape. Use a standardized split demonstration or two clearly labeled areas: one with normal SunTrace application and one without sunscreen, with the sticker used only to reveal exposure context where the product instructions and safety guidance support that setup. Avoid implying that the sticker measures protection or exact UV damage, and keep the disclaimer readable without delaying the reveal. Strengthen brand ownership by making the reapplication moment trigger a recurring SunTrace visual or line, such as the sticker reveal immediately prompting the product being taken from the user's bag. Retain the simple five-shot structure, but test a few highly recognizable forgetting moments and make the time cue more specific, such as a long afternoon outdoors or an activity continuing past the expected return time. Ensure every execution shows the product clearly at both initial application and reapplication so the behavior, rather than the sticker effect alone, remains the takeaway.
