==================== USER INPUT 1/1 ====================
Brand:
SunTrace

Product:
A facial sunscreen that is transparent when applied normally. The package includes reusable UV-sensitive stickers. When a sticker is placed on the skin before sun exposure and removed later, the protected area remains unchanged while the surrounding exposed area temporarily reveals the sticker shape.

Target audience:
Young adults aged 18-30 who understand that sunscreen is important but frequently forget to reapply it during outdoor activities.

Objective:
Make the invisible protective effect of sunscreen visually understandable and motivate users to remember sunscreen application and reapplication.

Key product benefit:
The sunscreen provides SPF 50 protection, while the included UV-sensitive demonstration stickers make the effect of sun exposure easier to visualize.

Channels:
TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social media communities.

Budget:
Low to medium.

Constraints:
Use only the real sunscreen and included demonstration stickers. Do not claim that the temporary visual demonstration measures exact UV damage. Do not use celebrities or expensive locations. The idea must be understandable within a short video.

==================== AGENT OUTPUT ====================
FINAL CREATIVE CONCEPT
**Concept: “New Plan. Reapply.”**

SunTrace turns an ordinary change of plans into a memorable sunscreen reminder. The reusable UV-sensitive sticker is not treated as a timer or measurement. Instead, its temporary outline becomes a visual reminder that time in the sun has passed and the day has moved on.

Each short video opens on the payoff: a creator removes a SunTrace sticker after being outdoors. The protected area remains unchanged while the surrounding exposed area temporarily reveals the sticker’s shape. On-screen text reads: **“New plan. Reapply.”**

The video then jumps back to the beginning of the outing. The creator applies transparent SunTrace SPF 50 and places one of the included stickers on their skin, following the package directions. The action unfolds in a familiar young-adult setting: leaving practice for lunch, moving from the library to the quad, finishing a picnic, or deciding to stay out after an afternoon walk.

At the activity change, the creator sees the recurring on-screen cue: **“New plan. Reapply.”** They take out SunTrace and reapply according to the product directions before continuing. A simple repeated sound or text transition marks the moment in every video, making the cue recognizable without requiring a particular sticker shape or extra production element.

The video ends with the sticker removed and its temporary outline visible, followed by the product and reusable stickers. The final copy reads: **“You can’t see sun exposure happening. Make the next plan your reminder.”** A clear disclaimer appears: **“Visual demonstration of exposure only. Does not measure UV damage or sunscreen performance.”**

SunTrace is essential because its transparent SPF 50 protection addresses the invisible risk, while its included reusable stickers make sun exposure visible in a quick, distinctive demonstration. The idea connects directly with young adults whose outdoor plans often extend or change unexpectedly, creating a practical behavioral trigger for remembering reapplication without claiming that the sticker determines an exact interval.

The format requires only the real SunTrace product, its included stickers, a creator, and ordinary campus or social locations. Each video follows the same simple structure: reveal, jump back, outdoor activity, change of plan, reapply, reveal. It is repeatable for TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social media communities while remaining clear, low-cost, and faithful to the product’s actual capabilities.

Evaluation reasons: The concept has a differentiated, product-specific demonstration: the reusable UV-sensitive sticker makes an otherwise invisible contrast between protected and exposed skin temporarily visible. The repeated "New plan. Reapply." cue gives the product a useful behavioral role and connects well with young adults whose outdoor activities change or extend unexpectedly. It directly supports the objective of making protection understandable while avoiding the prohibited claim that the sticker measures exact UV damage. The structure is well suited to short-form video and uses only the product, included stickers, a creator, and ordinary locations, so it is practical for the stated channels and budget. Originality is moderated because plan changes followed by a reminder to reapply are a familiar behavioral advertising device, and the core visual demonstration may be more distinctive than the campaign platform itself. Audience impact is likely clear and relevant, but the emotional hook is primarily functional; viewers may understand the message without finding it especially surprising or memorable. The opening reveal helps attention, although the temporary nature of the visual effect and the relationship between the sticker outline and reapplication could still require very clear editing and captions.
Refinement suggestions: Make the behavioral idea more ownable by showing increasingly recognizable real-life plan changes that young adults commonly experience, while keeping the same concise cue and sound. Establish the rule immediately: whenever the day changes from one outdoor plan to another, SunTrace becomes the reapplication trigger. Ensure the sticker reveal is demonstrated in a simple, tightly framed before-and-after shot, with wording that clearly distinguishes visualizing exposure from measuring damage or determining SPF duration. Consider shortening the repeated setup so the reveal and reminder arrive quickly, then use several variations tailored to campus communities, such as practice to lunch or library to outdoor study. Keep the product and included stickers visible at the reapplication moment and in the closing frame so the idea does not become merely a generic reminder campaign. The disclaimer should remain legible but concise, and the videos should consistently show reapplication according to the product directions rather than implying that a change of plans alone determines when reapplication is medically required.
