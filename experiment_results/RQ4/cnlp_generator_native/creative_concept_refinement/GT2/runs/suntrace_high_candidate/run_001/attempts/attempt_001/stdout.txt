==================== USER INPUT 1/1 ====================
Brand:
SunTrace

Product:
A facial sunscreen that is transparent when applied normally. The package includes reusable UV-sensitive stickers. When a sticker is placed on the skin before sun exposure and removed later, the protected area remains unchanged while the surrounding exposed area temporarily reveals the sticker shape.

Target audience:
Young adults aged 18-30 who understand that sunscreen is important but frequently forget to reapply it during outdoor activities.

Objective:
Make the invisible protective effect of sunscreen visually understandable and motivate users to remember sunscreen application and reapplication.

Key product benefit:
The sunscreen provides SPF 50 protection, while the included UV-sensitive demonstration stickers make the effect of sun exposure easier to visualize.

Channels:
TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social media communities.

Budget:
Low to medium.

Constraints:
Use only the real sunscreen and included demonstration stickers. Do not claim that the temporary visual demonstration measures exact UV damage. Do not use celebrities or expensive locations. The idea must be understandable within a short video.

==================== AGENT OUTPUT ====================
FINAL CREATIVE CONCEPT
**“Bookmark the Sun”**

The Reel opens on an immediate visual hook: a young adult places a SunTrace UV-sensitive sticker on their cheek, looks directly into their phone camera, and says, “This stays with me until my last plan today.” They apply SunTrace SPF 50, tap the sticker in the camera frame, and on-screen text establishes the repeatable ritual: “New stop. SunTrace check.”

A rapid sequence follows them through a real, changeable outdoor day: crossing campus, eating lunch outside, then meeting friends at a park. Every location begins with the exact same one-second shot: the person enters the phone-camera frame, taps the sticker, and reapplies SunTrace according to the product’s directions. Match cuts keep the sticker in the same position while the background and activity change, making it feel like a literal visual bookmark carried from one plan to the next.

At the final stop, the repeated frame returns one last time. Instead of another location change, the person removes the sticker, revealing its temporary shape against the surrounding exposed area. The quick pattern break makes the invisible effect of sun exposure immediately visible and delivers the payoff promised in the opening.

The video ends on a clean close-up of the revealed shape beside the SunTrace pack: “Your plans change. Keep your SunTrace check.” A secondary qualifier remains legible on screen: “Visual demonstration only; not a measure of UV damage.”

SunTrace SPF 50 is the protection used throughout, while its included reusable UV-sensitive sticker gives forgetful young adults a visible cue tied to the transitions already built into campus life and outdoor plans. The consistent tap, camera frame, and reapplication action create a recognizable behavior that students can easily recreate in their own TikToks, Reels, and university social posts using only the product, sticker, phone, and ordinary locations.

Weak criteria: executability
Evaluation reasons: The sticker as a literal visual bookmark across changing plans is memorable, product-specific, and more distinctive than a standard sunscreen reminder. Strategically, the repeated location transition turns an existing behavior in short-form video into a clear reapplication cue, keeps SunTrace SPF 50 central, addresses forgetful young adults, and includes the required limitation that the demonstration does not measure UV damage. The match-cut pattern and final sticker reveal provide an immediate hook, an understandable rhythm, and a satisfying visual payoff that should work well on TikTok, Reels, and university social channels. Execution is relatively affordable and uses only the product, sticker, phone, and ordinary locations, but maintaining convincing sticker placement, framing, continuity, and legible qualification text across an outdoor day creates some production risk. The concept also needs to show more precisely how sunscreen is applied around or in relation to the sticker so viewers do not mistake the revealed shape as proof that the sticker measures protection or exact UV damage.
Refinement suggestions: Preserve the bookmark structure, repeated tap, match cuts, and final reveal, but clarify the demonstration procedure in one concise visual close-up that follows the product and sticker directions exactly. Make the reapplication action unmistakable at each stop without slowing the edit, and avoid language implying that the sticker itself tracks UV exposure or confirms exact protection. Test whether the sticker can remain securely and consistently positioned through the depicted activities; if not, shorten the timeline or choose lower-motion stops while retaining the changing-plans idea. Keep the qualifier large and high-contrast enough to read on a phone, and pair the final pack shot with a direct reapplication prompt based on the product's directions rather than relying only on the broader phrase 'SunTrace check.'
