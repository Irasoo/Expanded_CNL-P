==================== AGENT OUTPUT ====================
FINAL CREATIVE CONCEPT
======================
**TinyTea: The Cold Pause, Everywhere**

TinyTea turns one ordinary student break into a worldwide relay. The campaign’s central action is always the same: open a chilled TinyTea, take the first cold sip, and hand the pause to someone else.

During the seven-day launch window, a globally famous celebrity appears in person at five short public “Cold Pause” events in five major international cities. Each event uses the same compact setup: a TinyTea-branded table, chilled bottles, a camera, and a single clear invitation for passers-by to take the pause and nominate the next person. The celebrity starts each city’s relay with the same close-up action, creating one recognizable piece of behavior rather than five different event formats.

In each city, a custom interactive digital billboard displays the celebrity taking the first sip. A simple bottle-linked interaction lets people trigger a city-specific response on the screen, such as the celebrity saying “Your turn” beside the participant’s city or campus. The billboard keeps the product visible and turns the international activity into a direct invitation to join.

The dedicated TinyTea AR application has one focused function: students point their phones at the TinyTea label to place the celebrity’s “cold pause” moment into their own surroundings. They record themselves taking the same first sip and passing the bottle or camera toward the next person. The experience is built from one reusable AR effect and one bottle-triggered action, so every city, billboard, and campus post uses the same visual language.

For local university students aged 18-25, the five cities are not the destination of the campaign; they are the opening signal. TinyTea invites students to recreate the relay in libraries, dorms, study spaces, cafés, and campus courtyards, posting where they take their cold pause on Instagram and TikTok. TinyTea reposts selected student videos throughout the first month, making local campus moments the continuing story after the launch events end.

All production follows one repeatable template: a cinematic close-up of the chilled bottle, the first cold sip, the handoff, and the line **“Take the pause. Pass it worldwide.”** The same compact event kit, billboard interaction, AR effect, shot list, and edit structure are used across all five cities and student content. TinyTea remains central because the ritual depends on its lightly sweetened bottled tea being served cold, while the simple handoff gives students an easy action to remember, recreate, and share.

EVALUATION
==========
Originality: 6.0
Strategic fit: 2.0
Audience impact: 6.0
Executability: 1.0
Weighted overall score: 3.8
Weak criteria: executability

Evaluation reasons:
The cold-sip handoff is a clear, repeatable behavior with some distinctiveness, and the idea connects the product's chilled format to a social pause that students can recreate. The worldwide relay and consistent visual template could generate recognizable content across Instagram and TikTok. However, the central execution is fundamentally incompatible with the supplied constraints: a very low budget below $1,000 cannot reasonably support a globally famous celebrity appearing in person, live events in five major international cities, interactive billboards in each city, a dedicated AR application, cinematic production, and completion within seven days. The strategic fit is therefore severely reduced because the concept accepts mandatory requirements that conflict with the budget and timeline instead of finding a workable way to address them. Audience impact is moderate: the handoff is easy to understand and potentially participatory, but the international celebrity and city spectacle may be less relevant to local university students than a campus-first idea, and the AR and billboard layers may distract from the simple product ritual.

Refinement suggestions:
Preserve the strongest asset: the single memorable action of taking a cold sip and passing the pause to someone else. First, make the concept explicitly campus-centered, using student relays in libraries, dorms, cafes, and courtyards as the primary story rather than treating them as an after-effect of an international campaign. Clarify how the mandatory celebrity, five-city events, billboards, AR application, cinematic production, seven-day schedule, and sub-$1,000 budget can coexist; as written, they cannot be delivered credibly, so the brief itself requires resolution before production. If the constraints are negotiable, replace the celebrity and international infrastructure with a local student creator, phone-shot content, one campus activation, and a lightweight social filter or label-triggered effect. If every constraint is genuinely mandatory, narrow the concept to a concise creative treatment and identify the required external funding, partners, production vendors, and schedule, while avoiding claims that the current budget and timeline can support it.
