==================== USER INPUT 1/1 ====================
Brand:
SunTrace

Product:
A facial sunscreen that is transparent when applied normally. The package includes reusable UV-sensitive stickers. When a sticker is placed on the skin before sun exposure and removed later, the protected area remains unchanged while the surrounding exposed area temporarily reveals the sticker shape.

Target audience:
Young adults aged 18-30 who understand that sunscreen is important but frequently forget to reapply it during outdoor activities.

Objective:
Make the invisible protective effect of sunscreen visually understandable and motivate users to remember sunscreen application and reapplication.

Key product benefit:
The sunscreen provides SPF 50 protection, while the included UV-sensitive demonstration stickers make the effect of sun exposure easier to visualize.

Channels:
TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social media communities.

Budget:
Low to medium.

Constraints:
Use only the real sunscreen and included demonstration stickers. Do not claim that the temporary visual demonstration measures exact UV damage. Do not use celebrities or expensive locations. The idea must be understandable within a short video.

==================== AGENT OUTPUT ====================
FINAL CREATIVE CONCEPT
======================
**SunTrace: “The Reminder You Can See”**

Each TikTok or Reel opens with a tight close-up of a SunTrace demonstration sticker being removed from a small, clearly identified test patch on the participant’s arm. The temporary sticker shape appears against the surrounding uncovered skin as the on-screen line reads: “My cue to reapply.”

The video immediately cuts back to the start of an ordinary outdoor activity: a campus walk, picnic, pickup game, or park study session. The participant applies transparent SunTrace SPF 50 normally to their face. Separately, they place the included UV-sensitive sticker on a small demonstration patch on their arm, leaving the surrounding test area uncovered. A concise label distinguishes the two actions: “SunTrace on face. Demo patch on arm.”

At a natural break in the activity, such as halftime, a study break, or stopping for water, the participant notices the sticker and reaches for SunTrace. The recurring cue appears: “Still outside? SunTrace again.” They visibly reapply the sunscreen to their face before continuing.

The sticker is removed at the end, returning to the opening reveal. The temporary contrast is presented only as a visualization of exposure on the uncovered demonstration patch, never as a test of the sunscreen or a measure of UV damage. The final frame shows the SunTrace SPF 50 package and reusable stickers with the behavior cue: “See the reminder. Reapply while you’re out.” A legible footer states: “Visual demonstration only. Does not measure exact UV damage.”

SunTrace remains central to both parts of the story: its sticker makes otherwise invisible sun exposure easier to understand, while the sunscreen provides the practical action the reveal is designed to prompt. The same short structure can be repeated across TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social communities using everyday participants, familiar campus activities, close-up framing, natural light, and consistent labels that keep the sunscreen application and separate demonstration patch unmistakably clear.

EVALUATION
==========

Weak criteria: originality, audience_impact

Evaluation reasons:
The concept gives a familiar short-form reveal structure a product-specific role: the sticker-shaped contrast makes otherwise invisible sun exposure visible, while the sunscreen is presented as the practical response. This is differentiated and memorable, although the reveal-and-rewind format and reapplication reminder are recognizable social-video devices rather than exceptionally original. Strategic fit is strong because the idea directly addresses forgetful young adults, demonstrates the included stickers, keeps SunTrace SPF 50 central, and clearly separates normal facial application from the uncovered arm demonstration patch. It also avoids claiming that the result measures exact UV damage. Audience impact is solid: the opening removal provides an immediate visual hook, and campus activities make the behavior relatable. However, the mid-activity reminder is less persuasive than the final reveal because the participant merely notices the sticker before its visual effect has been revealed, so the connection between exposure visualization and reapplication may feel constructed. Execution is straightforward for the specified short-video channels and low-to-medium budget, using only the product, stickers, ordinary participants, and accessible locations. The main practical risk is reliably producing a visible temporary contrast under variable weather, lighting, skin response, and exposure time while keeping the distinction between the uncovered demo patch and sunscreen performance clear within a very short runtime.

Refinement suggestions:
Strengthen the behavioral logic by establishing the sticker itself as a planned time-or-activity cue at application, such as pairing it with a natural checkpoint like halftime or a study break, rather than implying that its unrevealed visual effect triggers reapplication. Make the opening reveal, separate demo setup, reapplication action, package shot, and limitation statement readable with minimal copy and a consistent visual layout. Develop a repeatable shot protocol for exposure duration, close-up framing, natural-light continuity, and legibility across different skin tones, while avoiding any promise that every participant will show identical contrast. Preserve the strong reveal-and-rewind structure, but tighten each version around one activity and one clear cue so viewers immediately understand that the arm patch visualizes uncovered exposure and does not test the sunscreen on the face.
