==================== USER INPUT 1/1 ====================
Brand:
SunTrace

Product:
A facial sunscreen that is transparent when applied normally. The package includes reusable UV-sensitive stickers. When a sticker is placed on the skin before sun exposure and removed later, the protected area remains unchanged while the surrounding exposed area temporarily reveals the sticker shape.

Target audience:
Young adults aged 18-30 who understand that sunscreen is important but frequently forget to reapply it during outdoor activities.

Objective:
Make the invisible protective effect of sunscreen visually understandable and motivate users to remember sunscreen application and reapplication.

Key product benefit:
The sunscreen provides SPF 50 protection, while the included UV-sensitive demonstration stickers make the effect of sun exposure easier to visualize.

Channels:
TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social media communities.

Budget:
Low to medium.

Constraints:
Use only the real sunscreen and included demonstration stickers. Do not claim that the temporary visual demonstration measures exact UV damage. Do not use celebrities or expensive locations. The idea must be understandable within a short video.

==================== AGENT OUTPUT ====================
FINAL CREATIVE CONCEPT
======================
“See What You Usually Can’t” is a short-form video concept that turns SunTrace’s invisible SPF 50 protection into a memorable visual reminder. Each TikTok or Instagram Reel opens on a young adult applying the transparent facial sunscreen and placing one of the included reusable UV-sensitive demonstration stickers on their skin before an ordinary outdoor activity. On-screen text reads: “Sunscreen goes on clear. Sun exposure is harder to see.”

After the activity, the creator removes the sticker to reveal its temporary shape against the surrounding exposed area. A clear caption states: “A visual demonstration of sun exposure, not a measurement of exact UV damage.” The reveal is followed immediately by the creator reapplying SunTrace, connecting the surprising visual moment to the behavior the campaign wants viewers to remember. The closing message is: “You may not see protection working. Remember to reapply.” SunTrace and “SPF 50 facial sunscreen with reusable demonstration stickers” remain clearly visible in the final shot.

The videos use a fast before-activity-after-reveal-reapply structure that is understandable without sound and reproducible with only the real SunTrace sunscreen and included stickers. Variations can feature different sticker shapes and familiar outdoor contexts while preserving the same sequence. For university social media communities, the reveal becomes a simple participation format: students post their own short demonstration and finish by showing the reapplication step. The concept gives 18-30-year-olds a concrete, visually distinctive way to understand an otherwise invisible benefit while making reapplication the natural conclusion of every video.

EVALUATION
==========

Evaluation reasons:
Originality is strong because the concept turns the product's included UV-sensitive stickers into a distinctive visual reveal that is specific to SunTrace rather than relying on generic sunscreen education. Strategic fit is strong: it directly addresses young adults who forget to reapply, makes invisible SPF 50 protection easier to understand, gives the product and stickers central roles, includes the required limitation that the demonstration does not measure exact UV damage, and ends with the desired reapplication behavior. Audience impact benefits from a quick, sound-off-friendly reveal, familiar outdoor settings, and a repeatable participation format suited to TikTok, Reels, and university communities. The main limitation is that the sequence is somewhat predictable once viewers have seen one execution, and the relationship between the revealed shape, sunscreen protection, and the UV-sensitive sticker may require especially clear staging to avoid confusion. Executability is strong because it uses only the real product and included stickers, needs no celebrities or expensive locations, and has a simple reusable structure. However, production must account for variable sun exposure, timing, skin visibility, and continuity, which could make the reveal inconsistent across creators.

Refinement suggestions:
Preserve the reveal-and-reapply structure, but sharpen the opening by showing or teasing the final sticker shape in the first second before returning to the application step. Use concise labels such as "before," "after sun exposure," and "reapply" so viewers immediately understand the sequence without sound. Establish a consistent demonstration protocol for creators covering sticker placement, ordinary outdoor duration, camera angle, and lighting, while continuing to avoid any implication that the result measures exact UV damage. Make reapplication as visually prominent as the reveal rather than allowing it to become a brief end card. To sustain repeat viewing, vary the sticker shapes and outdoor situations while keeping the product, disclaimer, and behavioral conclusion identical. For student participation, provide a fixed short-video template and caption language so user posts remain clear, compliant, and recognizably branded.
