==================== AGENT OUTPUT ====================
FINAL CREATIVE CONCEPT
======================
**TinyTea’s “Cold Sip, Same Moment”**

TinyTea turns its launch into one shared student ritual: wherever students are in the world, they pause for the same simple action: open a cold bottle, take a lightly sweetened sip, and pass the moment to another university community.

The campaign begins with a short public launch event at the originating university, where a globally famous celebrity appears in person, takes the first TinyTea sip, and starts the relay. Within the same day, four matching micro-events take place in public university spaces in four other major international cities. Each event follows the same 15-minute format: a visible iced TinyTea station, a group cold-sip countdown, a student reaction recording, and a handoff to the next city. The local university audience remains the campaign’s focal point, with every international checkpoint ending by sending the relay back to students at the originating campus.

Each city uses one standardized interactive digital billboard design. The billboard displays the live relay status, the next university community, and a single scan prompt. Scanning opens the dedicated TinyTea AR app, where the bottle becomes a moving cold-sip marker and overlays the campaign line, **“Keep It Light. Keep It Cold.”** Students can capture a reaction and nominate another university group, but the live event and social participation work without requiring every student to download or use the app.

The execution is built as one compact, repeatable production package: one event layout, one run-of-show, one billboard template adapted five times, one AR asset, and one precise filming brief. Small student-led crews run the matching events using existing university or public spaces, while one professional production unit captures the originating celebrity appearance and assembles the five-city footage into a cinematic launch film with short Instagram and TikTok cutdowns. The campaign is completed during a single seven-day launch window, with university communities driving attendance, nominations, reposts, and local awareness.

TinyTea is the reason to participate, not a prop: every piece of content visibly demonstrates its cold serving occasion and lightly sweetened taste. The relay gives local students a memorable reason to try the new bottle, see their university represented in a global moment, and share TinyTea across the communities the launch needs to reach.

CREATIVE EVALUATION
===================
Originality: 7.0
Strategic fit: 4.0
Audience impact: 6.0
Executability: 1.0
Weighted overall score: 4.7
Weak criteria: executability
Evaluation reasons: The shared cold-sip relay is clearly differentiated from generic beverage launch content and gives TinyTea a recognizable participation ritual, supporting a 7 for originality. The product benefit is visible in the repeated cold-bottle action, and the student communities, Instagram, TikTok, and university settings are relevant to the audience. However, the international relay, celebrity appearance, billboards, AR app, and cinematic launch create a much larger spectacle than a small local company needs for a first-month awareness objective, so the product can become secondary to the production itself. This weakens strategic fit to 4. The countdown, handoff, nomination mechanic, and opportunity for students to see their university represented could earn attention and generate shareable content, supporting a 6 for audience impact. The concept is not executable as stated: a production budget below $1,000 cannot credibly cover a globally famous celebrity appearing in person, five international public events, custom interactive digital billboards in five cities, a dedicated AR application, professional cinematic production, and completion within seven days. Student-led crews and standardized templates reduce complexity but do not resolve the mandatory cost, access, logistics, technical, and scheduling requirements, resulting in a 1 for executability.
Refinement suggestions: Preserve the strongest idea—the simple cold-sip relay and the product-centered demonstration—but make the concept operationally credible by first resolving the conflict between the mandatory global requirements and the very low budget. The proposal needs a specific, evidence-based delivery model for celebrity participation, five-city events, billboard inventory, AR development, professional production, permits, staffing, travel, and seven-day approvals; without committed in-kind resources or confirmed partners, the concept should be treated as infeasible rather than merely streamlined. Clarify how the originating university remains the primary audience and how each international element directly drives local student awareness, rather than functioning as spectacle. Reduce audience friction by keeping the core participation mechanic available through social content and simple scans, while defining exactly what the AR experience adds. Also specify the measurable launch action for students—such as trying TinyTea, posting a cold-sip reaction, or nominating a campus—so the relay converts attention into awareness and trial.
