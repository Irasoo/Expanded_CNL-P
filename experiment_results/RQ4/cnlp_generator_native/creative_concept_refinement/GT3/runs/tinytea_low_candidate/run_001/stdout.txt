==================== AGENT OUTPUT ====================
FINAL CREATIVE CONCEPT
======================
**TinyTea Cold Moment: The Pause That Travels**

TinyTea turns the brief’s international spectacle into one recognizable student ritual: when the day gets too loud, students make a **TinyTea Cold Moment**. They open a chilled bottle of lightly sweetened TinyTea, hold it up to the camera, take one refreshing sip, and pause for three seconds before returning to class, work, or campus life.

During the first month after launch, five live public events take place at university-community gathering points in five major international cities. Each event uses the same compact format: one TinyTea-branded station, one custom interactive digital billboard, and one short “Cold Moment” performance by a globally famous celebrity appearing in person at the designated anchor event. The celebrity demonstrates the ritual without turning it into a conventional endorsement: the celebrity pauses, the billboard freezes the surrounding city scene for three seconds, and the message appears: **“Wherever your day takes you, take it cold.”**

The local university audience remains the campaign’s center. Students see the events through Instagram, TikTok, and local university communities, then make their own Cold Moment in familiar situations such as arriving late to a lecture, leaving a shift, finishing a study session, or meeting friends between classes. Every post shows the same simple proof of product benefit: a cold bottle, a visible open-and-sip action, and a brief refreshing pause. The campaign hashtag is **#TinyTeaColdMoment**.

The dedicated TinyTea augmented-reality mobile application contains one repeatable effect rather than a game: after scanning the billboard or event marker, students see a branded three-second “pause” overlay around their bottle, with the participating city and university community displayed. They can record the moment and share it directly to Instagram or TikTok. The same AR experience, billboard interaction, event script, and vertical video framing are used in all five cities, so each location adds recognition without creating a separate production.

The five events become a rolling **Cold Moment Relay**. Short vertical clips from each city are posted in sequence, while the local university community is invited to respond to the previous city with its own pause. The international locations provide scale and curiosity; the student posts provide the relevant behavior that TinyTea wants associated with the brand: a lightly sweetened iced tea as an easy, cold reset during a busy day.

To keep the execution repeatable within the seven-day production window and the sub-$1,000 campaign production budget, TinyTea produces one locked script, one billboard template, one AR application experience, one celebrity performance format, and one short-form edit package. Event footage is captured in the same vertical setup and assembled into platform-native edits rather than separate cinematic campaigns. The production budget covers only the shared creative adaptation, capture guidance, editing, and distribution through university communities; the mandatory celebrity appearance, five-city event access, billboard placements, dedicated application, and professional cinematic resources must be confirmed as already available or funded outside that production budget before launch.

CREATIVE EVALUATION
===================
Originality: 6.0
Strategic fit: 3.0
Audience impact: 7.0
Executability: 1.0
Weighted overall score: 4.3
Weak criteria: executability
Evaluation reasons: The three-second Cold Moment is a clear, repeatable ritual that connects meaningfully to drinking TinyTea cold, although the pause-and-share format is familiar social-content territory. The concept strongly reflects student situations, short-form channels, launch awareness, and the lightly sweetened cold-tea benefit. Its strategic fit falls sharply because it does not genuinely solve the brief's conflicting mandatory requirements: it conditionally assumes that celebrity access, international events, billboards, an AR application, and cinematic resources are available or funded outside the stated sub-$1,000 total campaign production budget. That changes the practical meaning of the budget without support from the brief. Professional cinematic production is also underdeveloped, and the proposed standardized vertical capture may not satisfy that requirement. The ritual and city relay could be understandable and engaging on Instagram and TikTok, with the bottle playing a visible role, but the international spectacle may distract from a small local beverage launch. Execution is not credible within seven days and below $1,000 because five international live events, five custom interactive billboards, an in-person globally famous celebrity, a dedicated AR application, and professional cinematic production inherently require substantial coordination and resources that have not been supplied.
Refinement suggestions: Preserve the Cold Moment ritual, consistent bottle action, student-life scenarios, and reusable vertical format because these are the strongest product-relevant elements. Before developing the concept further, require documented confirmation that every mandatory international asset, celebrity appearance, venue, billboard, application capability, and cinematic resource is already secured at no additional campaign cost; otherwise the brief has no realistically executable solution under its total budget and seven-day deadline. Clarify whether the celebrity must attend all five events or only one, define exactly what qualifies as professional cinematic production, and produce a seven-day schedule covering application delivery, billboard integration, event permissions, international logistics, filming, approvals, and distribution. Tighten the international activity around the local objective by making every event asset direct viewers to the local university launch and by giving local students the first or final relay moment. Avoid presenting external funding as an execution plan unless the brief explicitly confirms it.
