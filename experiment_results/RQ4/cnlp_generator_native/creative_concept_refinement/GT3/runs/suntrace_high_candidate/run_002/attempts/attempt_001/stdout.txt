Brand:
SunTrace

Product:
A facial sunscreen that is transparent when applied normally. The package includes reusable UV-sensitive stickers. When a sticker is placed on the skin before sun exposure and removed later, the protected area remains unchanged while the surrounding exposed area temporarily reveals the sticker shape.

Target audience:
Young adults aged 18-30 who understand that sunscreen is important but frequently forget to reapply it during outdoor activities.

Objective:
Make the invisible protective effect of sunscreen visually understandable and motivate users to remember sunscreen application and reapplication.

Key product benefit:
The sunscreen provides SPF 50 protection, while the included UV-sensitive demonstration stickers make the effect of sun exposure easier to visualize.

Channels:
TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social media communities.

Budget:
Low to medium.

Constraints:
Use only the real sunscreen and included demonstration stickers. Do not claim that the temporary visual demonstration measures exact UV damage. Do not use celebrities or expensive locations. The idea must be understandable within a short video.

==================== AGENT OUTPUT ====================
FINAL CREATIVE CONCEPT
======================
**The Reapply Check: Make the Invisible Reminder Visible**

SunTrace turns an ordinary outdoor break into a simple, repeatable reminder to apply and reapply sunscreen. The reusable UV-sensitive sticker creates the visual hook, while SunTrace SPF 50 remains the product that protects the skin.

Each TikTok, Instagram Reel, or university social post follows one compact routine filmed in a familiar setting: a campus walk, outdoor study break, park hangout, or sports sideline. The video opens with a close-up of the person applying SunTrace facial sunscreen, followed by placement of one included UV-sensitive sticker on the skin. An on-screen label establishes the demonstration clearly: “SunTrace SPF 50 applied. Sticker placed to show sun exposure.”

The person begins the activity. At a natural midpoint, such as arriving at the destination, taking a scheduled break, or returning to the group, the video cuts to the same close-up framing. The creator pauses, reapplies SunTrace, and returns to the activity. The only prompt is an on-screen interruption: “Still outside? Reapply SunTrace as directed.” This midpoint action is the behavior viewers are meant to copy.

Afterward, the creator removes the sticker in the same framing. The temporary sticker-shaped contrast creates the payoff, but the message stays precise: “The sticker makes exposure visible. It does not measure exact UV damage or sunscreen performance.” The reveal is a reminder that sun exposure continues during an activity, not a comparison proving how much protection the skin received.

The closing shot shows the SunTrace product and reusable stickers beside the creator’s face: “Apply. Reapply. Keep the reminder visible.” Each version uses the same three filmed moments, one sticker, one product, and one ordinary outdoor setting. Different activities provide variation without adding props, locations, effects, or extra production steps. The consistent setup, midpoint reapplication, and before-and-after sticker reveal make the format inexpensive to repeat across TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social communities while directly addressing young adults who remember sunscreen at the start of the day but forget it while staying outside.

===================

Evaluation reasons: The concept earns strong originality because it turns the included sticker demonstration into a repeatable, product-specific social format rather than relying on generic sunscreen education. Its strategic fit is also strong: SunTrace SPF 50 is visibly applied and reapplied, the midpoint interruption directly targets the audience's tendency to forget, and the language avoids claiming that the sticker measures exact UV damage or sunscreen performance. The familiar outdoor settings and concise reveal suit young adults and the specified channels. Audience impact is slightly lower because the sticker-shaped payoff is visually memorable, but the relationship between the reveal, sunscreen protection, and the midpoint reapplication may not be immediately clear; viewers could interpret the result as proof of performance despite the disclaimer, while a long disclaimer may be difficult to absorb in a short video. Executability is strong because the format uses only the real product, included stickers, ordinary locations, and three repeatable filming moments. Its main practical risks are maintaining matched close-up framing, allowing enough real exposure for a visible result, and fitting the setup, reapplication behavior, reveal, and qualification into a short runtime without losing clarity.
Refinement suggestions: Preserve the simple three-beat routine and visual reveal, but clarify what each element demonstrates. Use a very short opening label such as "Sticker demonstration, not a UV measurement," then keep the midpoint prompt focused on the actual behavior: "Still outside? Reapply as directed." At the reveal, explicitly separate the roles of the two components with concise wording such as "Sticker: makes exposure visible. SunTrace SPF 50: protects when used as directed." Strengthen the behavioral hook by tying reapplication to recognizable moments young adults already experience, such as halftime, the end of a study session, or arriving at a second location. Keep each version to one activity and use a fixed shot sequence and timing template so the reveal remains easy to follow across TikTok, Reels, and university accounts. Avoid implying that the final sticker contrast proves the effectiveness of the midpoint application, since the demonstration does not establish that comparison.
