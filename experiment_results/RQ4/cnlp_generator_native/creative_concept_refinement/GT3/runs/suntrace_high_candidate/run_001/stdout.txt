==================== USER INPUT 1/1 ====================
Brand:
SunTrace

Product:
A facial sunscreen that is transparent when applied normally. The package includes reusable UV-sensitive stickers. When a sticker is placed on the skin before sun exposure and removed later, the protected area remains unchanged while the surrounding exposed area temporarily reveals the sticker shape.

Target audience:
Young adults aged 18-30 who understand that sunscreen is important but frequently forget to reapply it during outdoor activities.

Objective:
Make the invisible protective effect of sunscreen visually understandable and motivate users to remember sunscreen application and reapplication.

Key product benefit:
The sunscreen provides SPF 50 protection, while the included UV-sensitive demonstration stickers make the effect of sun exposure easier to visualize.

Channels:
TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social media communities.

Budget:
Low to medium.

Constraints:
Use only the real sunscreen and included demonstration stickers. Do not claim that the temporary visual demonstration measures exact UV damage. Do not use celebrities or expensive locations. The idea must be understandable within a short video.

==================== AGENT OUTPUT ====================
FINAL CREATIVE CONCEPT
======================
“See What You Usually Can’t” is a short-form social video concept that makes SunTrace’s invisible sunscreen protection visually understandable and turns the reveal into a reapplication reminder.

Each TikTok or Instagram Reel opens on a young adult preparing for an outdoor activity. They apply SunTrace facial sunscreen normally, place one of the included reusable UV-sensitive demonstration stickers on the skin, and head outside. A simple on-screen prompt reads: “Sunscreen protection is invisible. Let’s make sun exposure easier to see.”

Later, the user removes the sticker on camera. The protected area remains unchanged while the surrounding exposed area temporarily reveals the sticker shape, creating an immediate visual payoff that is easy to understand in a short video. The reveal freezes briefly beside the message: “Sun exposure can be easy to forget when you can’t see it.” The user then holds up SunTrace and reapplies, followed by the closing line: “See the reminder. Reapply SunTrace SPF 50.”

A persistent caption clarifies: “Temporary visual demonstration only. Does not measure exact UV damage.”

SunTrace is central to both the demonstration and the behavior change: its transparent SPF 50 formula provides the stated protection, while its included reusable UV-sensitive stickers turn an otherwise invisible effect into a visible reminder. The concept uses only the real product and stickers and can be filmed in ordinary outdoor settings without celebrities or expensive locations. For university social media communities, students can post their own short reveal-and-reapply videos using the same opening prompt, reveal, disclaimer, and closing message, creating a repeatable format tailored to young adults who know sunscreen matters but often forget to reapply during outdoor activities.

Evaluation reasons: The concept is memorable and product-specific because it turns SunTrace's included UV-sensitive stickers into both a visual demonstration and a behavioral reminder rather than relying on generic sunscreen messaging. It strongly addresses the brief: SunTrace and its SPF 50 benefit are central, the reveal makes an invisible issue understandable, and reapplication is shown as the immediate response. The quick transformation provides a clear short-video hook for young adults, although the preparation and explanation before the reveal could slow attention if not tightly edited. Execution is practical for low- to medium-budget social content, uses ordinary outdoor settings and only the specified product and stickers, supports repeatable student-created posts, and includes the required limitation that the demonstration does not measure exact UV damage. Its main execution risk is that the reveal depends on real sun exposure and therefore may vary in timing or visual clarity.
Refinement suggestions: Lead with a brief preview of the completed sticker-shape reveal before showing the application sequence, then return to the full reveal to improve retention without losing clarity. Keep the setup visually compressed and make the reapplication action unmistakable, with the product name and SPF 50 readable at the payoff. Establish a simple filming protocol using only the real product and stickers, covering placement, exposure conditions, camera angle, and an alternate take when the visual result is faint. Preserve the persistent disclaimer and avoid language suggesting that the sticker measures UV damage or proves exact protection. For university posts, provide one concise repeatable format with fixed reveal, disclaimer, and reapplication beats while allowing students to vary the outdoor activity and sticker shape.
