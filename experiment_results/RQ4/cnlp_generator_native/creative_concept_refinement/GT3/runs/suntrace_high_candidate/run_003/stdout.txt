Brand:
SunTrace

A facial sunscreen that is transparent when applied normally. The package includes reusable UV-sensitive stickers. Young adults aged 18-30 who understand that sunscreen is important but frequently forget to reapply it during outdoor activities.

TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social media communities.

Low to medium.

==================== AGENT OUTPUT ====================
FINAL CREATIVE CONCEPT
======================
“Leave a Reminder, Not a Trace”

Core idea: SunTrace turns an otherwise invisible sunscreen habit into a memorable visual cue. A reusable UV-sensitive sticker temporarily reveals a recognizable shape after sun exposure, demonstrating that exposure can leave a visible trace even though facial sunscreen protection goes on transparent. The reveal becomes the prompt to remember SunTrace SPF 50 and reapplication during outdoor activities.

Principal execution: A short vertical video opens with a young adult preparing to go outside. They place a SunTrace demonstration sticker on their skin, apply the transparent facial sunscreen normally, and hold the SunTrace package to camera. On-screen text reads: “Sunscreen goes on clear. So it’s easy to forget.”

Fast cuts show the person continuing their outdoor activity. At a natural break, they reach for SunTrace and reapply. The video then jumps to the later sticker removal: the protected area remains unchanged while the surrounding exposed area temporarily reveals the sticker shape. The camera holds on the simple, immediate result as the text changes to: “Sun exposure can leave a trace. Don’t let protection leave your mind.”

The video closes on the real SunTrace sunscreen and reusable sticker with the line: “SunTrace SPF 50. Apply. Reapply. Remember.” A small on-screen clarification states: “Temporary visual demonstration only. Does not measure exact UV damage.”

Brand and product role: SunTrace is central at every stage: its transparent SPF 50 sunscreen provides the protection, while its included reusable stickers make sun exposure easier to visualize and create a concrete reminder for a habit that is otherwise invisible.

Audience and objective fit: The fast setup, delayed reveal, and concise reapplication message are designed for TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social communities. The sticker-shape reveal provides an immediate visual payoff for young adults who already value sunscreen but need a memorable cue to apply and reapply it during outdoor activities. The concept can be filmed in ordinary outdoor settings with the actual sunscreen and included stickers, without celebrities, expensive locations, or unsupported measurement claims.

CREATIVE EVALUATION
===================

Weak criteria: originality, strategic_fit, audience_impact, executability
Evaluation reasons: The concept is strongly product-specific: it turns SunTrace's transparent SPF 50 protection and included demonstration stickers into a clear visual reveal and a relevant memory device. Although transformation and reveal videos are familiar on short-form platforms, the line "Leave a Reminder, Not a Trace" and the sticker's direct connection to an otherwise invisible benefit give the approach meaningful distinction. Strategic fit is strong because SunTrace remains central throughout, reapplication is explicitly demonstrated, the message addresses forgetfulness, and the clarification avoids presenting the result as an exact measure of UV damage. The setup, fast progression, and reveal should be immediately understandable and provide a memorable payoff for young adults. Execution is practical with the real product, stickers, an ordinary outdoor setting, and no costly talent or location requirements. The main weakness is that the timing and cause of the reveal could become unclear in a very short edit, particularly if the application, reapplication, elapsed exposure, and sticker removal are compressed too aggressively. The wording around the surrounding exposed area must also avoid implying that every visible difference represents measured skin damage or that a single demonstration proves protection duration.
Refinement suggestions: Preserve the simple setup-and-reveal structure, but make the sequence visually unambiguous with concise time markers such as "Before going out," "Reapply," and "Later." Show the sticker placement and normal sunscreen application in one continuous close shot, then give the final removal enough screen time for viewers to understand the result before the closing message appears. Keep the package visible at the opening and close rather than forcing it into every shot. Strengthen the behavioral prompt by connecting reapplication to a recognizable activity break, such as returning to a bench or checking a bag, without prescribing an unsupported universal interval. Keep the clarification legible for the full reveal shot and use restrained wording that frames the sticker as a visual demonstration and reminder, not a diagnostic or exact UV measurement.
