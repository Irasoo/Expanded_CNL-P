==================== USER INPUT 1/1 ====================
Brand:
SunTrace

Product:
A facial sunscreen that is transparent when applied normally. The package includes reusable UV-sensitive stickers. When a sticker is placed on the skin before sun exposure and removed later, the protected area remains unchanged while the surrounding exposed area temporarily reveals the sticker shape.

Target audience:
Young adults aged 18-30 who understand that sunscreen is important but frequently forget to reapply it during outdoor activities.

Objective:
Make the invisible protective effect of sunscreen visually understandable and motivate users to remember sunscreen application and reapplication.

Key product benefit:
The sunscreen provides SPF 50 protection, while the included UV-sensitive demonstration stickers make the effect of sun exposure easier to visualize.

Channels:
TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social media communities.

Budget:
Low to medium.

Constraints:
Use only the real sunscreen and included demonstration stickers. Do not claim that the temporary visual demonstration measures exact UV damage. Do not use celebrities or expensive locations. The idea must be understandable within a short video.
================== END USER INPUT 1/1 ==================

==================== AGENT OUTPUT ====================

=== Final Creative Concept ===
**The SunTrace Check-In**

A short-form series turns an ordinary campus day into a visible sunscreen check-in. Each video opens on a quick reveal: the same reusable UV-sensitive sticker has left a temporary shape after a student returns from being outside. On-screen text says, **“Your sunscreen is invisible. Your sun stops don’t have to be.”**

The video then rewinds through three familiar moments: leaving class, meeting friends for lunch, and heading home. Before each outdoor stretch, the student applies SunTrace SPF 50, places the sticker on their face, and goes outside. When they come back indoors, they remove the sticker to reveal the temporary contrast between the protected area and the surrounding exposed area. The sticker is then placed on a new area after the student reapplies sunscreen, creating a repeatable visual check rather than a one-time demonstration.

The reveal is presented as a visual demonstration of sun exposure, not a measurement of exact UV damage. The same format can be filmed by different students in real campus settings using only a phone, SunTrace, the included reusable stickers, and natural daylight. Its recognizable rhythm, **“leave, protect, check, reapply,”** makes the product’s invisible SPF 50 benefit easier to understand while giving young adults a memorable cue at the moments they usually forget to reapply.

Closing line: **“Check your sun stops. Reapply with SunTrace SPF 50.”**
