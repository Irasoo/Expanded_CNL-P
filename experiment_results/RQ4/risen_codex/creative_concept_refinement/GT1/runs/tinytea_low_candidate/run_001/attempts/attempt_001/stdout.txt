==================== AGENT OUTPUT ====================

=== Final Creative Concept ===
**TinyTea: The Impossible Launch, Live**

TinyTea turns its contradictory launch brief into the campaign itself: a seven-day public test of whether a tiny local tea brand really needs a giant global spectacle to earn students’ attention.

The campaign begins with a cinematic hero film in which a globally famous celebrity appears in person and reads TinyTea’s deliberately excessive launch plan: live public events in five major international cities, custom interactive digital billboards in each city, a dedicated AR mobile application, and a complete production in seven days for less than $1,000. The celebrity then holds up a cold TinyTea and asks students: **“Would you notice the launch—or the tea?”**

For the next seven days, the same question plays out across the five live city events, their interactive billboards, and the AR app. Each experience lets participants reveal a virtual “TinyTea break” in the middle of the spectacle: a cold, lightly sweetened bottled tea appearing in an ordinary student moment such as between lectures, at a library desk, or on a campus bench. The app and billboards end with a simple prompt: **“Show us your smallest useful break.”**

Students in local university communities respond with phone-shot Instagram and TikTok videos of the most practical TinyTea break between two real campus activities. TinyTea reposts the strongest entries throughout the first month, linking the international spectacle back to the audience’s everyday routine. The recurring line is: **“No giant launch needed. Just cold, lightly sweetened tea between what’s next.”**

The celebrity, five-city events, billboards, AR experience, and cinematic film are not presented as ordinary ongoing brand infrastructure; they are the deliberately impossible launch checklist and the visual challenge that gives the campaign its hook. TinyTea remains the point of the story: despite the scale of the brief, its benefit is simple, affordable, and immediately relevant to students.

To keep the concept truthful and executable, the five-city events, celebrity appearance, billboard placements, AR application, cinematic production, seven-day schedule, and sub-$1,000 budget must be treated as externally supplied or formally approved campaign requirements before production. No celebrity, venue, billboard, technology, or production capability is claimed as secured by TinyTea. If those requirements cannot be supplied within the stated conditions, the campaign cannot be delivered as specified; the student-first local version is the practical fallback, not a claim that the mandatory elements have been completed.
