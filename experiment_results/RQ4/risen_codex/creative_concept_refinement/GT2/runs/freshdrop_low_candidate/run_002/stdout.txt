FINAL CREATIVE CONCEPT

## Concept: “The FreshDrop Handoff”

FreshDrop turns portability into a recognizable visual relay. Instead of simply appearing in a series of lifestyle scenes, the bottle is always handed from one moment to the next. A person receives it at the end of one activity, and that same handoff continues in a new setting with another person or a later moment in the day.

The bottle becomes the campaign’s recurring “handoff point”: work passes to a commute, a workout passes to a walk, and time at home passes to meeting friends. The idea gives ordinary day-in-the-life advertising a simple, repeatable structure while keeping the product physically central.

### Principal Execution

Create short vertical videos for Instagram Reels, Stories, and Facebook. Every video follows the same three-beat pattern:

1. **Receive:** A FreshDrop bottle enters clearly into the opening frame.
2. **Carry:** The person uses or carries it through an everyday moment.
3. **Hand off:** The bottle exits in one direction and is received from that same direction in the next scene.

For example:

- At a desk, one person picks up the bottle and hands it out of frame. A commuter receives it on the other side of the cut.
- A person leaving a gym bag passes it forward. Someone on an outdoor walk catches it and continues moving.
- A bottle is handed from a kitchen counter to a person heading out, then from that person to a friend arriving at a park.

The handoff should always use a similar camera distance, movement direction, and screen position. This consistent visual rule lets viewers recognize the campaign quickly, even with sound off, while the changing people and settings keep each execution fresh.

The opening text can be brief and consistent:

**“Ready for what’s next?”**

The final handoff lands on a clean product shot with the campaign line:

**“FreshDrop. Pass on the next moment.”**

The wording can also appear as on-screen text so the idea works without audio. The product remains visible at the beginning and end, rather than appearing only as a background lifestyle prop.

### Still and Carousel Adaptation

Still posts show two adjacent everyday moments connected by the bottle at the edge of the frame: one person’s hand releases it while another hand is about to receive it. A carousel turns the handoff into a sequence, with the bottle positioned consistently between panels:

- **Panel 1:** Work
- **Panel 2:** Commute
- **Panel 3:** Exercise or outdoor time
- **Panel 4:** Relaxation or meeting friends

Each panel repeats the same handoff position, creating a recognizable visual system without requiring special locations, unusual effects, or distinctive packaging.

### Brand and Product Role

FreshDrop is the object that makes the relay possible. Its role is simple and accurate: it provides drinking water in a portable bottle that can move with people through different parts of the day. The campaign’s distinctive mechanism is the repeated handoff, not a special product claim, package feature, or brand backstory. Any bottled-water brand could use the structure unchanged, as required by the brief.

### Audience and Objective

Adults aged 18–45 can immediately understand the ordinary settings and the simple question of what comes next. The opening handoff creates a clear reason to watch: who will receive the bottle next, and where will it go? The repeated movement and consistent brand presence make FreshDrop easier to recognize across multiple short videos, still posts, and carousels.

The emotional payoff is light and positive: the day keeps moving, and drinking water can move with it. The concept supports general awareness without depending on a narrow lifestyle identity or a specific audience insight.

### Production and Adaptation

The campaign can be filmed with conventional lifestyle photography and short-form video on a medium budget. It requires a small group of adult participants, ordinary locations, one consistent bottle, and straightforward cuts or match actions. The handoff can be performed by two people in the same location or made to look continuous across separate locations; no complex effects are necessary.

A single shoot can produce multiple short videos, still images, and carousels by changing the people, settings, and direction of the day while preserving the same three-beat structure. This makes the idea practical for Instagram and Facebook and repeatable enough to build recognition over time.
