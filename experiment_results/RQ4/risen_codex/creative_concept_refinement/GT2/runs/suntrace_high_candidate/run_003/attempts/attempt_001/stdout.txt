==================== USER INPUT 1/1 ====================
Brand:
SunTrace

Product:
A facial sunscreen that is transparent when applied normally. The package includes reusable UV-sensitive stickers. When a sticker is placed on the skin before sun exposure and removed later, the protected area remains unchanged while the surrounding exposed area temporarily reveals the sticker shape.

Target audience:
Young adults aged 18-30 who understand that sunscreen is important but frequently forget to reapply it during outdoor activities.

Objective:
Make the invisible protective effect of sunscreen visually understandable and motivate users to remember sunscreen application and reapplication.

Key product benefit:
The sunscreen provides SPF 50 protection, while the included UV-sensitive demonstration stickers make the effect of sun exposure easier to visualize.

Channels:
TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social media communities.

Budget:
Low to medium.

Constraints:
Use only the real sunscreen and included demonstration stickers. Do not claim that the temporary visual demonstration measures exact UV damage. Do not use celebrities or expensive locations. The idea must be understandable within a short video.

==================== AGENT OUTPUT ====================

FINAL CREATIVE CONCEPT

## Revised Concept: “The SunTrace Check-In”

SunTrace makes an invisible sunscreen habit visible through one simple, repeatable check-in: **apply before going out, check while you are still out, and reapply according to the product directions.**

### Core video format

Each TikTok, Instagram Reel, or university social post follows the same short vertical structure, filmed by one student on a phone in an ordinary campus setting.

**1. Hook — 0–2 seconds**  
Close-up of the reusable UV-sensitive sticker beside the SunTrace pack.

On-screen text:  
**“Sunscreen is invisible. This is your check-in.”**

**2. Clear demonstration setup — 2–6 seconds**  
In one uninterrupted close-up, the creator shows the real product and follows the SunTrace package directions for normal SPF 50 application and sticker placement. The video labels the steps clearly:

- **“Apply SunTrace normally.”**
- **“Place the reusable UV sticker as directed.”**

The sticker is shown in place before the creator goes outdoors. The video does not alter the sticker, add visual effects, or suggest that the sticker itself measures skin damage.

**3. Habit moment — 6–10 seconds**  
The creator walks between classes, sits outside, or meets friends. Two quick text check-ins appear during the ordinary activity:

- **“Heading out? Applied.”**
- **“Still outside? Reapply according to product directions.”**

No specific reapplication interval is invented, and the activity is filmed as normal outdoor use rather than as an invitation to seek prolonged sun exposure.

**4. Product reveal — 10–14 seconds**  
Using the same close-up framing as the opening, the creator shows the sticker after outdoor exposure. The sticker shape is visible in the surrounding exposed skin while the area beneath it remains unchanged.

The reveal carries an immediate, readable disclaimer:

**“Visual demonstration only—not a measure of UV damage.”**

The creator removes the sticker and says:

**“I couldn’t see sunscreen working before. Now I have a reminder to check in.”**

**5. End frame — 14–16 seconds**  
SunTrace SPF 50 and the reusable sticker appear together.

Final line:  
**“See the reminder. Remember to protect.”**

A final prompt reinforces the behavior:

**“Applied? Check in. Still outside? Reapply as directed.”**

### Why the format is practical

The series uses one creator, one phone, one reusable sticker, the real SunTrace sunscreen, and familiar university locations. Every video follows the same five-shot template, making the idea easy to repeat across TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social media communities while allowing simple changes in activity or setting.

The sticker and sunscreen remain central to every film: the sunscreen is applied normally, the sticker creates the recognizable visual contrast, and the reveal turns an invisible protective habit into a memorable check-in without claiming to measure exact UV exposure or damage.
