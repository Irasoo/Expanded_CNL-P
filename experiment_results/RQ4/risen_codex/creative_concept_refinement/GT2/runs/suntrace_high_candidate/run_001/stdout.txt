==================== USER INPUT 1/1 ====================
Brand:
SunTrace

Product:
A facial sunscreen that is transparent when applied normally. The package includes reusable UV-sensitive stickers. When a sticker is placed on the skin before sun exposure and removed later, the protected area remains unchanged while the surrounding exposed area temporarily reveals the sticker shape.

Target audience:
Young adults aged 18-30 who understand that sunscreen is important but frequently forget to reapply it during outdoor activities.

Objective:
Make the invisible protective effect of sunscreen visually understandable and motivate users to remember sunscreen application and reapplication.

Key product benefit:
The sunscreen provides SPF 50 protection, while the included UV-sensitive demonstration stickers make the effect of sun exposure easier to visualize.

Channels:
TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social media communities.

Budget:
Low to medium.

Constraints:
Use only the real sunscreen and included demonstration stickers. Do not claim that the temporary visual demonstration measures exact UV damage. Do not use celebrities or expensive locations. The idea must be understandable within a short video.

==================== AGENT OUTPUT ====================

FINAL CREATIVE CONCEPT

## SunTrace: “The Invisible Layer”

Create a short-form video series showing how SunTrace SPF 50 makes an invisible step visible.

In each TikTok, Instagram Reel, or university-community post, a creator places two reusable UV-sensitive demonstration stickers side by side—one on an area covered with SunTrace transparent facial sunscreen and one left without it. They go outside during an everyday outdoor activity, such as walking across campus, sitting outside, or meeting friends. Before leaving, the creator applies SunTrace to the protected area and says, “You can’t see sunscreen—but you can see why it matters.” After time outdoors, the stickers are shown together in close-up, making the contrast between the protected and exposed sides easy to understand. The video ends with the creator reapplying SunTrace and the prompt: “Going back outside? Reapply your invisible layer.”

The stickers serve as a simple visual demonstration of the difference between covered and exposed areas; the content explicitly avoids presenting them as instruments that measure exact UV damage. SunTrace’s role is the practical protection step: its transparent SPF 50 formula is applied before going outdoors and reapplied during continued outdoor activity. The demonstration stickers make that otherwise invisible protection easier to grasp without changing the product’s real function or making additional performance claims.

The format is designed for quick, repeatable filming with the real sunscreen, the included stickers, and ordinary university or outdoor settings. Its everyday scenarios speak directly to 18–30-year-olds who understand that sunscreen matters but may forget to reapply. By turning reapplication into the visible final action, the idea connects understanding with a memorable behavior: apply before going out, and reapply when staying outside.
