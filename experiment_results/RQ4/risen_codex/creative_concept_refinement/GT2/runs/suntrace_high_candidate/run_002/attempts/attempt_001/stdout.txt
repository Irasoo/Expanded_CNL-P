==================== USER INPUT 1/1 ====================
Brand:
SunTrace

Product:
A facial sunscreen that is transparent when applied normally. The package includes reusable UV-sensitive stickers. When a sticker is placed on the skin before sun exposure and removed later, the protected area remains unchanged while the surrounding exposed area temporarily reveals the sticker shape.

Target audience:
Young adults aged 18-30 who understand that sunscreen is important but frequently forget to reapply it during outdoor activities.

Objective:
Make the invisible protective effect of sunscreen visually understandable and motivate users to remember sunscreen application and reapplication.

Key product benefit:
The sunscreen provides SPF 50 protection, while the included UV-sensitive demonstration stickers make the effect of sun exposure easier to visualize.

Channels:
TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social media communities.

Budget:
Low to medium.

Constraints:
Use only the real sunscreen and included demonstration stickers. Do not claim that the temporary visual demonstration measures exact UV damage. Do not use celebrities or expensive locations. The idea must be understandable within a short video.
================== END USER INPUT 1/1 ==================

==================== AGENT OUTPUT ====================

FINAL CREATIVE CONCEPT

## **SunTrace “The Visible Check-In”**

A repeatable 8–10-second TikTok, Instagram Reel, or university-community video that makes the sunscreen habit easy to remember without suggesting that the sticker measures UV damage or times reapplication.

The video uses one fixed close-up of the same included UV-sensitive sticker throughout:

1. **Apply:** A student applies SunTrace SPF 50 to exposed skin before heading outdoors. The sunscreen disappears transparently. On-screen text: **“Protection can be invisible.”**
2. **Go:** Two or three quick cuts show an ordinary campus activity—walking between classes, sitting outside, or playing a casual sport. No time claim is made.
3. **Check and reapply:** The sticker’s temporary shape becomes visible in the surrounding exposed skin. The student points to it as a visible cue, picks up SunTrace, and reapplies according to the product directions. On-screen text: **“Visible reminder. Check. Reapply as directed.”**

The closing frame shows the product and sticker with the line: **“SunTrace: SPF 50 protection you can’t see—and a sticker that helps you remember.”** A compact caption remains on screen: **“The sticker is a visual demonstration only; it does not measure exact UV exposure, UV damage, or when to reapply.”**

The sticker is not presented as revealing the sunscreen working. SunTrace provides the protection; the reusable sticker simply creates a visible reminder of sun exposure when the sunscreen itself cannot be seen. The direct action—notice the sticker, then check the product directions and reapply—keeps the behavior clear and accurate.

For practical production, every video follows the same three-beat template, with one well-lit close-up for the sticker reveal and familiar campus locations instead of staged sets. Students can recreate the format with different everyday activities while keeping the same opening, reveal, and closing text. This keeps the idea inexpensive, recognizable across TikTok, Reels, and university social channels, and short enough that the product, visual cue, and reapplication prompt are not crowded together.
