==================== USER INPUT 1/1 ====================
Brand:
FoldBrew

Product:
A premium instant coffee concentrate packaged inside an edible, water-soluble coffee cup sleeve. When the sleeve is placed in hot water, it dissolves and becomes one serving of coffee.

Target audience:
Urban office workers aged 22-35 who regularly buy takeaway coffee but want a faster and lower-waste option at work.

Objective:
Drive first-time trial of FoldBrew and make the product's unusual "dissolve the packaging to make the coffee" behavior memorable.

Key product benefit:
The package itself becomes the coffee, so the user does not need a coffee machine, coffee pod, or disposable coffee sachet.

Channels:
Instagram, TikTok, and short-form digital video.

Budget:
Medium.

Constraints:
Use ordinary product photography and short-form video production. Do not use celebrities, large public installations, or fictional environmental claims. The central idea must visibly demonstrate the package dissolving into the drink.
================== END USER INPUT 1/1 ==================

==================== AGENT OUTPUT ====================

=== FINAL CREATIVE CONCEPT ===
**The Bin Reflex**

FoldBrew turns a familiar office reflex into a product reveal: the moment someone assumes the sleeve is finished and moves it toward the bin.

Each vertical video opens on an urban desk during a recognizable workday moment—a meeting about to start, the first login of the morning, or an afternoon slump. A worker holds the FoldBrew sleeve over a wastebasket. Before it leaves frame, on-screen text cuts in:

**“That sleeve is your coffee.”**

The worker redirects it into a clear mug of hot water. A tight, continuous macro shot visibly captures the sleeve dissolving and the water becoming one serving of coffee. The worker takes the mug back to the desk, turning an apparent cleanup moment into a coffee break without a machine, pod, or separate coffee sachet.

Every film follows the same memorable ritual: **hand toward bin, text interruption, sleeve into mug, visible dissolve, finished coffee.** The changing office situations keep the series relatable while the repeated action makes FoldBrew’s unusual behavior recognizable.

The end card reads:

**“Not the bin. The mug.”**  
**“The package becomes the coffee.”**

A final trial prompt—**“Place the FoldBrew sleeve in hot water.”**—makes first use clear. FoldBrew remains central throughout: its sleeve is the object viewers nearly discard, the product being placed in the mug, and the coffee revealed at the end. The concept speaks directly to office workers by dramatizing a familiar desk-side habit, while making the package-to-coffee transformation impossible to miss in a short-form feed.
