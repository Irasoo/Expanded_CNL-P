==================== USER INPUT 1/1 ====================
Brand:
SunTrace

Product:
A facial sunscreen that is transparent when applied normally. The package includes reusable UV-sensitive stickers. When a sticker is placed on the skin before sun exposure and removed later, the protected area remains unchanged while the surrounding exposed area temporarily reveals the sticker shape.

Target audience:
Young adults aged 18-30 who understand that sunscreen is important but frequently forget to reapply it during outdoor activities.

Objective:
Make the invisible protective effect of sunscreen visually understandable and motivate users to remember sunscreen application and reapplication.

Key product benefit:
The sunscreen provides SPF 50 protection, while the included UV-sensitive demonstration stickers make the effect of sun exposure easier to visualize.

Channels:
TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social media communities.

Budget:
Low to medium.

Constraints:
Use only the real sunscreen and included demonstration stickers. Do not claim that the temporary visual demonstration measures exact UV damage. Do not use celebrities or expensive locations. The idea must be understandable within a short video.
================== END USER INPUT 1/1 ==================

==================== AGENT OUTPUT ====================

=== FINAL CREATIVE CONCEPT ===
**SunTrace “Bookmark Your Sun Day”** turns the included UV-sensitive sticker into a physical bookmark for the moments when young adults move through an outdoor day—and a visible cue to reapply.

In a short TikTok, Reel, or university-community video, a student applies SunTrace SPF 50 and places the reusable sticker on their skin before leaving for an ordinary campus activity. The video labels this moment **“Chapter 1: Start protected.”** After walking between classes, sitting outside, or meeting friends, they remove the sticker in a close-up. The protected area remains unchanged while the surrounding exposed area temporarily reveals the sticker shape: **“The sun kept the page.”**

Instead of ending with the reveal, the creator immediately treats it as the day’s reapplication bookmark. Before continuing outdoors, they apply SunTrace again, with the sequence: **“Mark the start. See the exposure. Reapply for the next chapter.”** The same structure can be repeated across different everyday campus routines, making the sticker a recognizable SunTrace ritual rather than a one-time visual trick.

The idea gives the product a specific role: SunTrace makes invisible protection easier to understand, while its included sticker turns a natural break in the day into a memorable prompt to reapply. It speaks directly to young adults who know sunscreen matters but lose track of it during changing activities. The execution uses one person, the real sunscreen, one reusable sticker, natural outdoor light, and a close-up phone video. A concise end caption states that the sticker is a visual demonstration of sun exposure, not a measurement of exact UV damage or skin damage.
