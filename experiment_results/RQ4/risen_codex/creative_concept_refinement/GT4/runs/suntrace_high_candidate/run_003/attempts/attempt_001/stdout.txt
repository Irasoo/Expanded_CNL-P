==================== USER INPUT 1/1 ====================
Brand:
SunTrace

Product:
A facial sunscreen that is transparent when applied normally. The package includes reusable UV-sensitive stickers. When a sticker is placed on the skin before sun exposure and removed later, the protected area remains unchanged while the surrounding exposed area temporarily reveals the sticker shape.

Target audience:
Young adults aged 18-30 who understand that sunscreen is important but frequently forget to reapply it during outdoor activities.

Objective:
Make the invisible protective effect of sunscreen visually understandable and motivate users to remember sunscreen application and reapplication.

Key product benefit:
The sunscreen provides SPF 50 protection, while the included UV-sensitive demonstration stickers make the effect of sun exposure easier to visualize.

Channels:
TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social media communities.

Budget:
Low to medium.

Constraints:
Use only the real sunscreen and included demonstration stickers. Do not claim that the temporary visual demonstration measures exact UV damage. Do not use celebrities or expensive locations. The idea must be understandable within a short video.

==================== AGENT OUTPUT ====================

=== FINAL CREATIVE CONCEPT ===
**SunTrace: Don’t Let Your Plans Outlast Your SPF**

A short-form video series turns an ordinary outdoor day into a sequence of “sun chapters.” The recurring question is: **“Are you still on the same SPF?”**

Each video opens at the exact moment plans unexpectedly continue: lunch becomes a walk, a study break becomes a game, or a quick campus stop becomes an afternoon outside. A friend asks, **“Coming?”** The person looks at the sunscreen they applied earlier, then hesitates.

The video cuts to the start of the day. They apply SunTrace SPF 50 to a small area of skin as directed and place one reusable UV-sensitive sticker over it before going outside. After the first outdoor activity, they remove the sticker in a clear close-up. The protected area remains unchanged while the surrounding exposed area temporarily reveals the sticker’s shape, creating a visible **“chapter mark”** from the time spent outside.

On-screen text beside the reveal reads: **“A visual demonstration, not a measurement of UV damage.”**

The person then treats the reveal as a checkpoint between plans. Before joining the next activity, they apply SunTrace again and say, **“New plan. New application.”** The sticker is shown being placed again before they head back outside, reinforcing its role as a reusable visual reminder rather than a UV meter.

Every episode follows the same distinctive format: **unexpected plan extension → first SPF chapter → sticker chapter mark → new plan → reapply before going back out.** The situations change, but the chapter-mark reveal always interrupts the easy assumption that one morning application covers the whole day.

The closing line is: **“When your plans get a new chapter, give your SPF one too. SunTrace SPF 50. Apply and reapply.”**

The concept uses only SunTrace sunscreen, the included reusable UV-sensitive sticker, natural daylight, one or two people, and familiar campus or everyday locations. It makes the product’s normally invisible protection visible while connecting the sticker directly to the target audience’s real reapplication trigger: an outdoor day that unexpectedly keeps going.
