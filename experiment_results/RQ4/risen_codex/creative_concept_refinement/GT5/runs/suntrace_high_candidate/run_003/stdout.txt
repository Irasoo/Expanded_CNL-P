Creative brief: 

==================== USER INPUT 1/1 ====================
Brand:
SunTrace

Product:
A facial sunscreen that is transparent when applied normally. The package includes reusable UV-sensitive stickers. When a sticker is placed on the skin before sun exposure and removed later, the protected area remains unchanged while the surrounding exposed area temporarily reveals the sticker shape.

Target audience:
Young adults aged 18-30 who understand that sunscreen is important but frequently forget to reapply it during outdoor activities.

Objective:
Make the invisible protective effect of sunscreen visually understandable and motivate users to remember sunscreen application and reapplication.

Key product benefit:
The sunscreen provides SPF 50 protection, while the included UV-sensitive demonstration stickers make the effect of sun exposure easier to visualize.

Channels:
TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social media communities.

Budget:
Low to medium.

Constraints:
Use only the real sunscreen and included demonstration stickers. Do not claim that the temporary visual demonstration measures exact UV damage. Do not use celebrities or expensive locations. The idea must be understandable within a short video.
================== END USER INPUT 1/1 ==================

==================== AGENT OUTPUT ====================

=== FINAL CREATIVE CONCEPT ===
**SunTrace: See What Sunscreen Does**

SunTrace turns an invisible protective effect into a simple before-and-after moment. In each short TikTok or Instagram Reel, a young adult applies the transparent SPF 50 facial sunscreen before heading outside, places one of the included reusable UV-sensitive stickers on their skin, and goes about an ordinary outdoor activity such as walking across campus, sitting outside, or meeting friends. Later, they remove the sticker on camera. The skin beneath the sticker remains visually unchanged while the surrounding exposed area temporarily reveals the sticker’s shape.

The reveal is the central creative device: the sunscreen itself remains transparent, but the sticker makes the difference between protected and exposed areas easier to understand. The video clearly frames the result as a visual demonstration of sun exposure, not a measurement of exact UV damage. A concise closing message connects the demonstration to the audience’s real habit: “You can’t always see sunscreen working. Remember to apply, and reapply during your day.”

The product is present in every step: the real SunTrace SPF 50 sunscreen creates the protected area, and the included reusable sticker makes that protection visible after outdoor exposure. The format is designed for low- to medium-budget production, using real people, everyday university surroundings, and close-up phone footage rather than celebrities or expensive locations. The same repeatable video can be shared through university social media communities, encouraging students and young adults to recreate the sticker reveal with the product already in the package.

By giving sunscreen a visible moment of proof without overstating what the demonstration means, “See What Sunscreen Does” makes an otherwise invisible benefit memorable. The temporary sticker-shaped reveal becomes a practical reminder to use sunscreen before outdoor activities and to remember reapplication when the day continues.
