Creative brief: 

==================== USER INPUT 1/1 ====================
Brand:
SunTrace

Product:
A facial sunscreen that is transparent when applied normally. The package includes reusable UV-sensitive stickers. When a sticker is placed on the skin before sun exposure and removed later, the protected area remains unchanged while the surrounding exposed area temporarily reveals the sticker shape.

Target audience:
Young adults aged 18-30 who understand that sunscreen is important but frequently forget to reapply it during outdoor activities.

Objective:
Make the invisible protective effect of sunscreen visually understandable and motivate users to remember sunscreen application and reapplication.

Key product benefit:
The sunscreen provides SPF 50 protection, while the included UV-sensitive demonstration stickers make the effect of sun exposure easier to visualize.

Channels:
TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social media communities.

Budget:
Low to medium.

Constraints:
Use only the real sunscreen and included demonstration stickers. Do not claim that the temporary visual demonstration measures exact UV damage. Do not use celebrities or expensive locations. The idea must be understandable within a short video.
================== END USER INPUT 1/1 ==================

==================== AGENT OUTPUT ====================

=== FINAL CREATIVE CONCEPT ===
**SunTrace’s “Next Stop”** is a short-form video series built around a familiar young-adult habit: going out for one quick plan and ending up spending the whole day outside.

Each video opens with a person applying SunTrace SPF 50 before leaving for a simple first stop, such as walking across campus. One included UV-sensitive sticker is placed on the application area, clearly establishing it as a demonstration tool. The day then expands unexpectedly: the walk becomes lunch, lunch becomes a study session outdoors, or a quick errand becomes time with friends. Each change of plan appears as a fast “next stop” transition.

Before continuing to the next stop, the person pauses to reapply SunTrace. The video then shows the sticker after sun exposure: its shape has appeared on the surrounding exposed skin, while the area covered by sunscreen remains visually unchanged. The reveal becomes a recognizable “SunTrace” left by the day outdoors, making the invisible contrast easy to understand without presenting it as an exact measurement of UV damage. A small caption reads: “Demonstrates sun exposure, not exact UV damage.”

The recurring line is: **“Plans changed. Your sunscreen habit should too.”** Each video ends with **“Reapply before your next stop.”** The product is central to both parts of the idea: the real sunscreen creates the protected area in the demonstration, and the included sticker makes the otherwise invisible result visible. The unexpected extra stop gives the audience a specific, memorable reapplication cue tied to how they actually spend time outdoors.

The format is designed for TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social media communities. It can be filmed with real SunTrace, one reusable sticker, a phone, and ordinary campus or social locations, with different everyday “next stops” creating a repeatable series without changing the core idea.
