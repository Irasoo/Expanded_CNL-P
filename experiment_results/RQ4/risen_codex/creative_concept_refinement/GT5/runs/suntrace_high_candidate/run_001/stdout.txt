Creative brief: 

==================== USER INPUT 1/1 ====================
Brand:
SunTrace

Product:
A facial sunscreen that is transparent when applied normally. The package includes reusable UV-sensitive stickers. When a sticker is placed on the skin before sun exposure and removed later, the protected area remains unchanged while the surrounding exposed area temporarily reveals the sticker shape.

Target audience:
Young adults aged 18-30 who understand that sunscreen is important but frequently forget to reapply it during outdoor activities.

Objective:
Make the invisible protective effect of sunscreen visually understandable and motivate users to remember sunscreen application and reapplication.

Key product benefit:
The sunscreen provides SPF 50 protection, while the included UV-sensitive demonstration stickers make the effect of sun exposure easier to visualize.

Channels:
TikTok, Instagram Reels, and university social media communities.

Budget:
Low to medium.

Constraints:
Use only the real sunscreen and included demonstration stickers. Do not claim that the temporary visual demonstration measures exact UV damage. Do not use celebrities or expensive locations. The idea must be understandable within a short video.
================== END USER INPUT 1/1 ==================

==================== AGENT OUTPUT ====================

=== FINAL CREATIVE CONCEPT ===
**SunTrace: Make the Invisible Visible**

SunTrace turns a normally invisible sunscreen habit into a short, repeatable visual check-in. In each TikTok, Instagram Reel, or university social post, a young adult begins with a close-up of their face before heading outdoors. They apply SunTrace SPF 50 normally, showing that the facial sunscreen becomes transparent, then place one of the included reusable UV-sensitive stickers on the sunscreened skin before leaving for an ordinary campus activity, walk, picnic, or outdoor break.

The video returns to the same close-up later. The creator removes the sticker, and the temporary contrast appears: the protected area remains unchanged while the surrounding exposed area reveals the sticker’s shape. A simple caption reads, “You couldn’t see the protection. Now you can see the difference.” The video then cuts to the creator applying SunTrace again before continuing their day, with the closing message: “Apply before you go out. Reapply when your outdoor day continues.”

The visual is presented as a demonstration of SunTrace’s protective effect, not as an exact measurement of UV exposure or skin damage. A small on-screen note makes that clear: “Demonstration only—not a measurement of UV damage.” Because the stickers are reusable, the same format can be recreated across different days and shared by students and young adults through campus social communities without celebrities, special locations, or expensive production. Each post uses the same simple structure—transparent application, normal outdoor activity, sticker removal, visible temporary result, and a reapplication prompt—so the audience quickly understands both the product benefit and the habit SunTrace is encouraging.
